ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · LONDON

ODEAL

The London alté crooner who turned a bedroom recording into a global R&B redefinition — LVRN signed, MOBO blessed, and still making it up in the studio.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · NOVEMBER 19, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

Every few years London throws up a voice that refuses the R&B box it was filed in. Hillary Dennis Udanoh — Odeal — is the current case. Born in Germany, raised across Nigeria, Spain and the UK, he makes alté-inflected R&B that drinks in Yoruba choruses, grime cockiness and late-night confessionals, and he built it the hard way: bedroom beats, self-released EPs, and a community called OVMBR he assembled around him instead of waiting for a label.

Then came “Soh-Soh” (2023). The track off Sundays At Zuri's caught fire through a viral dance trend, racked up 20 million-plus Spotify streams, and pulled co-signs from SZA and Victoria Monét — which is how Atlanta indie powerhouse LVRN (the Summer Walker and 6LACK machine) came calling. He kept his sound anyway. “I've been screaming out for the longest that I'm not straight R&B,” he told NME. The 2024 EP Lustropolis, his first under LVRN, proved the point: brooding, syrupy, unmistakably his.

WHY LONDON CLAIMS HIM

OVMBR — his creative hub — throws its parties in East London warehouses, and his London shows have escalated from HERE at Outernet to Brixton with a sold-out crowd singing every word back at him. He raps like London talks and sings like Lagos dreams, and the scene treats him as its own rising headliner rather than an industry plant.

THE KEY TRACK

“Soh-Soh” (2023). A Yoruba-sweetened slow burner that turned a dance trend into a career. It hit number three on the UK Afrobeats chart, number twelve on Billboard's US Afrobeats chart, and made Odeal the UK's most exportable alté voice overnight.

THE VOICE

Odeal's voice is the instrument the whole scene is studying right now — honeyed but never soft, full of little melodic left-turns that make the simplest hook feel like something you haven't heard before. He moves between pidgin, English and Yoruba mid-sentence the way Londoners move between dialects mid-conversation. Nobody's doing the alté-to-R&B pipeline like this.

START HERE

“Soh-Soh,” then the Lustropolis EP for the mood, then Thoughts I Never Said for the bedroom origin story. Then catch an OVMBR night before the O2 Arena bills him as the headliner — it's already on the schedule.

Odeal isn't the future of UK R&B. He's the present — the scene just hasn't caught up yet.
