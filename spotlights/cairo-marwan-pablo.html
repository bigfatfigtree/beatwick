ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · CAIRO

MARWAN PABLO

The godfather of Egyptian trap — retired at the peak, returned like he'd never left, and changed Cairo's sound forever.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 1, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

The name is a statement: Pablo, after Picasso, because Marwan Matawa considers himself a new generation of rap. The Alexandria-born MC started as Dama, rebranded in 2017, and by 2019 — the golden run with producer Molotof, “El Gemeza,” “Sindbad,” “Free” — he had invented Egyptian trap as the country knows it.

Then, in February 2020, he did the unthinkable: he quit. Deleted the videos, went quiet, cited personal and religious reasons. Egypt's rap scene held its breath for a year — and on February 25, 2021, he exhaled. “Ghaba” (Jungle) pulled more than four million YouTube views in its first 24 hours. The CTRL EP followed. That October, his New Cairo concert sold out to over 26,000 fans — and the Musicians' Syndicate banned him from performing, which in Cairo is just another kind of coronation.

WHY CAIRO CLAIMS HIM

Alexandria made him, but Cairo made him immortal — the city where the trap scene he pioneered became a movement, where the New Cairo show that broke attendance records happened, and where every young Egyptian rapper now works in the shadow of his comeback. Cairo doesn't just listen to Marwan Pablo. It argues about him, which is better.

THE KEY TRACK

“Ghaba” (2021). The comeback single that broke the Egyptian internet — trap with an Arabic soul, and the sound of an artist returning on his own terms.

THE AFTERMATH

The comeback didn't fade. The Last Piece of Art (2023) proved the pen was still sharp, a Diesel ambassadorship made him a fashion fixture, and a sold-out London show in October 2024 confirmed the diaspora was all in. The ban that was supposed to end him became a footnote; the music became the headline.

THE BLUEPRINT

Every Egyptian trap artist working today is working in Pablo's wake — the Arabic-flow cadences, the Molotof-style production, the idea that Egyptian rap can be art and not just entertainment. He built the lane, left it, came back, and owned it again. That's not a career. That's a mythology.

START HERE

“Ghaba,” then the CTRL EP, then “El Gemeza” for the pre-retirement peak.

Most artists fear the hiatus. Marwan Pablo turned his into a legend.
