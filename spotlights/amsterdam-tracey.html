ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · AMSTERDAM

TRACEY

Tom Ruijg makes house and electro like a love letter to Detroit — the Amsterdam producer whose Skyfall rewired the Voyage Direct faithful.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MAY 20, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen →

Start with “Skyfall” — the Voyage Direct premiere.

Tom Ruijg had been releasing music under his own name for the better part of a decade — on Bangbang!, Karat, Hot Waves, Soweso — when he invented Tracey, and the alias unlocked everything. Tracey's palette, as RA put it, runs from the far-sighted futurism of Detroit to the deep-space pulse of electro, left-of-centre electronic experimentalism and British IDM. In practice: warm, dreamy, melodic electronic music with serious dancefloor grunt, made with studio partner Darling's collection of vintage drum machines.

The calling cards stack up fast. Skyfall — his first 12" for Tom Trago's Voyage Direct and the first Tracey release — balanced cascading, new-age-influenced synths against crunchy, distorted beats, and RA heard “a pan-generational collaboration between Tracey, early Detroit futurists, and early '90s ambient techno.” The follow-up Testarossa EP kept the lineage. Then Metamorphosis (2019) on Midland's Intergraded and Aus Music took the electro route: RA called the title track “one of the strongest tracks Ruijg has written,” imagining “Detroit In Effect with less sleaze and more poise.”

WHY AMSTERDAM CLAIMS HIM

Tracey is deep in the Amsterdam furniture: Voyage Direct, Shelter Amsterdam showcases, the Tom Trago orbit, the Rush Hour-adjacent world. His music is what Amsterdam's underground actually sounds like when the tourists go home — not the EDM the city's famous for, but the house-and-electro continuum the locals protect.

THE KEY TRACK

“Skyfall” (Voyage Direct). The Tracey origin moment: starry electronics, crystalline leads, a swing only vintage drum machines give. “Metamorphosis” (Aus) is the harder, sweatier counterpoint.

THE VOICE

An instrumental voice, but a distinctive one — colourful synth motifs and ear-catching melodies over sturdy dancefloor grooves, emotive and life-affirming but never less than energy-packed. He sometimes sounds like Larry Heard, other times like Drexciya or E.R.P. The through-line is warm reverie, even when the beats have some bite.

START HERE

Skyfall for the mission statement, Metamorphosis for the electro chapter, and his RA mix biog for the full map of influences. Then play it loud enough to rattle the windows.

Tracey writes fond tributes to Detroit from a studio in Amsterdam. The formula keeps working because the love is real.
