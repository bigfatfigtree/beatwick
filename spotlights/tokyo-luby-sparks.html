ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · TOKYO

LUBY SPARKS

Five Tokyo music nerds who sing in English, worship at the altar of Lush, and turned a university club breakup into the city's best shoegaze band.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · DECEMBER 16, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Somewhere” — the official music video.

Luby Sparks started because Natsuki Kato was the only guy in his university music club who cared about indie rock. Everyone else wanted to play soul and R&B; he was the kid who spent his CD-shop money traveling solo to Tokyo just to see My Bloody Valentine. So he left, wrote his own songs, and recruited four fellow weirdos into what he told Metropolis Japan is a band of "music nerds" — a shoegaze revival band built to be a gateway drug to the great old music for young people.

The gang — Kato on bass and vocals, Erika Murphy on vocals, Sunao Hiwatari and Tamio Sakuma on guitars, Shin Hasegawa on drums — formed in 2016 and immediately aimed outward. They played Indietracks Festival in Derbyshire in 2017, the only Japanese band on the bill. Their self-titled debut arrived in January 2018, recorded entirely in London and produced by Max Bloom of Yuck. Later records Songs for the Daydreamers and Search + Destroy kept the fuzz coming.

They sing in English, which in Tokyo's indie scene is either brave or perverse, and for Luby Sparks it's both. It lets the songs sit next to their idols: the Cocteau Twins' Robin Guthrie remixed their 2019 single "Somewhere," and nobody raised an eyebrow. "Tangerine" is named for Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind; Murphy changes her hair color like the film's Clementine. They love the source material so much they rebuilt it from scratch.

WHY TOKYO CLAIMS THEM

Because Tokyo's shoegaze underground is one of the densest in the world, and Luby Sparks are its most internationally wired export — the band that bridges the Koenji livehouse circuit to London studios and British festival fields without losing the bedroom-nerd core. The name came from the film Ruby Sparks, respelled to "Luby" so Google wouldn't bury them. That is music-nerd diligence you can hear in every guitar line.

THE KEY TRACK

"Somewhere" (2019). A dream-pop single so on-the-nose that Robin Guthrie of the Cocteau Twins remixed it — proof Luby Sparks aren't cosplaying the 90s; they're in conversation with it.

THE SOUND

Two guitars at the core, dual boy-girl vocals, walls of shimmer that resolve into actual hooks. Shoegaze and dream pop with an indie-pop spine — "Somebody Else," "A Thousand Miles," the No Joy remix of "Depression" — lush, melancholic, and obsessively arranged by people who own too many 90s compilations and know exactly how to use them.

START HERE

"Somewhere" (then the Guthrie remix, obviously), then the debut album, then Search + Destroy for the harder end of the band. Finish with "Tangerine" and go rewatch the film.

Luby Sparks loved minor music so much they made some of the least minor music in Tokyo.
