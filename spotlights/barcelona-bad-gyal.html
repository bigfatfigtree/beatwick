ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BARCELONA

Bad Gyal

The Vilassar de Mar singer who took dancehall to Catalonia — and made Barcelona the capital of Spanish perreo.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MAY 14, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Alba Farelo was born in 1997 in Vilassar de Mar, a coastal town in Barcelona province, and started the way every modern star starts: uploading to YouTube. In 2016 she posted a Catalan-language version of Rihanna's "Work" — "Pai" — and the internet did the rest. What followed wasn't a viral accident. It was a takeover: dancehall, reggaeton, and dembow, sung in Spanish, Catalan, and English, from a corner of Europe nobody expected it from.

The early mixtapes — Slow Wine (2016), Worldwide Angel (2018) — were the blueprint: airy dancehall riddims, deadpan delivery, and a persona that treated the whole thing like it was easy. "Fiebre" became the anthem; "Zorra" the statement of intent. She signed to Interscope, then went independent again — the major-label detour that taught her the Barcelona way was better: own the masters, own the vision, own the room.

Barcelona is the engine. The city's clubs — Razzmatazz, the whole Eixample late-night circuit — were her laboratory, and Primavera Sound's embrace made her a hometown headliner in waiting. She made it normal for Catalan artists to make urbano music without apology, and for Spanish pop to look to Barcelona instead of Madrid. La Joia (2024), her debut album, consolidated it all: the perreo, the pop instincts, the total self-possession.

What she represents is bigger than the hits: the proof that the Latin urban wave isn't a Latin American monopoly. A kid from the Barcelona suburbs took Kingston's rhythms, sang them in three languages, and made them the sound of Spanish youth culture. The perreo capital moved across the Atlantic, and it isn't moving back.

The key track

"Fiebre" — the breakout, the thesis, the song that still empties chairs. A dancehall lilt, a hook that won't leave, and the exact moment Barcelona's underground became Spain's mainstream.

Start here

Worldwide Angel (2018) — the mixtape that built the world, lean and undeniable. Then La Joia (2024) for the full album statement, and "Zorra" when you need the attitude dialed to maximum.

The take: Bad Gyal took the Caribbean's rhythms to Catalonia and made them native. Barcelona's clubs raised her; she returned the favor by putting the city on the global urbano map. The perreo speaks Catalan now.
