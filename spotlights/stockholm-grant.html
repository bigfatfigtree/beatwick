ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · STOCKHOLM

GRANT

The Swedish indie-pop dramatist from a farm outside Stockholm whose Waterline earned a Grammis nod and a P3 Gold Future Artist crown.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · SEPTEMBER 2, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen →

Start with “Waterline” — the 2018 breakthrough single — the official VEVO video.

Alma Caroline Cederlöf grew up on a farm outside Stockholm, singing among the horses and imagining different possibilities of life. Then she chose a name that felt monumental — GRANT, all caps — and started making pop music she wanted to be “raw and ugly,” unpolished on purpose. Her 2018 debut In Bloom arrived with blow and string instruments, a raspy voice, and melodies the Line of Best Fit called tiny revelations. The single “Waterline” did the rest: a show-stopping Grammis performance, a Newcomer of the Year nomination, and Swedish national radio P3 naming her Artist of the Future.

What sets her apart in Sweden's pop factory is the drama. Her music gets described as “James Bond-associative” — big, cinematic, wounded — with lyrics rooted in anxiety, relationships and the black hole of young-adult feeling. The Vertigo EP (2020) pushed it further into hazy art pop and UK-garage tributes. She toured Scandinavia with Tove Styrke, played the big summer festivals, and took the sound to London and Berlin. Sweden makes a lot of perfect pop; GRANT makes pop that refuses to be perfect, and that's the point.

WHY STOCKHOLM CLAIMS HER

She's the indie counterweight to the Max Martin machine — the artist Stockholm's alternative scene points to when someone says Swedish pop is all polish. P3 Gold's Future Artist prize is the industry's way of admitting the underground was right about her first.

THE KEY TRACK

“Waterline” (2018). The breakthrough: dramatic, windswept, enormous. It's the song that took her from the farm to Allsång på Skansen to the Grammis stage, and it still sounds like an arrival.

THE VOICE

Raspy, direct, and built for big rooms — a voice that carries hurt without softening it. Her songwriting has a poet's attention to detail, whether the mood is ecstatic or stormy, and the arrangements let the emotion do the heavy lifting. “I want to create music that is raw and ugly,” she said. The beauty is in how deliberate the ugliness is.

START HERE

In Bloom front to back, then the Vertigo EP for the weirder edges, then find the Grammis “Waterline” performance footage. The farm kid had range all along.

GRANT wanted a name she could make her own. She did — in all caps, on purpose.
