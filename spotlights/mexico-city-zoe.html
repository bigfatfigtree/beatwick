ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MEXICO CITY

Zoé

Mexico City's psychedelic romantics who proved the Unplugged format had a pulse.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MAY 6, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Zoé formed in Mexico City in 1994 — León Larregui on vocals, Sergio Acosta on guitar, Jesús Báez on keyboards, Ángel Mosqueda on bass, Alberto Cabrera on drums — and spent the better part of a decade as the capital's great almost-was, too psychedelic for the mainstream and too melodic for the underground. Then they outlasted everybody.

The breakthrough was Memo Rex Commander y el Corazón Atómico de la Vía Láctea (2006), and the coronation was MTV Unplugged: Música de Fondo (2011) — recorded at Mexico City's Churubusco Studios, debuted at number one in Mexico, and swept three Latin Grammys, including Best Alternative Music Album. "Labios Rotos" took Best Rock Song. An acoustic record as a career peak: only Zoé could make restraint feel like a flex.

They are a Mexico City band in the deepest sense — raised on the capital's clubs, forged in its indie scene, now headlining its biggest rooms. The 2018 Grammy for Aztlán (Best Latin Rock, Urban or Alternative Album) just made the international paperwork match what the city already knew.

The band's visual world — Larregui's androgynous spaceman persona, the cosmic artwork — made them icons in the streaming age, and records like Prográmaton (2013) kept the streak alive. Their influence runs through a generation of Mexican indie bands who learned from Zoé that weird and massive aren't opposites. Twenty-plus years in, they're the rare band that's both a legacy act and a current one.

Few bands survive the leap from cult act to institution without losing the plot. Zoé did it by never changing the plot — just the size of the room. The spaceman persona, the cosmic melancholy, the melodies: all intact, all bigger.

The key track

"Labios Rotos," debuted on the Unplugged. Larregui's wounded croon, Denise Gutiérrez of Hello Seahorse! on harmonies, and a melody that lodges in the brain permanently — the definitive Mexican rock ballad of the 2010s, and the song that turned a career band into an institution.

Start here

The Unplugged version of "Labios Rotos" first — accept no substitutes. Then "Soñé" and "Vía Láctea" for the cosmic side, and Memo Rex Commander in full when you're ready for the deep end.

Most bands peak and fade. Zoé peaked, unplugged, and became permanent.
