ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MUMBAI

Shreya Ghoshal

She won a TV contest at 16, sang for Devdas at 18, and never gave the microphone back.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · AUGUST 26, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Shreya Ghoshal was born in 1984 and won the television singing competition Sa Re Ga Ma at sixteen. That alone would be a career. But Sanjay Leela Bhansali heard her, cast her as the voice of Paro in Devdas (2002), and "Bairi Piya" — recorded when she was eighteen — won her the National Film Award and a Filmfare. She has been Bollywood's definitive female voice ever since.

Two decades later, the numbers are absurd: thousands of songs across Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, and more. In an industry that discards voices yearly, Ghoshal's stayed at the top through sheer, unreasonable technical perfection — and through Mumbai's fickle tastes cycling through three generations of heroines who all needed her voice.

She records in what colleagues describe as single takes, and her live shows — precise to the decimal — are the gold standard Mumbai's playback singers are measured against. The machine runs on her voice; she runs on discipline.

Mumbai roots

Ghoshal is a Mumbai story because Bollywood is a Mumbai story. The playback system — where actors lip-sync to singers' voices — means the city's film studios are where voices become immortal, and Ghoshal conquered them all: Yash Raj, Dharma, Bhansali's operatic sets. Her voice has played the heroine in hundreds of films shot within a few square miles of Andheri and Goregaon.

The lore is in the sessions: music directors who write songs specifically for her range, knowing she'll nail the impossible alaap in one take. Mumbai's studios run on her reliability the way the local trains run on schedule — mostly, and miraculously.

One key track

"Bairi Piya" (2002). The Devdas debut — a classical-tinged lament sung by an eighteen-year-old with the control of a veteran. It announced not just a singer but a new standard.

Bollywood changes heroines every five years. It hasn't changed its voice in twenty.

Start here

The Devdas soundtrack (2002) — five songs, all hers, all flawless. Then pick any year since; she's been on the year's biggest soundtrack every single time.
