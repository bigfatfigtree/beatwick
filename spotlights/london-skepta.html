ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · LONDON

Skepta

The Tottenham MC who carried grime on his back for a decade — then made it the world's problem.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JANUARY 13, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Joseph Adenuga started as a DJ on Tottenham's pirate stations — Heat FM, then his own sets — before picking up the mic himself. By 2005 he'd co-founded Boy Better Know with his brother JME: a crew, a label, and eventually a uniform. BBK became grime's most important institution, and Skepta became its chief executive, head of A&R, and best MC all at once.

Tottenham is the other pole of grime's origin story — Wiley's Bow to the east, Skepta's Meridian Estate to the north. The early mixtapes (Greatest Hits, Microphone Champion) were pure road rap, but Skepta's real genius was patience. While the industry chased the next Dizzee, he built BBK into a self-sufficient economy: own label, own tours, own rules. When grime's second wave arrived in the mid-2010s, he was already standing on top of the building.

"Shutdown" (2015) was the detonation — a skeletal, menacing track that Drake co-signed by wearing a BBK shirt at Wireless, and suddenly North American hip-hop was paying attention to a Tottenham estate. "That's Not Me" (2014) had already drawn the line in the sand: a return to eski-beat basics, JME on the hook, the video shot on a housing estate for almost nothing. Then Konnichiwa (2016) won the Mercury Prize, and grime had its official coronation.

What separates Skepta from every other grime success story is that he never left the culture to join the industry — he dragged the industry to Tottenham. The fashion world came calling (he's walked runways and fronted campaigns), the Americans came calling (features with Drake, A$AP Rocky, Wizkid), and through it all the message stayed the same: this is Boy Better Know, this is Tottenham, take it or leave it.

The key track

"Shutdown" (2015). Two minutes and twelve seconds of pure menace — a beat like a slammed door, a hook that's just a location pin dropped on the map. It's the sound of a scene realizing it didn't need permission anymore.

Start here

Konnichiwa (2016) — the Mercury winner, the victory lap, the definitive document. Then "That's Not Me" for the raw blueprint, and the early Microphone Champion for the hungry years.

The take: Skepta is grime's proof that independence isn't a consolation prize — it's the whole game. He built the label, the crew, and the audience, then let the world come to Tottenham. It did.
