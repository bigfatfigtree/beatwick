ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · GLASGOW

HUMOUR

Glasgow's feistiest post-punk quintet — NME-approved chaos, surreal lyrics, and a debut album Learning Greek that made the weirdness official.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 14, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “pure misery” — the official music video.

Humour formed across the 2021 lockdowns, live together in Glasgow, writing and recording at home with the music as a backdrop for frontman Andreas Christodoulidis's lyrics — which are sometimes about letting people down, sometimes about pets dying, sometimes about trying to say something when you don't have anything worth saying. It's off-kilter post-punk, erratic indie-pop and discordant post-hardcore, disorientating and immediate in equal measure. NME called their debut EP “a riot of invention.” They were barely a band yet.

The receipts came fast: Pure Misery (2022) and A Small Crowd Gathered To Watch Me (2023), both on So Young Records, both praised by Radio 1, 6 Music, DIY, Dork, CLASH and Brooklyn Vegan. Early shows supporting Do Nothing and Folly Group marked them out as a must-see live band; PRS Foundation funded them to showcase at SXSW in 2024. Then the debut album Learning Greek (August 2025), recorded at Post Electric in Edinburgh with Idlewild's Rod Jones — eleven songs into Andreas's private worries, tongue-in-cheek and brazenly biting, with the jagged hardcore edges of the EPs blended into more melodic guitar lines. The weirdness survived the album process intact.

WHY GLASGOW CLAIMS THEM

They're the So Young Records Glasgow stable at its most unhinged — labelmates with VLURE, formed in lockdown bedrooms, sharpened in King Tut's and Sneaky Pete's. Andreas draws all the band's artwork too, making each song “look the way that it sounds.” In a city with a post-punk lineage going back decades, Humour are the current proof it's still mutating.

THE KEY TRACK

“Pure Misery” (2022). The debut EP's title track — a choppy, unhinged paean to the uncertainty of fronting a band, written about being a singer expected to have something important to say and realizing you don't. For the album era, “Neighbours.”

THE VOICE

Andreas's delivery is the instrument: surreal, dynamic, veering between spoken-word poetics and jarring screams, with lyrics that read like poetic fairytales — a polar explorer lost on Wrangel Island, jazz as the soundtrack to a breakdown. The band treats lyricism as the forefront and the music as the frame, which is the opposite of how most post-punk works, and it's why they stand out.

START HERE

Learning Greek for the full statement, Pure Misery for the raw arrival, then a live set — the unhinged lyrical energy of the frontman is the whole point.

Humour make music about having nothing to say, and say it better than anyone.
