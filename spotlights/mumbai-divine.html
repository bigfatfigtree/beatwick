ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MUMBAI

Divine

From the chawls of Mumbai to Bollywood's biggest stage — Divine made the city's streets rhyme.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · APRIL 22, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Vivian Fernandes was born in Mumbai in 1990 and grew up in the chawls of Andheri, where the city's hip-hop scene was being invented in stairwells and on cracked phone screens. As Divine, he started rapping in English, switched to Hindi and Mumbai street slang, and accidentally created gully rap — the sound of the city's underbelly talking back.

"Yeh Mera Bombay" (2013) was the mission statement. Then came 2019: Zoya Akhtar's Gully Boy, a Bollywood blockbuster loosely based on his and Naezy's lives, with "Apna Time Aayega" as its anthem. Overnight, the underground was the mainstream — and Divine was its reluctant king.

He still reps the gully in every interview, and the gully reps him back — his face is on walls across Andheri that no billboard company paid for. Fame in Mumbai is rented; respect is owned.

Mumbai roots

Divine's Mumbai is the one the postcards skip. His verses map the city's real geography: the local trains, the police checkpoints, the housing colonies where three generations share two rooms. Gully rap was born because Mumbai's street kids had stories and cheap smartphones, and the combination turned out to be a genre.

His Gully Gang label and festival stages are now the pipeline: kids from Dharavi to Kurla battle-rap their way from cyphers to contracts. Bollywood still calls — the film industry can't resist a hit — but Divine's center of gravity stays in the gullies. The city's establishment learned to come to him.

One key track

"Apna Time Aayega" (2019). "My time will come" — four words that turned a film anthem into a generational motto. Ranveer Singh performed it on screen; half of Mumbai has it tattooed on their outlook.

Gully Gang, his label and crew, has become the pipeline the scene always needed — cyphers in Dharavi feeding directly into festival lineups, with Divine as the proof that the route exists.

Bollywood spent decades singing about the streets. Divine is the first one who actually lived on them.

Start here

Kohinoor (2019) — his debut album, named for the diamond the British took. The metaphor is not subtle, and neither is he.
