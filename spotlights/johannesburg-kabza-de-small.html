ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · JOHANNESBURG

KABZA DE SMALL

The King of Amapiano turned a township bedroom setup into the most imitated sound on the planet.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · FEBRUARY 19, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Kabelo Motha didn't wait for permission. From Pretoria, armed with a laptop and an obsession with the log drum — amapiano's unmistakable rolling bassline — Kabza De Small produced his way to the top of the most exciting dance movement on earth. The title stuck because nobody could argue with it: the King of Amapiano.

The coronation was thorough. I Am the King of Amapiano: Sweet and Dust (2020) codified the sound. The Scorpion Kings partnership with DJ Maphorisa (2019 onward) turned it into an industry. “Nana Thula” (2022) with Nkosazana Daughter and Sino Msolo became one of the defining records of the genre's global era — and he just kept producing, because the crown is defended in the studio, not on the timeline.

WHY JOBURG CLAIMS HIM

Pretoria-born, but the Gauteng house corridor is his laboratory — the Joburg studios, clubs, and taxi-rank sound systems where the log drum was perfected and battle-tested. Amapiano was born in the townships and raised in this city's rooms. Kabza just became its loudest evangelist.

THE KEY TRACK

“Nana Thula” (2022) with Nkosazana Daughter and Sino Msolo. The log drum at its most hypnotic, the vocals at their most yearning — the track that made the rest of the world stop calling amapiano a “new” sound and start admitting it was already better than theirs.

THE WORK ETHIC

Nobody out-produces him. Multiple albums and EPs a year, a release schedule that treats the studio like a shift job — KOA II Part 1 (2022), the endless Scorpion Kings drops, the features. In an era of two-year album cycles, Kabza's output is a flex in itself: the crown is defended at the desk, not on the timeline.

THE INFLUENCE

Count the log drums in any amapiano record made after 2019 and you're counting Kabza's children. His sound design — the specific bounce, the swing, the space — became the genre's default setting, which means half the world's dancefloors are now moving to rhythms he programmed in a Pretoria bedroom.

START HERE

“Nana Thula,” then “Imithandazo” with Mthunzi, then the KOA II Part 1 album. Bring comfortable shoes.

Every genre gets one true king. Amapiano got Kabza — and he's still in the studio.
