ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · CAIRO

3PHAZ

An anonymous Cairo producer who rebuilt mahraganat for the international club — Pitchfork-listed, Boiler Room-approved, and still nobody knows his name.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 12, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

3Phaz stays anonymous on purpose. Music first, identity later — never, if he can help it. The Cairo-based producer took Egypt's mahraganat and shaabi street sound and fed it through experimental club machinery: powerful kicks, sharp hand drums, fast-paced rhythmic explorations, synth flute-like melodies that hit like street wedding music from the future. The debut LP Three Phase (2020), released on the Egyptian label 100Copies, was a statement of intent — mahraganat rebuilt as DJ tools for bold dancefloors.

The world came calling. Singles and remixes landed on Rinse, Boomkat, Polaar, Nashazphone and Irsh. His Nuits Sonores set was featured on Boiler Room in 2019. Then the sophomore LP Ends Meet (February 2023) arrived on Discrepant's Souk sub-label, followed by the DramTraTrax EP (May 2023) on Tra Tra Trax — both landing on end-of-year lists at Pitchfork, The Quietus and Bandcamp. Live sets across Europe followed. Through it all, the mask stayed on: an entity, not a personality, conjuring Cairo's past and future into one percussive dimension.

WHY CAIRO CLAIMS HIM

Because he's the bridge nobody else could build. Mahraganat is the sound of Cairo's working-class weddings and street celebrations; 3Phaz proved its raw materials — the mizmar-style synth lines, the relentless percussion — could hold their own on any experimental club lineup in Europe. The city exports a lot of music; it rarely exports it this fearlessly.

THE KEY TRACK

"Dawayer" (2020), from Three Phase. Everything the project promises in one track: the street-synth melodies, the drum programming that feels both ancient and alien, the sense that a Cairo wedding DJ and a Berlin club engineer are fighting for the same mixer — and both winning.

THE ANONYMITY

It isn't a gimmick. In a scene where personality is currency, 3Phaz treats the mask as an editorial decision: the music has to stand above anything else. No face, no brand, no distraction — just rhythm. It's working. The records keep getting bigger while the person behind them stays exactly as invisible as he wants to be.

START HERE

Three Phase first, then Ends Meet for the evolution, then the DramTraTrax EP for the club weapons.

3Phaz gave Cairo's street sound a passport. Nobody's seen his face, but everybody's heard the drums.
