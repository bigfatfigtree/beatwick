ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MELBOURNE

Amyl and the Sniffers

Melbourne pub rock with its shirt off — four friends, two chords, zero interest in your approval.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · SEPTEMBER 16, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Amyl and the Sniffers formed in Melbourne in 2016 — Amy Taylor on vocals, Dec Martens, Gus Romer, and Bryce Wilson behind her — and immediately started playing like the last forty years of rock history was a rumor. Pub rock at hardcore speed: two-minute songs, maximum volume, lyrics about landlords, cops, and having the best worst time of your life.

Comfort to Me (2021) and its single "Guided by Angels" took them from Collingwood band rooms to Glastonbury's biggest stages without sanding off a single edge. Cartoon Darkness (2024) proved the fury had range. Taylor is the most electrifying frontperson of her generation, and she knows it — which is exactly the point.

Taylor's stage presence is confrontational in the way only Melbourne finds charming — feral, funny, and completely in control. The chaos is choreographed; the sweat is real.

Melbourne roots

This is the most Melbourne band imaginable, and that's saying something in this city. They came up through the band-room circuit's loudest corners — the Tote's front bar, the Bendigo Hotel's chaos years — where the audience is half the show and nobody's filming vertically. Melbourne's pub-rock lineage runs from AC/DC's early days through Cosmic Psychos; Amyl grabbed the baton and sprinted with it.

The city's influence is in the attitude: aggressively local, allergic to pretension, funny as hell. Taylor's between-song banter is pure Melbourne — self-deprecating, profane, and smarter than the suits. They export the city's id to the world and bring back bigger stages to play on.

One key track

"Guided by Angels" (2021). The riff arrives like a bar fight and the chorus is pure release. Two minutes and change — because anything longer would be showing off.

Their live reputation did the heavy lifting early: festival bookers who caught twenty minutes of chaos put them on main stages, and the band has never played down to a room since. The bigger the stage gets, the more it looks like the Tote front bar.

Rock was supposed to be dead. Nobody told the four loudest people in Melbourne.

Start here

Comfort to Me (2021) — thirteen tracks, thirty-six minutes, no ballads. Play it loud enough to annoy the neighbors; that's the intended listening environment.
