ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · TOKYO

Kenshi Yonezu

The Vocaloid ghost from Tokushima who moved to Tokyo and became the voice of a generation's grief.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 11, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Before he was Kenshi Yonezu, he was Hachi — an anonymous producer uploading Vocaloid songs to Nico Nico Douga, part of the internet's strangest hit factory. He revealed his face, moved to Tokyo, signed to a major label, and then did something the Vocaloid scene had never produced: a genuine, stadium-filling, chart-destroying pop auteur.

"Lemon," released in 2018 as the theme for the drama Unnatural, became one of the best-selling digital singles in Japanese history — a funeral ballad that the entire country adopted as its own. Stray Sheep (2020) sold over a million copies in its first week. He draws his own artwork, directs his own videos, and writes songs that treat sadness as a craft.

He rarely gives interviews and never explains the songs, which in Tokyo's media-saturated pop world reads as its own kind of statement. The work does the talking; the talking would only get in the way.

Tokyo roots

Yonezu's Tokyo is the city's internet-native underground made flesh. He came up in the same digital petri dish as the Vocaloid producers playing Liquidroom-adjacent clubs and the anime-adjacent artists colonizing Shibuya billboards — a generation that learned music from screens before stages. Tokyo gave him the industry; the internet gave him the audience; he gave both something neither expected.

His videos and artwork carry the city's visual language — the neon loneliness, the crowded solitude — and his concerts at venues like Tokyo Dome turned the Vocaloid generation's bedroom culture into arena spectacle. He also wrote "Paprika," the song performed by the kids' unit Foorin as NHK's official support song for the Tokyo 2020 Olympics: the ghost became the establishment, on his own terms.

One key track

"Lemon" (2018). The grief anthem that outsold everything. A waltz-time ballad about loss that Japan played at weddings and funerals alike — because Yonezu understands that the two aren't that different.

He started as a voice without a face. Now he's the face the whole country makes when it needs to cry.

Start here

Stray Sheep (2020) — fifteen tracks, every one a single in spirit. It's the rare blockbuster album that sounds like it was made by one person in one room, because it was.
