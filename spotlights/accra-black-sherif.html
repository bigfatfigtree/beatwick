ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · ACCRA

BLACK SHERIF

From Konongo to the top of Nigeria's charts — the most unlikely global star Ghana has ever produced, and the most honest voice in African music.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 12, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Mohammed Ismail Sherif's rise broke every rule. No Lagos co-sign, no industry godfather, no pop polish — just a kid from Konongo in Ghana's Ashanti Region whose raw, confessional drill records started moving so fast that the industry had to invent new ways to measure them.

“Second Sermon” (2021) went viral, the Burna Boy remix detonated it, and then “Kwaku the Traveller” (2022) did the impossible: a Ghanaian song topping Nigeria's charts — debuting at number one on the TurnTable Top 50 — while landing at number five on Billboard's US Afrobeats chart. His debut album, The Villain I Never Was (2022), confirmed it wasn't luck. Nobody sounds like Blacko. Everybody wishes they did.

WHY ACCRA CLAIMS HIM

Konongo made him, but Accra made him a star — the city's studios, student crowds, and late-night rooms where the sermons became scripture. He sings in Twi, English, and pidgin like the city talks, and December in Accra has a new soundtrack because of him.

THE KEY TRACK

“Kwaku the Traveller” (2022). Produced by Joker Nharnah, it's a song about outgrowing your hometown that made every hometown on the continent feel seen. The hustler's anthem for a generation that refuses to sit still.

THE VOICE

The instrument is the voice itself — raspy, urgent, cracking in all the right places. Black Sherif sings like someone who has seen things, because he has, and the Twi-English-pidgin blend makes every song feel like a conversation overheard in Accra traffic. Nobody else on the continent sounds like this. Everybody else wishes they did.

THE HONESTY

His real innovation isn't sonic, it's emotional. Black Sherif made vulnerability commercial in a scene built on bravado — songs about grief, failure, and starting over that somehow fill stadiums. The Villain I Never Was is a villain origin story where the villain is just a kid trying to do right. Accra recognized itself immediately.

START HERE

“Kwaku the Traveller,” then “Second Sermon” for the raw origin, then “Sacrifice” for the growth. Then the whole debut album.

Black Sherif didn't chase the industry's approval. He outgrew the need for it.
