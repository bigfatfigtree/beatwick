ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · KINGSTON

Sean Paul

Kingston's dancehall ambassador who made the whole world say 'dutty yeah'.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JANUARY 7, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Ryan Francis Henriques was born in Kingston in 1973, and before he was a deejay he was an athlete — he played water polo for Jamaica's national team. Then Kingston's studios got hold of him, and the athlete became Sean Paul: the man who would carry dancehall into the global pop charts without sanding off a single edge of the patois.

The breakthrough was Dutty Rock (2002). "Get Busy" went to number one on the Billboard Hot 100, "Like Glue" and "I'm Still in Love with You" followed it up the charts, and the album won the Grammy for Best Reggae Album. Then The Trinity (2005) did it again — bigger. For a stretch in the mid-2000s, Sean Paul was simply one of the biggest pop stars on earth, and he did it in full Kingston flow.

That's the part that matters: he never translated himself. The rapid-fire delivery, the dancehall slang, the riddim-first construction — all of it went to number one intact. He proved that a Kingston street sound didn't need an interpreter, just a big enough stage. Two decades later he's still the reference point every dancehall crossover gets measured against.

The second act was just as savvy: a two-decade run of collaborations with pop's biggest names — Beyoncé on "Baby Boy" (a 2003 chart-topper), Sia, Dua Lipa on "No Lie" — kept him on the charts while dancehall kept evolving around him. He never chased the sound. He just kept being the sound.

The key track

"Temperature" (2005). The single from The Trinity that hit number one on the Hot 100 and never really left the culture — weddings, clubs, and radio still run it like it's new. The riddim bubbles, the hook lands in about four seconds, and the whole thing is engineered for maximum bodies-moving-per-minute.

Start here

"Temperature" first, then "Get Busy" to hear the breakthrough. Then Dutty Rock in full — it plays like a greatest-hits record because it basically is one. Finish with "No Lie" (2016, with Dua Lipa) to hear the second act.

Plenty of artists have had hits. Sean Paul had a whole language go platinum.
