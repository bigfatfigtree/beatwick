ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MUMBAI

KAAM BHAARI

From Kandivali's streets to Gully Boy to his own label Art Nagar — Mumbai's rawest rapper did it the hard way and never blinked.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 25, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Zeher” — the official IncInk video.

Kunal Pandagale started uploading rap videos to YouTube at fifteen, from a financially struggling household in Kandivali where his school education was sponsored by the Save the Children India Trust. Nobody handed him a studio. He took the long way around: winning the Jack & Jones "Don't Hold Back" contest, performing with Ranveer Singh, and ending up in Gully Boy (2019) — where he lent his voice to the film's "Kaam Bhaari" track and wrote the lyrics for "Kab Se Kab Tak." The gully found the mainstream. The gully didn't soften.

Later in 2019, Ranveer Singh and Navzar Eranee launched their independent label IncInk, and its very first release was Kaam Bhaari's "Zeher" — a fast, venomous rap number produced by RĀKHIS that opened with the artist venting his frustrations and ended with Ranveer Singh himself showing up in the video. It was the loudest possible launch: a star's label debuting with a kid from Kandivali, not the other way around. "Mohabbat" (2020) earned him an MTV Europe Music Award nomination for Best India Act, and suddenly the underground had a diplomat.

Then he did the most Kaam Bhaari thing imaginable: he walked away. In 2022 he left IncInk and went fully independent under his own imprint, Art Nagar — rapper, lyricist, producer, label boss. "Pataka" (2022), "Rubaab," "Badhte Aage" followed, all produced under his Bhaari Beatz handle. The industry gave him a platform; he used it as a diving board.

WHY MUMBAI CLAIMS HIM

Because Kaam Bhaari is Mumbai's gully rap in its purest commercial form — aggressive flow, raw Hindi lyrics, zero industry polish, and a life story that doubles as the scene's origin myth. He even started a homemade food delivery service in Kandivali East during the pandemic. Nobody else in Indian hip-hop has a résumé like this: street rapper, film lyricist, actor, producer, food entrepreneur, label owner.

THE KEY TRACK

"Zeher" (2019). The first song IncInk ever put out, and still the definitive Kaam Bhaari record — rapid-fire verses about fake people and small minds, delivered like a warning shot. The video, with Ranveer appearing halfway through, made him the face of a movement overnight.

THE SOUND

Hard-hitting boom-bap DNA with a desi street edge: direct verses, anthemic hooks, beats that hit like they were mixed in a chawl stairwell. "Mohabbat" showed the soulful side — a laid-back, introspective love letter — but even at his softest, Kaam Bhaari raps like he's got something to prove.

START HERE

"Zeher," then "Mohabbat" for the contrast, then "Pataka" for the independent-era energy. If you still need convincing, "Mehfil-E-HipHop" puts him shoulder to shoulder with Mumbai's finest.

Kaam Bhaari didn't get discovered. He got too loud to ignore — then built his own house around the noise.
