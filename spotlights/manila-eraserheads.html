ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MANILA

Eraserheads

The band that gave the Philippines its songbook — and proved you could sing in Tagalog and conquer a nation.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · NOVEMBER 26, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

In 1989, four students at the University of the Philippines in Diliman started a band because the campus needed one. Ely Buendia, Raimund Marasigan, Buddy Zabala, and Marcus Adoro called themselves Eraserheads, played the Quezon City club circuit, and within four years had released Ultraelectromagneticpop! — the album that turned Filipino-language rock into a national industry.

Before them, singing in Tagalog was a commercial risk. After them, it was the whole game. They were the Beatles-and-Pixies synthesis the Philippines didn't know it was waiting for: literate, funny, furious, and completely unbothered by the idea that rock had to come from somewhere else.

Their breakup remains the great unsolved case in Filipino pop — no scandal big enough to justify it, no reunion permanent enough to end it. Manila has learned to live with the ambiguity the way it lives with traffic: it's just part of the landscape now.

Manila roots

The Eraserheads are a Manila band down to the jeepney fumes. They cut their teeth at Club Dredd on Timog Avenue, the Quezon City room where the '90s alternative scene lived; their songs are full of UP campus slang, Manila traffic, and the particular heartbreak of Philippine middle-class life. "Ang Huling El Bimbo" is about a childhood friend — and about every kid who grew up on the same street and ended up somewhere else.

They broke up in 2002 and Manila never really got over it. Every reunion — 2008, 2009, and the massive 2022 "Huling El Bimbo" concert — has felt less like nostalgia than like the city checking its own pulse. The 70s Bistro in Anonas still hosts the bands they made possible.

One key track

"Ang Huling El Bimbo" (1995). Six minutes that start as a music-hall waltz and end as a national monument. It's the rare song that functions as a shared memory for an entire country — play the first three notes in any Manila bar and watch the room stand up.

Every Filipino band since is either covering the Eraserheads or running from them. Most are doing both.

Start here

Cutterpillow (1995) — the album with "El Bimbo," "Overdrive," and "Huwag Mo Nang Itanong." Front to back, it's the sound of a band that knew it was writing the canon in real time.
