ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · JAKARTA

Rich Brian

A homeschooled kid from West Jakarta learned English from YouTube and accidentally became Indonesia's first global rap star.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JUNE 3, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Brian Imanuel Soewarno was born in Jakarta in 1999, homeschooled, and raised helping out at his parents' café in a middle-class West Jakarta neighborhood. He learned English by watching YouTube — Rubik's Cube tutorials, comedy sketches, rap videos — and in 2016, at sixteen, he uploaded "Dat $tick": a teenager in a pink polo rapping over a trap beat with total conviction. It got hundreds of millions of views.

Signed to 88rising, he rebranded from Rich Chigga to Rich Brian, released Amen (2018) — which debuted in the top 20 of the Billboard 200 — and became the first Indonesian artist to hit 10 million monthly Spotify listeners. The kid who taught himself English from the internet ended up teaching the internet about Indonesia.

He's spoken about the imposter syndrome of it all — a kid from a Jakarta café suddenly explaining Indonesia to American talk shows. The accent is the point: he sounds like where he's from, and where he's from is the story.

Jakarta roots

Rich Brian's Jakarta is the city's digital-native generation made flesh. He never came up through the TV studios or the dangdut circuit; he came up through a bedroom, a laptop, and a Wi-Fi connection — the exact infrastructure millions of young Jakartans share. His breakout proved that Jakarta's kids didn't need the industry's permission to reach the world, just a upload button.

He's been open about the weirdness of the trajectory: a homeschooled kid suddenly doing press in Los Angeles, repping a country most interviewers couldn't find on a map. But the throughline never changed — his videos and visuals keep circling back to Jakarta street culture, and his success cracked open the door for the whole 88rising Indonesia wave behind him.

One key track

"Dat $tick" (2016). The one that started it all — deadpan, lo-fi, and funnier than it had any right to be. Ghostface Killah and 21 Savage ended up on the remix. A pink polo has never carried more weight.

He learned a language from the internet, then used it to introduce his country to the world. Fair trade.

Start here

Amen (2018) — the debut that proved the viral moment wasn't a fluke. Then The Sailor (2019), recorded between New York, LA, and Jakarta, for the homesick sequel.
