ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · JAKARTA

HINDIA

The .Feast frontman who built a second identity as Jakarta's most honest singer-songwriter — and turned two decades of feeling into a movement.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MAY 27, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Evaluasi” — the official music video.

Daniel Baskara Putra has two careers, and both of them are him. Born in Jakarta in 1994, he formed the five-piece rock band .Feast in 2012 and spent years as its frontman — a brash, politically charged act whose singles "Peradaban," "Berita Kehilangan," and "Kami Belum Tentu" were sung at political demonstrations in Jakarta and revered as anthems of the radicalized. Then in 2018 he built a second identity, Hindia, for everything the frontman couldn't say. As he told Bandwagon: he wanted to be recognized as a singer-songwriter, not just the frontman of some rock band. So he became both.

Hindia's debut solo album, Menari dengan Bayangan (2019), was the quiet opposite of .Feast's nationalist roar — introspective, mellow, built from the Bon Iver and Circa Survive records he'd loved since youth. Its launch concert at Studio Palem Kemang in South Jakarta that December was a four-act, fifteen-track affair with collaborators like Kunto Aji, Nadin Amizah, Sal Priadi, and Iga Massardi on hand — a family gathering for the new identity. The follow-up, the two-part Lagipula Hidup Akan Berakhir (2023), took him to sold-out stadiums, and the 2025 mixtape Doves, '25 on Blank Canvas kept the introspection coming.

He also runs Sun Eater, the label nurturing Indonesia's next wave — and moonlights in Lomba Sihir with his live-band members, plus a day job as a lecturer at the University of Indonesia. Jakarta's indie scene doesn't just orbit Hindia; parts of it were literally founded by him.

WHY JAKARTA CLAIMS HIM

Because no one embodies the city's split personality better: the protest-singer firebrand and the bedroom confessionalist, the stadium and the Kemang studio, the politics and the poetry. Hindia writes about mental health, societal pressure, and millennial dread the way Jakarta feels it — crowded, tender, and refusing to look away.

THE KEY TRACK

"Evaluasi" — the Hindia single that crystallized his alter ego's sound, mellow and searching, the sound of the frontman stepping out of the spotlight and into himself. For the political side, .Feast's "Peradaban" is the one they still sing at demonstrations.

THE SOUND

Warm, literate, gently experimental indie-pop with singer-songwriter bones — acoustic warmth layered with collaborators' textures, lyrics that read like journal entries from a generation. .Feast is the opposite pole: traditional-Indonesian-inflected rock built for a crowd.

START HERE

Menari dengan Bayangan front to back, then "Evaluasi," then .Feast's "Peradaban" to meet the other half of the man.

Hindia split himself in two and made Jakarta bigger for it. One city, two careers, zero wasted feeling.
