ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · AMSTERDAM

Afrojack

Spijkenisse's wall of sound — the producer who made Dutch house hit like a truck and never turned it down.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MAY 28, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Nick van de Wall was born in 1987 in Spijkenisse, a town in the Rotterdam sprawl, and learned production the Dutch way: early, obsessively, and at volume. By his early twenties he was Afrojack, and "Take Over Control" (2010) — with Eva Simons on vocals — announced the hardest-hitting producer of the Dutch house wave. The drop didn't build. It detonated.

The Afrojack sound is maximalism as a moral position: sirens, saws, and basslines with no interest in subtlety. It conquered America first — the 2010-2012 EDM explosion ran on Dutch fuel, and Afrojack was the refinery. His remix of Madonna's "Revolver" won the Grammy for Best Remixed Recording in 2011. "Give Me Everything," his production for Pitbull with Ne-Yo, was inescapable. The Las Vegas residencies followed, and the Wall Recordings label became a launchpad for the next generation of Dutch hitters.

Amsterdam's dance establishment is his natural habitat even if Spijkenisse is his hometown: the ADE week, the studio culture, the whole Dutch infrastructure that treats DJing as a national industry. Afrojack is the bridge between Tiësto's trance generation and Garrix's big-room era — the guy who proved the Dutch could do loud better than anyone on earth, and then did it for a decade straight.

Forget the World (2014) was the debut album, but the real catalogue is the singles run: "No Beef" with Steve Aoki, "The Spark," a hit rate that kept him in the DJ Mag top ten for years. Subtlety was never the point. Impact was.

The key track

"Take Over Control" (2010). Eva Simons commanding the verses, then a drop that sounds like a building being demolished in rhythm. It's the definitive Dutch house record — the moment the Netherlands' volume knob broke off.

Start here

Forget the World (2014) for the album statement, then the singles run: "No Beef," "The Spark," "Ten Feet Tall." Play them loud. That's not a suggestion — it's the genre.

The take: Afrojack is Dutch dance music's id — the loudest, proudest version of the Amsterdam machine. While others chased credibility, he chased impact, and the impact is still ringing.
