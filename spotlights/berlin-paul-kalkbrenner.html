ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BERLIN

Paul Kalkbrenner

Berlin's techno everyman — the DJ who carried the city's sound from the Bunker to the big screen.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · APRIL 30, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Paul Kalkbrenner was born in Leipzig in 1977 and grew up in Berlin, coming of age as the Wall fell and the city's empty buildings filled with sound systems. He started releasing on Ellen Allien's BPitch Control in the early 2000s — melodic, emotional techno with a pop instinct the harder Berlin school didn't have. Then in 2008 he did something no techno producer had done: he starred in a movie about himself, basically, and it worked.

Berlin Calling is the film — Kalkbrenner playing DJ Ickarus, a Berlin DJ spiraling through the club scene, with a soundtrack he wrote himself. "Sky and Sand," recorded with his brother Fritz Kalkbrenner on vocals, became one of the best-selling German singles of all time and the definitive Berlin techno anthem: euphoric, melancholic, and impossible to hear without picturing the city at dawn. The film turned the Berlin club myth into cinema, and Kalkbrenner into its face.

His lane has always been the emotional middle of Berlin techno — not the Berghain bunker, not the minimal laboratory, but the big melodic statements that work for 50,000 people. His live sets are full hardware performances, no laptop fakery, and they've headlined festivals across Europe for over a decade. Albums like 7 (2015) and Parts of Life (2018) kept the formula evolving without abandoning it.

What makes him a Berlin artist in the fullest sense is that he's the scene's translator: the guy who took the underground's sound and made it legible to the mainstream without watering it down. The purists grumble; the crowds don't care. Berlin techno has its cathedrals and its monks — Kalkbrenner is its town crier, and the town loves him for it.

The key track

"Sky and Sand" (2008). Fritz's weary vocal, Paul's sunrise synths, and the most-played Berlin track of the century. It's the sound of the afterhours becoming the main event — euphoria with the comedown already in it.

Start here

The Berlin Calling soundtrack (2008) — the whole story in one record. Then "Aaron" for the deeper cut, and 7 (2015) for the mature, widescreen version of the sound.

The take: Paul Kalkbrenner made Berlin techno human-sized — the melody inside the machine. The film made him famous; the music made him last. Every festival main stage playing emotional techno is his legacy, with or without the credit.
