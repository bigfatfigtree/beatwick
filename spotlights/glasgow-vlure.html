ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · GLASGOW

VLURE

Glasgow's trance-punk five-piece went from sticky indie venues to a Bobby Gillespie collaboration — Escalate is the sound of the city's subterranean dancefloors.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 1, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

No official “Cut It” video could be verified — start with the record.

VLURE have a sound like no other, because nobody else thought to do this. The Glasgow five-piece — Hamish Hutcheson (vocals), Alexandra Pearson (vocals/synths), Conor and Niall Goldie (programming, production, guitar, bass) and Carlo Kriekaard (drums) — twist EDM-tinged trance around broody analogue synths and spoken-word punk poetry, creating music with one intention: the raw bubbling over of intense emotion. It's the sound of coming of age in Glasgow, the press keeps saying, and the press keeps being right: sticky indie venues, after-hours house parties, the city's subterranean dancefloors.

The arc is a DIY fever dream. Debut EP Euphoria on So Young, self-recorded and self-produced singles like “Cut It” (2023), the Heaven Sent EP, the rave anthem “Better Days” (2024) — and then the debut album Escalate (September 2025) on Music For Nations, co-produced by Conor with Manni Dee, closing with “A Clear Tide” featuring none other than Primal Scream's Bobby Gillespie, from the same Glasgow streets. They supported Primal Scream at the Barrowland in 2026, got nominated for the Anchor Award for best live act at Reeperbahn, and played Pitchfork Paris and The Great Escape along the way.

WHY GLASGOW CLAIMS THEM

They're the city's guitar-meets-club answer to its own history — Screamadelica's grandchildren raised on trance and post-punk. Gillespie's guest spot isn't a gimmick, it's a passing of the torch: the old dance-rock band endorsing the new one, in the Barras, in front of the faithful.

THE KEY TRACK

“Cut It” (2023). Thrashing drums, soaring synths, Hamish's punk poetry and Alexandra's erratic whisperings of “can you feel it?” — a flurry of chaotic rave energy that remains their mission statement. For the album era, “Heartbeat.”

THE VOICE

Two voices, actually — Hamish's Iggy-adjacent punk-poetry bark and Alexandra's synth-wrapped counter-whispers — plus Conor's production, which blurs live electronics, jarring guitars and heavy club rhythms into one euphoric smear. One review heard peak-era Prodigy through a post-punk lens. The live show is where it detonates: bare-chested, in the crowd, total psychodrama.

START HERE

Escalate in full — it's the complete statement. Then “Cut It” and “Better Days” for the singles era, then find a live review and start planning a trip to Glasgow.

VLURE defy the rules, rip up trends and create new ones. Glasgow taught them how.
