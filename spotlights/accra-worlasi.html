ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · ACCRA

WORLASI

Accra's alté conscience — a rapper, singer, illustrator and festival founder who did it himself, then built a stage for everyone else.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · AUGUST 19, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

No official “Kilode” video could be verified — start with the Dios EP.

Worlasi is what happens when an artist refuses every shortcut. The Accra-based rapper, singer, songwriter and visual artist has spent a decade as one of the champions of Ghana's alternative scene — and done it without the industry's permission. His 2019 album WORLA ("The Man and the God") was a 10-track statement recorded live with his day-one band SENKU, Osibisa-inspired and obsessed with how power corrupts. Then came Dios (2020) — "did it ourselves" — the joint EP with producer Drvmroll that led with the lovelorn "Kilode" and played like a manifesto for Ghana's alté wave.

He didn't stop there. The.Rap.Y (2023) — therapy, say it out loud — was a seven-track tour through Afro-Cuban jazz, hip-hop and Afropop, lyrically sharp about corrupt elites, ambition, and love, and praised as one of the most daring Ghanaian projects in years. Offstage, he founded WORLAFEST, his annual arts and music festival at Alliance Française Accra, with an art exhibition and a bill of Ghana's independent finest. He raps and sings in English, Ewe, Fante, Ga and pidgin. Nothing about Worlasi is small except his willingness to compromise — which is nonexistent.

WHY ACCRA CLAIMS HIM

Because he built the infrastructure he couldn't find. WORLAFEST isn't just a concert — it's an annual proof that Accra's independent scene doesn't need the mainstream's blessing. His festival lineups read like a who's-who of the alté underground, and his live band shows with SENKU are the realest thing on a Ghanaian stage.

THE KEY TRACK

"Kilode" (2020), the lead single from Dios. Lovelorn, unhurried, beautifully produced — it distills everything Worlasi does well: vulnerability without weakness, craft without stiffness, and a hook that sits in your head for days.

THE CONSCIENCE

Worlasi's pen is the sharpest in Accra's alté scene. He writes about power — who has it, what it does to them, what it costs everyone else — with a specificity that makes the political feel personal. WORLA was about humans playing god on earth. Nothing since has made the subject feel less urgent.

START HERE

Dios for the sound, The.Rap.Y for the lyricism, WORLA for the full live-band experience. Then find the nearest WORLAFEST and thank us later.

Worlasi did it himself. Then he built a festival so nobody else has to do it alone.
