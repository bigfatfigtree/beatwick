ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MONTREAL

OURI

A classically trained cellist who ran straight into Montreal's rave scene — Ouri's music is orchestral composition and electronic grit, fused.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · APRIL 1, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Ossature” — the official video — strings, dread, and VFX in equal measure.

Ourielle Auvé's biography reads like a dare: born and raised in French Guiana, classically trained on harp, cello, and piano, moved to France, then landed in Montreal at sixteen to study composition — and instead of the concert hall, walked straight into the city's rave scene. As Ouri, she's spent a decade building something almost nobody else makes: electronic music with orchestral bones, trip-hop shadows, whispered French vocals, and industrial abrasions tearing through the beauty. Her debut album Frame of a Fauna (2021) made the Polaris Music Prize longlist. Her 2025 follow-up Daisy Cutter did the same. The underground knew. The lists are just catching up.

The path ran through EPs Superficial (2017) and We Share Our Blood (2019), a MISM x Boiler Room Montreal appearance in 2018, a signing to Ghostly International abroad and Make it Rain at home, and official remixes for the likes of Tokimonsta. Frame of a Fauna — fourteen tracks moving from strings to field recordings to future trip-hop, recorded between London, Berlin, and Brazil — is a record about birth, death, and constant rebirth that sounds exactly like that sentence. Along the way she formed Hildegard with Helena Deland, a side project that earned its own Polaris longlist, and became a MUTEK regular — the festival that basically certifies Montreal's electronic avant-garde.

WHY MONTREAL CLAIMS HER

Ouri is the Montreal electronic scene's thesis made flesh: conservatory training meets warehouse culture, classical heritage colliding with club experimentation. She found her voice in the city's raves as a producer, DJ, and composer while still a teenager, and everything since — the cello-led live shows, the industrial edge, the cinematic live A/V work — carries that collision. MUTEK keeps putting her on the biggest stages because she represents the city's entire proposition in one artist.

THE KEY TRACK

"Ossature" — the first single from Frame of a Fauna, and the whole Ouri method in miniature: dreamy, atmospheric strings punctured by industrial abrasion, rebellious choruses fighting submissive verses. It's a track that keeps trying to keep itself disciplined and soft, and keeps failing beautifully.

THE INSTRUMENTS

Harp, cello, piano — and then the machines. The orchestral training isn't decoration; it's the architecture. Ouri composes like someone who grew up inside classical music and then decided to see what it looks like on fire.

START HERE

Frame of a Fauna first, then Daisy Cutter, then the Hildegard album Jour 1596 for the collaborative dimension. Catch her live at MUTEK if the timing lines up — that's the native habitat.

Ouri turned conservatory training into rave music. Montreal's electronic underground has never sounded richer for it.
