ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · TORONTO

Drake: the 6 God built Toronto's music economy

Before Drake, Toronto was a feeder market. Then the kid from Degrassi turned the 6ix into a global sound.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · NOVEMBER 13, 2025 · 4 MIN READ

Every city gets one artist who changes its area code into a brand. For Toronto, it's Aubrey Graham — the former teen-TV actor from Forest Hill who became the most commercially dominant rapper alive and, in the process, made "the 6ix" the most valuable nickname in music.

The mechanism was OVO Sound: Drake's label and creative universe, run out of Toronto with producer Noah "40" Shebib, whose murky, late-night, R&B-drunk production became a sound the entire industry spent a decade imitating. The "Toronto sound" is really the 40 sound — spacious, nocturnal, emotionally oversharing — and it colonized pop radio from 2011 onward.

Why Toronto claims him

Drake repays the city like a municipal bond. He's the Raptors' global ambassador, he shut down the city with OVO Fest year after year, and his lyrics read like a TTC map: Weston Road, the CN Tower, views from the 6. When the Raptors won the 2019 championship, the parade route might as well have been his album rollout. Local lore says every Toronto rapper's career now runs through the OVO ecosystem — get the co-sign, get the city.

Critics can argue about the pen; nobody can argue about the infrastructure. Studios, engineers, videographers, and a generation of artists from the Weeknd to Daniel Caesar built careers in the lane Drake paved.

Toronto didn't have a music industry identity before Drake. Now the identity has a passport, a label, and a basketball team.

The empire kept expanding: OVO's clothing line, the annual OVO Fest that shut down the city's summer, and a run of number-one albums so consistent it started to feel like a municipal service. Toronto's skyline shows up in his videos; his face shows up on the city's murals. The 6ix isn't a nickname anymore — it's an economy, and he's the central bank.

Key track

"Started From the Bottom" — the mission statement, the meme, the hometown thesis. A kid from the north side of Toronto narrating his own origin myth until the myth became the economy.

Start here

Take Care (2011) for the full nocturnal-Toronto blueprint. Then Nothing Was the Same for the victory lap. Best experienced on a night drive down the Gardiner with the lake on your right.

Love him or debate him, Drake did what no Toronto artist had ever done: he made the world learn the city's slang.
