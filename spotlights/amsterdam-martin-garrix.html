ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · AMSTERDAM

Martin Garrix

The kid from Amstelveen who released "Animals" at 17 and never left the top of dance music again.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 28, 2025 · 2 MIN READ

Martijn Garritsen was born in 1996 in Amstelveen, a suburb stitched to Amsterdam's southern edge. At eight he saw Tiësto play the Athens Olympics opening ceremony on television and decided, with the terrifying clarity of a child, that this was the job. At 16 he was releasing tracks. At 17 he released "Animals" — and dance music's center of gravity shifted to the Netherlands for good.

"Animals" was a phenomenon: a minimal big-room grenade with a drop that sounded like a warning siren, number one in a dozen countries, and the youngest artist ever to top the Beatport chart. It made Garrix the face of the Dutch house wave — the third generation after Tiësto and Armin van Buuren — and he handled the pressure by simply out-working everyone. By 20 he was the youngest-ever number one in the DJ Mag Top 100, a title he'd take again and again.

Amsterdam is the machine behind the miracle. The city is dance music's capital the way Nashville is country's: the labels, the studios, the management, the clubs, and every October the Amsterdam Dance Event — the industry's annual pilgrimage, where Garrix's hometown shows are the week's hardest ticket. In 2016 he founded STMPD RCRDS, his own label, and started signing the next wave himself. The prodigy became the pipeline.

What's kept him at the top isn't just the drops — it's the range. The GRX alias for harder sounds, the pop crossovers ("In the Name of Love" with Bebe Rexha, "Scared to Be Lonely" with Dua Lipa), the long-awaited debut album Sentio (2022) that finally showed the club purist underneath the hitmaker. While his peers chased residencies into creative retirement, Garrix kept releasing like he was still trying to get noticed.

The key track

"Animals" (2013). The drop that launched a thousand festivals. Strip away the memes and it's still a ruthlessly efficient piece of sound design — proof that a 17-year-old from the Amsterdam suburbs understood the main stage better than veterans twice his age.

Start here

Sentio (2022) for the club side — eleven tracks of peak-time Garrix with no pop compromises. Then the BYLAW EP (2018) for the harder GRX-leaning cuts, and "High on Life" with Bonn if you want to hear what euphoria sounds like when it's engineered properly.

The take: Garrix is what happens when Amsterdam's dance-music assembly line produces a once-in-a-generation talent: the kid who saw Tiësto on TV became Tiësto's heir, then built his own factory. The suburbs of Amsterdam have exported bigger hits than most countries.
