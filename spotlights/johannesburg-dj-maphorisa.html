ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · JOHANNESBURG

DJ MAPHORISA

The most in-demand producer in African music — from Mafikizolo's biggest hit to amapiano's global throne.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JUNE 4, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Before he was the King of Features, Themba Sonnyboy Sekowe was a member of Uhuru, the trio behind Mafikizolo's “Y-tjukutja” (2014) — one of the defining South African songs of the decade. Then DJ Maphorisa went solo and did something harder: he became indispensable.

The solo run is staggering. “Dali Nguwe” (2019) with Sjava launched the era. The Scorpion Kings partnership with Kabza De Small (2019 onward) turned amapiano into an export industry. And co-producing “Mnike” (2023) with Tyler ICU and Tumelo.za gave the genre its biggest global record. If a song is moving in Southern Africa, there's a decent chance Phori's fingerprints are on the board.

WHY JOBURG CLAIMS HIM

Soshanguve-raised, but the Gauteng corridor is his studio — the Joburg sessions, clubs, and producer camps where the continent's biggest records get made. Maphorisa is the city's unofficial A&R: if he touches your track, the whole industry is listening by morning.

THE KEY TRACK

“Dali Nguwe” (2019) with Sjava. The moment Maphorisa proved he didn't need a group to make a classic — a slow-burning masterpiece that still soundtracks every Joburg golden hour.

THE HITMAKER ECONOMY

BlaqBoy Music, his label, is the other engine — a home for the producers and artists orbiting his sound. Maphorisa doesn't just make hits; he manufactures hitmakers, running Joburg's producer camps like a studio system. The credits list reads like a phone book of everyone who matters in amapiano.

THE RANGE

What's easy to forget is the range: from the soulful crawl of “Dali Nguwe” to the percussive assault of “Mnike,” from gqom-adjacent experiments to straight pop. Maphorisa is a chameleon with a signature — you can always tell it's him, even when he's doing something he's never done before.

THE LEGACY

From Uhuru’s kwaito-pop dominance to the amapiano throne, Maphorisa has now shaped two distinct eras of South African music — a feat almost nobody else can claim. The throughline is instinct: he hears what the country wants before the country knows it wants it.
