ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · LAGOS

AYO MAFF

From the Bariga streets of Lagos to a chart-topping link-up with Fireboy DML — the self-styled Prince of the Street carrying street-hop into a new generation.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · FEBRUARY 25, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Dealer” — with Fireboy DML — the official video.

Ayorinde Mafoluku Ayodele grew up in Somolu, Bariga — one of the Lagos neighborhoods where the street-hop canon was written — and he writes like he knows it. He started releasing at 17: the debut single "Jama Jama" (2023), then the two-track STREET ANTHEM / 7 DAYS (January 2024). Then came the earthquake. "Dealer," his collaboration with Fireboy DML, topped charts across the board in 2024 and turned a Bariga kid into a national conversation overnight.

The follow-up was swift and confident: Maffian, his debut EP, landed on 15 August 2024 — seven tracks including "Last Week" — and a studio album, Prince of the Street, followed in 2025. That's the arc in full: street kid, co-sign, project, album, title claimed. Ayo Maff moves at Lagos speed because Lagos speed is all he's ever known. The sound is gritty street rap melting into melodic Afrobeats, midtempo and unhurried, with a young voice that sells ambition better than any press release could.

WHY LAGOS CLAIMS HIM

Bariga isn't just where he's from — it's the thesis. Street-hop's lineage runs through these blocks, and Ayo Maff carries it openly: the hustle narratives, the midtempo bounce, the refusal to sand the edges off. When Lagos radio needed proof the street sound had a next generation, he showed up on cue.

THE KEY TRACK

"Dealer" (2024), with Fireboy DML. The video crossed 2.5 million YouTube views in months, the song topped Apple Music Nigeria, and the pairing — one of street-hop's new princes next to one of Afrobeats' established kings — felt like a formal handover. It's the song that made everything after it possible.

THE HUSTLE

What separates Ayo Maff from the other Bariga kids with a phone is discipline. The release cadence is relentless — singles, a two-tracker, an EP, an album in under two years — and the music keeps its midtempo poise while his peers chase trends. He raps and sings about the climb like someone still on the ladder, which, at his age, he is.

START HERE

"Dealer" first, then "Last Week" and the full Maffian EP. Finish with Prince of the Street to hear the title being earned in real time.

Ayo Maff didn't borrow the street's crown. He's building his own, one Bariga block at a time.
