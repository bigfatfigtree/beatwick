ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BUENOS AIRES

FIN DEL MUNDO

Buenos Aires' all-female shoegaze revelation — dreamy post-rock apocalypses, KEXP and Radio 3 spins, a first European tour, and an album called Todo Va Hacia el Mar.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JUNE 17, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “El incendio” — live on KEXP — the show-closer in full flight.

The end of the world never sounded this serene. Fin del Mundo — guitarist/vocalist Lucía Masnatta, guitarist Julieta Heredia, bassist Julieta Limia, and drummer Yanina Silva — is Buenos Aires' all-female post-rock/shoegaze four-piece, and their name is the whole thesis: dreamy apocalypses, melodies drifting through beautiful ruins.

The discography is two EPs compiled into one long-player: the self-titled debut (2020) and La Ciudad Que Dejamos (2022), gathered as Todo Va Hacia el Mar, released on Spinda Records in November 2023. From "La Noche" through "El Fin del Mundo," the tones are serene, the structures hold, and the last track reaches broader, heavier ground. Then came their first European tour — France, Switzerland, Italy, Gibraltar, Spain — because word travels fast when a band sounds like this.

WHY BUENOS AIRES CLAIMS THEM

Because a four-piece of women making dreamy post-rock in a city that exports trap and perreo is exactly the kind of stubborn, necessary counter-signal that keeps Buenos Aires interesting. Critics and fans alike have called them one of the great revelations of rock in Latin America — and revelations don't happen by accident.

THE KEY TRACK

"El incendio." The closer, where the Sonic Youth-style indie of their earlier material gets more complex melodicism around it and builds tension into a decisive resolution. It's the moment the dream catches fire — and the whole reason to listen.

THE REACH

Their music is being played by DJs at Radio 3 and KEXP and podcasts from all over the world; magazines like RockZone, Mondo Sonoro, Ruta 66, Rolling Stone, and The Obelisk keep writing about them. That's a remarkable footprint for an underground four-piece from Argentina — and it's still just the first record.

The growth between the two EPs is the quiet headline. The 2020 material drifts; the 2022 side pushes harder behind the rhythms, builds bigger washes, and closes with gang vocals and tension that resolves on purpose. Thirty-four minutes of two halves that clearly outgrew each other — which makes their next full-length the most anticipated record in Latin American guitar music's underground. A band that figured out its own format this fast doesn't stay underground long.

START HERE

"El incendio," then "La Noche," then the full Todo Va Hacia el Mar — 34 minutes of the end of the world, beautifully arranged.

Fin del Mundo named themselves after the apocalypse. Then they made it the prettiest sound in Buenos Aires.
