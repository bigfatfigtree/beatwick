ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MANILA

MUNIMUNI

Quezon City's indie-folk poets turned Tagalog introspection into 'makata pop' — and took a pandemic hiatus just to come back bigger.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JULY 8, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Sa Hindi Pag-alala” — the official lyric video from the Simula EP.

The name means "to ponder, to reflect, to daydream" — and Munimuni is the rare band that actually lives up to its own branding. Formed at the University of the Philippines Diliman in 2012, the five-piece (Adj Jiao, John Owen Castro, Jolo Ferrer, Josh Tumaliuan, Ben Ayes) built a genre they call makata pop: Filipino poetry fused with indie-folk and indie-pop melodies, sung in Tagalog, arranged with a flute where other bands would put a guitar solo. It's daydream music for a country that needed something to daydream about.

Their 2017 debut EP Simula introduced the formula — "Sa Hindi Pag-alala," "Sa'yo," "Marilag" — and the band became the kind of act that co-headlines with The Japanese House at Karpos Live, then plays a school-night show to a packed Manila venue anyway. Songs like "Minsan," "Solomon," and "Kalachuchi" are slow-burn anthems: deep, poetic, and quietly massive, the kind of music that turns a concert into a group therapy session with better harmonies.

In September 2020, at the height of the pandemic, Munimuni announced a hiatus — the industry was in freefall, and they stepped back with unusual honesty, telling fans the darkness needed its own time. They returned in 2021 and haven't stopped evolving since. The 2024 Alegorya concert was a homecoming to their UP Diliman alma mater and the longest set they'd ever played — nearly three hours, charting a journey from indie-folk to something looser, more exploratory, still unmistakably theirs.

WHY MANILA CLAIMS THEM

Because Munimuni is Manila's conscience with a melody. They formed on a university campus, they sing in Filipino about Filipino feelings, and their concerts — like the 2019 Kulayan Natin show and the 2024 homecoming — are generational gatherings. In a scene that can feel import-obsessed, Munimuni made looking inward the coolest move in the room.

THE KEY TRACK

"Sa Hindi Pag-alala" (2017). The song that started the makata-pop movement — a meditation on forgetting that every Filipino seems to know by heart. Start here and you'll understand the band's entire thesis: sometimes the most powerful thing a song can do is sit still with you.

THE SOUND

Indie-folk with a distinctly Filipino soul: fingerpicked guitars, Castro's flute drifting through arrangements like a memory, Jiao's gentle vocals, and lyrics that read like poetry because they are. "Bawat Piyesa," "Tahanan," "Bakunawa" — every song is a small, careful world.

START HERE

"Sa Hindi Pag-alala," then "Sa'yo" and "Minsan," then the Simula EP. If you're still standing, the Alegorya live recordings will finish the job.

Munimuni taught Manila that reflection is a genre — and that a band named after daydreaming can keep a whole city awake.
