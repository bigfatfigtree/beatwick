ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BARCELONA

Rosalía

A Catalan flamenco student rewired pop in three languages — and made Barcelona the center of the Latin avant-garde.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · DECEMBER 12, 2025 · 2 MIN READ

Rosalía Vila Tobella was born in 1992 in Sant Esteve Sesrovires, a town outside Barcelona, and did the most un-pop-star thing imaginable: she went to music school. At Barcelona's Catalonia College of Music she studied flamenco seriously — palmas, cante, the whole discipline — while quietly absorbing everything else. When she finally broke, she didn't just cross over. She redrew the map.

Los Ángeles (2017) was the thesis statement: stark, traditional, astonishing. El Mal Querer (2018) was the detonation — a flamenco concept album about a toxic relationship that won the Latin Grammy for Album of the Year and made a centuries-old form sound like the future. Then Motomami (2022) tore up the thesis entirely: reggaeton, bachata, jazz, hyperpop, dembow, sometimes in the same song. "Saoko" sounds like a motorcycle crash you want to be in.

Barcelona is the through-line. She learned the craft there, played her first big rooms there, and headlined Palau Sant Jordi as a hometown hero. The city's Primavera Sound crowd — the most taste-making audience in Europe — claimed her early and never let go. Rosalía made it possible to be a global pop star from Catalonia without sanding off a single edge: the Catalan, the Spanish, the flamenco handclaps, all of it intact.

Her influence is now structural. "Con Altura" with J Balvin proved the Latin mainstream would follow her anywhere; "Despechá" proved she could do pure perreo-pop at scale; "La Noche de Anoche" with Bad Bunny proved she could out-sing anyone in the room. A generation of Spanish and Latin artists now treats genre like a wardrobe, not a uniform — and they're all wearing her hand-me-downs.

The key track

"Malamente" (2018). The El Mal Querer opener: flamenco palmas, a synth bass that sounds like a warning, and a vocal that moves from whisper to wail in four bars. It's the song that made the world stop and ask what exactly this was — and then learn the answer was flamenco.

Start here

El Mal Querer (2018) — the concept album, the masterwork, the one that will still be taught in classes. Then Motomami (2022) for the chaos, and "Con Altura" when you need to remember she can also just make a hit.

The take: Rosalía treated flamenco like a living language and pop like raw material — and built the most interesting career in music out of the combination. Barcelona didn't just produce a star. It produced a new way of being one.
