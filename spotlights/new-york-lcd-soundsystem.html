ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · NEW YORK

LCD Soundsystem: Brooklyn's dance-punk professors

James Murphy made middle-aged anxiety danceable and turned a farewell show at MSG into a resurrection.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JANUARY 22, 2026 · 4 MIN READ

James Murphy was already in his thirties — a sound engineer and DFA Records co-founder — when he started LCD Soundsystem as what was supposed to be a one-off dance-punk project. Instead he became the patron saint of aging gracefully on the dancefloor, writing the definitive songs about being too old for the club and going anyway.

"Losing My Edge," the 2002 debut single, was a joke about record-collector anxiety that accidentally invented the band's whole thesis: self-awareness as a groove. Sound of Silver (2007) and This Is Happening (2010) turned that joke into canon. Then Murphy did the most New York thing possible: he broke up the band with a sold-out farewell at Madison Square Garden in 2011, filmed it for the documentary Shut Up and Play the Hits, and reunited five years later anyway.

Why New York claims them

LCD is a love letter to the city's nightlife infrastructure — the DFA parties, the Williamsburg warehouse era, the Bowery Ballroom and Terminal 5 residencies that feel like civic rituals now. "New York, I Love You but You're Bringing Me Down" is the city's official complicated-relationship anthem, sung back at the band by twenty thousand people who mean every word. Murphy's other gig — designing sound systems for venues like his own club — makes him as much an architect of NYC nightlife as a performer in it.

LCD Soundsystem is what happens when the guy running the soundboard decides he's got something to say — and it turns out he's the funniest, saddest voice in the room.

Don't forget the label. Murphy co-founded DFA Records in 2001, and it became the definitive document of the 2000s New York underground — the Rapture, !!!, Hot Chip's American home. LCD was the flagship, but DFA was the fleet. Between the band, the label, and the sound systems, Murphy has shaped how New York hears itself for a quarter century.

Key track

"All My Friends" — seven and a half minutes of piano repetition building into the great midlife-crisis anthem. "Where are your friends tonight?" has never hit harder than at 1 a.m. in Brooklyn.

Start here

Sound of Silver for the peak, then the American Dream (2017) comeback record. Best consumed live — the band's residencies sell out in minutes for a reason.

New York has a thousand bands. It has exactly one that made growing older sound like the best night out of your life.
