ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MELBOURNE

Courtney Barnett

Melbourne's deadpan poet laureate turned rambling indie rock into an international language.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JANUARY 7, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Courtney Barnett was born in Sydney in 1987, but she belongs to Melbourne the way the trams do. She moved south, started the Milk! Records label with her partner, and in 2013 released "Avant Gardener" — a song about having a panic attack while gardening that somehow became the most relatable indie rock single of the decade.

Her trick is conversational surrealism: she sings the way Melbourne talks — flat, funny, suddenly devastating. Sometimes I Sit and Think, and Then I Speak (2015) earned her a Grammy nomination for Best New Artist and proved that rambling, wordy, defiantly unpolished guitar rock could still conquer the world in the streaming era.

She's also the rare export who never left. While peers relocated to London or LA, Barnett stayed in Melbourne, kept releasing on Milk!, and let the world come to the northern suburbs. The city repays loyalty; she tested the theory and won.

Melbourne roots

Barnett is the patron saint of Melbourne's band-room circuit. She came up through the sticky-carpet ecosystem — The Tote in Collingwood, The Corner Hotel in Richmond, the Northcote social clubs — where the city's real A&R has always happened: a Tuesday night, forty people, and a band that's better than the headliner. Milk! Records, run out of Melbourne, became the model for how the city's indie scene could export itself without moving to Sydney or London.

Her songs are full of the city's weather and its neuroses. "Depreston" is about house-hunting in the northern suburbs; "Pedestrian at Best" is about hating yourself at parties. It's all extremely Melbourne: self-deprecating, overcast, and smarter than it lets on.

One key track

"Avant Gardener" (2013). An asthma attack, a yard full of weeds, a melody that won't leave. It's the rare song about anxiety that actually sounds like anxiety — and like laughing about it with a friend.

She made mumbling sound like literature and Melbourne sound like the center of the universe. Both were already true.

Start here

Sometimes I Sit and Think, and Then I Speak (2015) — eleven songs, zero filler, and the best opening line of the decade: "Put me on a pedestal and I'll only disappoint you."
