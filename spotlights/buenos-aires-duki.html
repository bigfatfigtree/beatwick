ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BUENOS AIRES

Duki

From the Parque Rivadavia rap battles to the top of Latin trap — Almagro's favorite son.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JUNE 25, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Mauro Ezequiel Lombardo was born on June 24, 1996, in Almagro, Buenos Aires. Before the chains and the stadiums, he was a teenager freestyling in the city's plazas — and in 2016 he won El Quinto Escalón, the Parque Rivadavia rap-battle competition that functioned as Buenos Aires' underground draft. The prize was a recording session. He turned it into a movement.

The early singles — "No Vendo Trap," "She Don't Give a Fo," "Hello Cotto" — racked up millions of YouTube views and made Duki, alongside Ysy A and Neo Pistea (with whom he formed the trio Modo Diablo), the lead voice of Latin trap in Argentina. His debut album Súper Sangre Joven arrived in 2019; its lead single "Goteo" earned a Latin Grammy nomination for Best Rap/Hip Hop Song.

He is a Buenos Aires story through and through: the porteño kid who went from park battles to collaborating with Bad Bunny ("Hablamos Mañana," 2020) without leaving the city's orbit. The trap generation — Khea, Cazzu, Lit Killah, Tiago PZK — all runs through the door he kicked open.

The live show is a phenomenon — tens of thousands of porteños rapping every word back at him, the pogo at full tilt. He's headlined the country's biggest rooms and festivals, and the follow-up albums — Desde el Fin del Mundo (2021), Antes de Ameri (2023) — consolidated the reign. The kid from the park battles is the establishment now. The park battles, presumably, continue.

The key track

"Goteo" (2019). The drip anthem — Duki's slippery, melodic flow over a beat that sounds like money moving. It was the song that took Argentine trap from the plazas to the charts, and it still goes off like a fire alarm in a jewelry store.

Start here

"Goteo" first, then Súper Sangre Joven in full, then "She Don't Give a Fo" for the hungry early days. Then go find the El Quinto Escalón battle footage — every legend has an origin tape, and his is on YouTube.

He won a rap battle in a park and ended up running a genre. The park is still there. So is he, in a manner of speaking.
