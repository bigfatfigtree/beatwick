ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · ATLANTA

SLIMESITO

Clayton County's bilingual plugg ghost — running Atlanta's underground since the SoundCloud era, no label deal in sight.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JULY 15, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

“Yo No Se” was a SoundCloud-only 2016 release — no official video exists.

Every rap city has a figure the mainstream never quite figures out but the streets never stop talking about. In Atlanta, that's Slimesito. Eric Xavier Claros — born in Maryland, raised on Atlanta's south side, based in Clayton County — has been putting out dark, minimalist plugg and trap since the mid-2010s, rapping in English and Spanish over beats that sound like they were recorded in a parking garage at 3 a.m. His catalog is a labyrinth: Bloody Slime, EL SLIMELORD, KUTHROAT, Zombieland, GTA9 — and a loyal following that treats each drop like scripture. The FADER put him on Blogly, their discoveries column, in 2024. The underground put him on a pedestal a decade ago.

The origin story is pure Atlanta underground: a 2015 meeting with K$upreme, Lil Yachty's affiliate, locked him in, and the 2016 SoundCloud single "Yo No Se" — one of his earliest releases — started the local buzz that never died. Since then it's been collaborations with Trippie Redd, Duwap Kaine, and Surf Gang's Evilgiane, whose 2024 joint tape EVILSLIME was 22 minutes of locked-in raps and riveting beats that The FADER couldn't stop replaying. Production from Al Chapo, Dolan Beatz, MexikoDro, and SenseiATL keeps the sound in the family. "Queen Latifa" alone has millions of streams. No label, no rollout, no problem.

WHY ATLANTA CLAIMS HIM

Slimesito is the other Atlanta — not the trap giants, not the pop crossovers, but the SoundCloud-born, Clayton County reality that the city's underground actually runs on. Bilingual delivery, Panamanian roots, street-level storytelling: he's proof the city's rap ecosystem has rooms the industry never walks into. When the national press needs a "discovery," they find Slimesito. The locals found him nine years earlier.

THE KEY TRACK

"Yo No Se" — the 2016 original, and still the mission statement: menacing, minimal, and unmistakably Sito. For the current form, "MAKE IT HOME" from EVILSLIME is ninety seconds of long-O end rhymes that show the pen never dulled.

THE SOUND

Dark plugg and trap stripped to the bone: bass, bars, and sound effects. The bilingual bars give it a texture nobody else in the Atlanta underground has — Spanish phrases landing inside English verses like they were always meant to be there. It sounds like Clayton County feels at night.

START HERE

EVILSLIME with Evilgiane, then RSG, then the Bloody Slime EP for the early menace. Work backward through the mixtapes at your own pace — there are many.

Slimesito is Atlanta's underground memory — a decade deep, still independent, still the name the city says first when nobody's watching.
