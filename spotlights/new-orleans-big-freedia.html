ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · NEW ORLEANS

Big Freedia: the Queen of bounce

She took New Orleans bounce from the housing projects to Beyoncé's Formation. The Queen diva remains undefeated.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · SEPTEMBER 30, 2026 · 4 MIN READ

Bounce music was New Orleans' best-kept secret for twenty years — a hyperlocal, call-and-response party music born in the city's housing projects, built on the "Triggerman" beat and pure crowd energy. Then Freddie Ross Jr., performing as Big Freedia, became its global ambassador, and the secret got out.

Freedia's rise was grassroots and glorious: legendary live shows, the Fuse reality series Big Freedia: Queen of Bounce, and then the ultimate co-sign — Beyoncé sampling Freedia's voice on "Formation" (2016). When the biggest pop star on earth needs your ad-libs to make her point, you've won. A Grammy for the Renaissance album work followed, because the industry finally caught up to what New Orleans knew.

Why New Orleans claims her

Freedia is bounce's living archive and its future. She came up in the scene's church — the block parties, the clubs, the second-line-adjacent energy — and she never sanitized it for export. Her shows are participatory rituals: the call ("You already know!"), the response, the twerk circle as communion. She's also a cultural bridge, bringing bounce to audiences that had never heard of it while keeping the sound rooted in the neighborhoods that made it.

Local lore: ask about the world-record twerk attempts. New Orleans doesn't do anything halfway, including its Queen diva.

Bounce was a neighborhood secret. Big Freedia made it a world language — and never translated a word of it.

The co-signs keep coming because the sound is undeniable. Drake put Freedia's voice all over "Nice for What," one of the biggest hits of 2018, and every few years another pop star discovers what New Orleans knew in the '90s: bounce makes everything better. Through it all, Freedia stayed rooted — still repping the city, still commanding the stage like the Queen diva she is.

Key track

"Explode" — the quintessential Freedia record: the "Triggerman" backbone, the commanding voice, the pure kinetic joy. This is what a New Orleans block party sounds like with the volume maxed.

Start here

Press (2018) and the Central City EP for the essentials, then find live footage — Freedia is a live artist first, and the recordings are just evidence.

The Queen of Bounce didn't change for the world. The world changed its tempo to match.
