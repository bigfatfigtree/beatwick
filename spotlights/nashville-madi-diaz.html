ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · NASHVILLE

MADI DIAZ

Nashville's indie-folk survivor — 14 years in, an ANTI- deal, a Grammy nod, and the best breakup record of the decade.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · APRIL 22, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Madi Diaz is the patron saint of the Nashville almost-made-it — except she finally made it, fourteen years in. Raised in Lancaster County, Pennsylvania, Berklee dropout, Philly punk basements to Nashville to LA and back to Nashville with a few grand left in the bank: for a decade and change she paid the bills with co-writes and sync placements while her own records gathered dust. Then came the ANTI- Records deal in February 2021, the breakup album History of a Feeling that August, a Jimmy Kimmel appearance, an NPR Tiny Desk — and the call from Harry Styles, who'd heard her on a playlist and wanted her on guitar for his Love On Tour band. Overnight success, fourteen years in the making.

History of a Feeling is the record that explains the whole wait: a direct, introspective breakup album co-produced with Andrew Sarlo (Big Thief, Bon Iver), written as her long-term partner went through gender transition — heartache and compassion tangled together on every song. Then 2024's Weird Faith did the harder thing, writing honestly about falling in love again, and earned her Grammy nominations for Best Folk Album and Best Americana Performance for "Don't Do Me Good," her duet with Kacey Musgraves. The Philly kid who grew up on Hole and Bikini Kill became Nashville's most honest songwriter the long way.

WHY NASHVILLE CLAIMS HER

Because she's the East Nashville indie scene's proof of concept — the songwriter who stayed when the industry didn't call, kept writing, and got rewarded for the catalog instead of the connections. She moved back from LA in 2017 feeling chewed up; the city rebuilt her. Now her headline tours run through clubs and theaters on the strength of songs written in living rooms, and the younger Nashville indie acts cite her the way she once cited the punks.

THE KEY TRACK

"Man in Me" — the first ANTI- single, and the sound of an artist finally getting the production her songs always deserved. Or "New Person, Old Place" for the purest dose of the History of a Feeling ache: direct, unadorned, devastating.

THE PEN

Diaz writes pop-folk earworms with an honesty that earns the catchiness — the hooks land because the feelings are real. "Woman in My Heart" was written in a waking dream while learning to part with someone; "Same Risk" opens Weird Faith by asking if love could ruin your life. She doesn't flinch. That's the whole brand.

START HERE

History of a Feeling, then Weird Faith, then the It's Okay to Be Alone EP (2018) for the bridge between eras. The Tiny Desk from her living room is essential viewing.

Madi Diaz outlasted the industry's timeline and wrote the records that proved the wait was worth it.
