ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · ACCRA

SARKODIE

The fastest tongue in African rap built Ghana's hip-hop economy one December at a time.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · DECEMBER 10, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

There is fast, and then there is Sarkodie — Michael Owusu Addo, the Tema rapper whose Twi flows move at speeds that have made veteran MCs from London to New York visibly nervous. In 2019 he won Best International Flow at the BET Hip Hop Awards, but by then Accra had known for a decade: nobody on the continent raps like this man.

He built an empire without leaving home. Albums like Highest (2017) and a run of era-defining singles made him the blueprint for how an African rapper stays independent, stays wealthy, and stays relevant. His annual Rapperholic concert has become December in Accra's rap Super Bowl — the night the whole city shows up to confirm what it already knows.

WHY ACCRA CLAIMS HIM

Tema-born, Accra-raised, and permanently present. Sarkodie didn't relocate to Lagos or London to become Africa's biggest rapper — he stayed, built the infrastructure around himself, and made the industry come to him. Every Ghanaian MC under thirty is working in a house he framed.

THE KEY TRACK

“Adonai” ft. Castro (2014). A tribute-turned-anthem after fellow star Castro's disappearance that year — and the song that proved Sarkodie could carry grief and groove in the same breath. It remains one of the defining records of the decade in Ghana.

THE LANGUAGE OF IT

Here's the thing the industry still hasn't fully processed: Sarkodie did it in Twi. At a time when every A&R on the continent was begging rappers to switch to English for the crossover, the fastest tongue in African rap stayed in his mother tongue and made the world come to him. The lesson of Sarkodie's career is that local isn't a limitation — it's a weapon.

THE EMPIRE

Sarkcess Music, the Rapperholic concert franchise, the endorsements — Sarkodie runs one of the most durable independent operations in African music. No major-label rescue, no relocation, no reinvention. Just bars, business, and December.

START HERE

“Adonai,” then “Otan” (2023) for the late-career fire, then the Highest album. Try to keep up.

Rap gods are supposed to be distant. Sarkodie just built his throne in Tema and let the world fly in.
