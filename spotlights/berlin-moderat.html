ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BERLIN

Moderat

Two Berlin electronic institutions plus one ghostly voice — the supergroup that made techno cry.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JANUARY 27, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Moderat started as a one-off: Modeselektor — Gernot Bronsert and Sebastian Szary, Berlin's bass-music pranksters — teaming up with Apparat, aka Sascha Ring, the city's melancholy electronic balladeer. The 2002 EP was supposed to be a lark. It took seven years to become an album, and when Moderat finally landed in 2009, it was clear this was no side project. It was the sound of Berlin electronic music growing up and getting its heart broken.

The alchemy is the personnel. Modeselektor bring the muscle — hip-hop drums, dub pressure, rave chaos, all of it filtered through their Monkeytown Records mischief. Apparat brings the ache — that fragile, processed voice singing about distance and damage over the top. Together they make music that works at 3 a.m. in a warehouse and at 3 p.m. on headphones, which is a trick almost nobody else pulls off.

Berlin is baked into every layer. All three members came up through the city's post-Wall ecosystem — Modeselektor and Apparat both launched via Ellen Allien's BPitch Control — and the group's visual identity, built with the Pfadfinderei design collective, has the same stark, architectural confidence as the city itself. Their live shows became legendary: a wall of light and fog, Ring's voice floating over Bronsert and Szary's controlled detonations.

II (2013) was the commercial breakthrough, III (2016) the darker, bigger statement. Then they split — announced a hiatus in 2017 after a final run of shows — and Berlin mourned. The 2022 return with More D4ta felt like the city exhaling. Some supergroups are marketing. This one is chemistry: three Berlin lifers who separately defined the city's sound, together defining what it feels like.

The key track

"A New Error" (2009). The opener of the debut album and still the mission statement — a beat that stalks, synths that bloom like neon, and Ring's voice arriving like a transmission from a better world. It's the sound of the Berlin night rendered as pop music.

Start here

II (2013) — "Bad Kingdom," "Milk," the whole arc from tension to release. It's the most complete version of the Moderat idea. Then the self-titled debut for the rawer original, and III when you're ready for the comedown.

The take: Moderat proved Berlin's electronic scene could do emotion without going soft and scale without going stupid. The supergroup that actually deserved the name — because the city that made them is in every bar.
