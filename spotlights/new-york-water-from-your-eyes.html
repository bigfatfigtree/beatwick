ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · NEW YORK

WATER FROM YOUR EYES

Brooklyn's weirdest pop duo — Nate Amos and Rachel Brown make experimental dance music that keeps accidentally becoming everyone's favorite record.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · NOVEMBER 26, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Life Signs” — the official music video, directed by Rachel Brown.

Every DIY scene has a band that makes songs nobody can describe and everybody plays. In Brooklyn, that's Water From Your Eyes. Nate Amos and Rachel Brown met at a DIY show in Chicago in 2016, moved to Brooklyn, and started building a catalog of knotty, funny, heartbreakingly melodic music that blends dance music, post-punk, and jokes nobody else would dare put on a record. Then the "weird little project" signed to Matador Records in 2023, and the joke was on anyone who thought they wouldn't fit.

Both halves arrived with their own followings — Amos as This Is Lorelei, Brown as Thanks for Coming — but the duo is where the chemistry detonates. 2019's Somebody Else's Song earned them Pitchfork and Stereogum co-signs from the Brooklyn DIY underground; 2021's Structure was a concept album that poked fun at concept albums. Then came Everyone's Crushed (2023), their Matador debut, a record so twisty and tender it became a year-end-list fixture. Their seventh album, It's a Beautiful Place, arrived in August 2025 — and by then they'd already opened for Interpol in front of roughly 160,000 people in Mexico City's Zócalo.

WHY BROOKLYN CLAIMS THEM

They are the platonic ideal of a Brooklyn DIY band that ate the industry instead of the other way around. Years of Wharf Cat and Exploding in Sound releases, house shows, and self-deprecation built a fanbase so loyal that when Matador came calling, nobody felt betrayed — it felt like a heist. They still write like people with nothing to lose: Rachel's effortless vocals over Amos' impossibly layered instrumentals, lyrics that swing from devastating to deeply unserious within the same verse.

THE KEY TRACK

"Life Signs" (2025), the lead single from It's a Beautiful Place — jittery, euphoric, and built around one of those choruses that makes you laugh and then immediately get emotional about it. If you're starting from zero, though, go to "14" from Everyone's Crushed: strings, electronic drone, Brown's floating vocal, and the single funniest opening line you'll hear all year. Yes, it's the "throw up" one.

THE CHEMISTRY

The real story here is the partnership. They've dated, broken up, kept making records through it, and the music got better — Everyone's Crushed is literally about learning to collaborate as exes. There's no precedent for a pop band this playful about its own emotional wreckage. The humor isn't a shield; it's the instrument.

START HERE

Everyone's Crushed, then It's a Beautiful Place, then Structure for the deep end. Check the solo projects — This Is Lorelei and Thanks for Coming — for the raw materials.

Water From Your Eyes makes experimental music with pop instincts. The scene heard them first. The world is just catching up.
