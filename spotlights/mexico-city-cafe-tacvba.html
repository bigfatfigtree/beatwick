ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MEXICO CITY

Café Tacvba

Four kids from Satélite who became the most inventive band in rock en español.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · NOVEMBER 19, 2025 · 2 MIN READ

In 1989, in Ciudad Satélite — the planned suburb north of Mexico City — four friends started a band called Alicia Ya No Vive Aquí. They soon renamed themselves after the Café de Tacuba, the legendary downtown restaurant that has been serving poblano mole since 1912. Rubén Albarrán, Meme, Joselo Rangel, and Enrique Rangel became Café Tacvba, and rock en español was never the same.

The landmark is Re (1994), a sprawling double album that treated genre like a buffet: ska, punk, bolero, norteño, electronica, and jokes, often inside a single song. "La Ingrata" — a norteño parody so good it became an anthem — sits next to experiments nobody else in Latin rock would attempt. They followed it with the electronic Revés/YoSoy (1999), an instrumental record released as two EPs, because of course they did.

Mexico City is all over this band: the suburban-kid hunger of Satélite, the downtown mythology of the café, the capital's appetite for beautiful weirdness. They went from club gigs to headlining the Auditorio Nacional and anchoring Vive Latino lineups, proving that the strangest band in the room could also be the biggest.

They're visual pranksters, too — Albarrán performs in costume, the artwork is deliberately odd, and their 1995 MTV Unplugged remains a landmark of the format in Spanish. Three decades in, they've influenced basically every interesting band in Mexico. In this city, loving Café Tacvba isn't a taste. It's a civic duty.

The key track

"Eres," from Cuatro Caminos (2003). Three minutes of exposed nerve — Albarrán's voice at its most tender over a melody that sounds inevitable, like it was always in the air over the city. It became the wedding song of a generation and the proof that avant-garde instincts and mass emotion can live in one track.

Start here

Begin with "Eres," then play Re in full — it's a two-hour education. Follow with "Volver a Comenzar" and "La Ingrata" to hear the full range: the romantic, the rowdy, and the completely unhinged.

Every scene has one band that makes everything else possible. In Mexico City, it's the four weirdos from Satélite who never once played it safe.
