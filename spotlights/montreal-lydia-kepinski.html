ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MONTREAL

LYDIA KÉPINSKI

Montreal's francophone chaos-pop queen — she turned a Francouvertes win into two Polaris-longlisted albums of pure theatrical pop.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 11, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

There is nobody else in Quebec pop doing what Lydia Képinski does, mostly because nobody else would dare. Born in Montreal on June 1, 1994 — her debut album Premier juin is literally named for her own birthday — Képinski won the Francouvertes song competition in 2017 and immediately started building the most theatrical, unpredictable career in francophone indie music: pop-rock with eurodance streaks, drag-culture fever, elaborate live productions, and songs about Pie-IX Boulevard that treat the city's ugly corners like mythology. Two Polaris Music Prize longlists. Six ADISQ nominations. Zero interest in playing it safe.

Premier juin (2018) arrived fully formed: bilingual-tinged French pop that moves between synth euphoria and rock grit, anchored by the songwriting that earned "Apprendre à mentir" a SOCAN Songwriting Prize nomination. The 2022 follow-up Depuis — built with multi-instrumentalist Blaise Borböen-Léonard — turned up the drama, drawing on drag culture and tour-life disorientation, launching at SXSW and carrying her through more than a hundred shows across Quebec, France, Belgium, and the US. In 2024 she dropped "Juste amis" with Simon Proulx of Les Trois Accords — the chaos-pop queen linking up with Quebec's most beloved rock institution, and it worked.

WHY MONTREAL CLAIMS HER

Her shows are Montreal events: full-costume, full-choreography, fake-collapse-mid-song productions at Pop Montreal, the Francos, and the Mile End's Théâtre Fairmount, where she turned the floor into a dance pit she had to officially declare a "safe space for dance." She writes in French about the city in French — the tunnels, the boulevards, the bad neighborhoods — and the francophone underground treats her like a generational talent because she is one. Her label is the tiny independent Chivi Chivi. The machine is her.

THE KEY TRACK

"Apprendre à mentir" — the 2017 single that started everything. It's the mission statement: clever, melodic, slightly unhinged French pop about the performance of honesty, delivered by a singer who treats every show like it might be her last. Start there, then let "Premier juin" and "Pie-IX" fill in the map.

THE THEATRE

Képinski doesn't perform songs so much as stage productions that happen to have great songs in them. The choreography, the costumes, the mid-show collapses that turn out to be part of the act — it's the most committed live show in Montreal pop, and the francophone press has been documenting the spectacle for years.

START HERE

Premier juin first, then Depuis, then the EP for the raw early version. Watch a full concert video after — the recordings only tell half the story.

Lydia Képinski made French-language pop the most fun room in Montreal. The Polaris lists agree. The rest of the world is late.
