ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · PARIS

YOA

Paris' electro-pop prodigy went from theater kid to Victoires de la Musique revelation — La Favorite is the most fun French pop record in years.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 18, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Matcha Queen” — the clip officiel.

Yoa started in the theater. You can hear it — every song is a little staged, a little costumed, a little bit winking at the audience. Yoanna Bolzli, born in Paris in 1998, co-wrote and performed a play for two years before releasing bedroom pop that France 24 called the work of a “young Parisian to follow.” France followed. Her debut album La Favorite dropped in January 2025 on the indie Panenka Music, and the Victoires de la Musique named her Révélation Scène the same year.

The sound is electro-pop with teeth: pulsating electronics, brutally honest lyrics about friendship, reconstruction, mental health and sexuality, delivered with a mischievous irony that made her a press darling. The 2024 single “Matcha Queen” was the breakout; the Nulle EP sharpened the edges; La Favorite — fifteen tracks, all killer — turned her into the girl the festivals fight over. She played the Olympia in April 2025 and has the Salle Pleyel dates that signal you've properly arrived.

WHY PARIS CLAIMS HER

Born and raised in Paris, signed to Panenka, selected for Les Inouïs du Printemps de Bourges and the Francofolies — Yoa is a product of the French talent pipeline at its best. Her all-female live crew and theatrical stagecraft made her the word-of-mouth act of the 2025 festival season, and she did it singing in French about things French pop usually whispers about.

THE KEY TRACK

“Matcha Queen” (2024). The single that broke her: fizzy, self-aware, endlessly quotable pop about being the moment. It's the track every French DJ under 30 knows by heart, and the reason La Favorite had an audience waiting before it dropped.

THE VOICE

Conversational and theatrical at once — like the coolest girl at the party explaining something devastating while the beat drops. Her lyrics are cash and feminist, teased with irony, and her phrasing does half the comedy work. On stage she's pure energy: dancers, drama, and that Victoires-winning live reputation fully earned.

START HERE

La Favorite in full — fifteen tracks, no skips. Then “Matcha Queen” again, then the Nulle EP for the darker bedroom origins.

Yoa turned a theater degree into French pop's best show. The stages are only getting bigger.
