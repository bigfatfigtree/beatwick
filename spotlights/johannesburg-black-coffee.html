ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · JOHANNESBURG

BLACK COFFEE

From Umlazi to a sold-out Madison Square Garden — the DJ who turned South African house into a global language.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · NOVEMBER 13, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

On October 7, 2023, Nkosinathi Maphumulo became the first South African to headline a sold-out Madison Square Garden. It was the latest impossible room for a man from Umlazi, Durban, who rebuilt house music in South Africa's image: deep, patient, and heavy enough to move a 20,000-seat arena without ever raising his voice.

The story runs through Joburg. A taxi-rank accident in his youth rerouted a possible football career toward the decks, and the city became his laboratory — Soulistic Music in 2005, the 2009 breakout of “Superman” sung by Bucie, and endless club sets that built the reputation. In 2022 he became the first African DJ to win a Grammy, for Subconsciously. The Garden was just confirmation.

WHY JOBURG CLAIMS HIM

He's KZN-born, but Joburg is where the sound matured: the city's house scene, its all-night rooms, its uncompromising dancefloors. And when New York finally needed a lineup, he brought Joburg with him — Major League DJz, Bucie, Msaki and Oskido all on the MSG bill. He didn't just play the Garden. He smuggled the city in.

THE KEY TRACK

“Superman” ft. Bucie (2009). Hypnotic, soulful, and still the blueprint — the song he once said deserved to be sung by Beyoncé without changing a thing. Drake borrowed it for More Life; the world never forgot who made it.

THE IBIZA YEARS

Long before the Garden, there was Hï Ibiza — his long-running residency that made him the face of Afro-house in Europe and proved his sound could hold a room that had heard everything. The residency turned into a laboratory: sets that moved from deep house to amapiano-adjacent grooves without ever losing the thread, and a global audience that learned to trust his patience.

THE LABEL

Soulistic Music, founded in 2005, is the other half of the legacy — a pipeline for the next generation of South African house talent. Black Coffee doesn't just open doors for Joburg's producers; he builds the rooms behind the doors.

START HERE

“Superman,” then “We Dance Again” with Nakhane, then Subconsciously front to back. That's the curriculum.

Joburg has produced global sounds before. Black Coffee is the one who carried one all the way to the Garden.
