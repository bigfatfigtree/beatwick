ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · AMSTERDAM

UPSAMMY

Amsterdam's IDM naturalist turns melting ice and rustling leaves into Dekmantel-certified electronica — the De School residency that became a career.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · APRIL 15, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

No official “Melt in My Heated Hand” video could be verified — start with the record.

Thessa Torsing went to art school to study Image and Media Technology, spent a winter making Ableton tracks to escape an unfulfilling internship, and accidentally invented a whole musical world. As upsammy she makes IDM that sounds grown rather than built — percolating rhythms, slowed-down rustling leaves, lilac chimes rising and falling over dub-techno hums. The chunk of melting ice on the cover of her 2020 debut Zoom (Dekmantel) is the metaphor: an ode to looking closely at tiny, impermanent things.

She broke through with a residency at Amsterdam's beloved De School, where RA called her one of the scene's most promising new artists, and the records kept leveling up: Zoom's impish late-'90s IDM spirit, the Bend EP's dualistic electronica, and 2023's Germ in a Population of Buildings — a fluid, sharpened meditation on intelligent design and natural wonder. She's put out music on Nous'Klaer, AD 93 and PAN, done an NTS residency, and played Dekmantel, Le Guess Who? and Robert Johnson Berlin. Organic and synthetic, melted together, unmistakably hers.

WHY AMSTERDAM CLAIMS HER

The De School residency is the origin story — the club that defined Amsterdam's underground decade made her a resident when she was barely known. She lives in Amsterdam, records its nature sounds, and carries the city's whole post-Dekmantel electronic lineage (ambient, electro, IDM, dub techno) in her sets, which can go from low-key ambient bliss to off-kilter electro workouts in an hour.

THE KEY TRACK

“Melt in My Heated Hand” (2020). The Zoom opener: woozy, melting, alive — the ice cube on the cover rendered as sound. For the later era, “Embrytone” (2024) is superbly intricate rhythms and chimes, upsammy at her most detailed.

THE VOICE

An instrumentalist's voice — her signature is texture, not singing. She fuses ambient, IDM, techno and half-time drum and bass into settings that are playful with intensity and deeply emotive, blending the organic and synthetic so completely you can't tell where the field recording ends and the synth begins. “Straight lines aren't really her thing,” as the press keeps noting.

START HERE

Zoom for the debut magic, Germ in a Population of Buildings for the mature thesis, and an archived NTS set for the DJ side. Then try listening to rain differently.

upsammy hears music in melting ice. After five minutes of her records, you will too.
