ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BARCELONA

MUSHKAA

Bad Gyal's little sister became Catalan urbano's most self-reliant star — self-released, singing in Catalan, and headlining festivals on her own terms.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JUNE 10, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Being Bad Gyal's younger sister gets you noticed; doing everything yourself keeps you there. Irma Farelo, from Vilassar de Mar on the Maresme coast, started releasing music as Mushkaa in 2021 with the single “Res Kla” — 100,000 Spotify streams in no time, from an artist who was 17 and unsigned. She's been unsigned ever since, on purpose. Her first EP, Tas Loko Mixtape (2023), was self-released and still won the Enderrock award for best urban album by critics' choice, plus Best Revelation for her. The La Mercè festival booked her to sing for 18,000 people.

Her thing is Catalan-language urbano with a sentimental, nostalgic streak — reggaetón, R&B, dancehall and Latin trap, sung slowly and intimately, unlike anything else in the genre's current hyper-speed era. The debut album Sexy Sensible (2024) produced with Roots and Bexnil doubled down on the formula and sold out two Apollo shows in Barcelona. Then she swerved: Nova Bossa (2025) folds bossa nova, samba, cumbia and bachata into her world, with the single “LOTUS” shot in Rio de Janeiro. She headlined Festival B in 2026 with a five-piece band like it was a house party. Nobody in Catalonia is moving like her right now.

WHY BARCELONA CLAIMS HER

The new Catalan urban scene — Julieta, The Tyets, Figa Flawas, Mushkaa — is the most exciting thing in Spanish music, and Mushkaa is its most independent operator. She sings in Catalan, self-releases, tours the festival circuit (Sónar, Canet Rock, Primavera's orbit) and keeps the majors at arm's length. Barcelona built the scene; she just refuses to sell it back.

THE KEY TRACK

“Sembla mentida” (2023). The track that doubled her debut single's numbers and became the scene's anthem — slow, aching, nostalgic urbano in Catalan. The “LOTUS” video from Nova Bossa is the current-era flex.

THE VOICE

Soft where the genre is hard, intimate where it's brash. Mushkaa's voice moves at a tempo the dembow world usually sprints past — that “sentimental and nostalgic” touch the Catalan press keeps reaching for. She learned trumpet for ten years as a kid, and there's a musicality in her phrasing most urbano singers don't bother with.

START HERE

Tas Loko Mixtape for the origin story, Sexy Sensible for the statement, Nova Bossa for the curveball. Then check whether your city has a La Mercè — it doesn't, but her tour might reach you anyway.

Mushkaa could have ridden a famous surname. She built her own lane, in her own language, on her own label.
