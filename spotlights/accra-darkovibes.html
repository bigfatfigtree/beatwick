ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · ACCRA

DARKOVIBES

La Meme Gang's best-dressed enigma — the high priest of Accra's alté scene who put highlife, Ga rhythms and Jamestown pride back at the center of Ghanaian cool.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JULY 29, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Mike Tyson” — with Runtown — the official video, shot in Jamestown.

Paul Nii Amu Andrew Darko was born in Labone, in the Greater Accra Region, and came up through the city's alté underground with the La Meme Gang collective — the crew behind the 2017 La Meme Tape that redrew Accra's musical map. His 2016 breakout single "Mercy" announced a voice unlike anything on Ghanaian radio, and a 2017 Ghana Music Awards nomination — for his turn on Edem's "Egboame" remix — confirmed the industry had noticed. Then Vogue UK came calling, profiling his style during the Chalewote street art festival. Fashion, sound, attitude: the full package arrived at once.

The debut album Kpanlogo (2020) was the thesis statement — named for the Ga dance and music form born in Accra in the 1960s, and a love letter to Jamestown, the neighborhood that raised his sound. Its single "Mike Tyson" with Runtown filmed its video in a Jamestown boxing gym, because where else? The Cornerstone EP followed the same year. His collaborator list reads like a map of where Ghanaian music actually lives: KiDi ("Bless Me"), Stonebwoy ("Stay Woke"), King Promise ("Inna Song"), Davido ("Je M'appelle"), Runtown ("Body Riddim"). He glides between Ga, Twi, English and pidgin like the city talks.

WHY ACCRA CLAIMS HIM

Darkovibes made local pride a global aesthetic. While his peers chased generic Afrobeats polish, he went the other way — into Ga rhythms, Jamestown imagery, highlife textures — and made it the coolest thing in the room. Accra's alté scene has a king, and he dresses like one.

THE KEY TRACK

"Mike Tyson" (2020), with Runtown. The Kpanlogo statement of intent: a warning shot to the industry filmed among Jamestown's boxers, dancers and relics. The Kpanlogo drum pattern — one of the oldest key rhythms in sub-Saharan music — hits like a fact being stated, not argued.

THE STYLE

Both kinds. The music fuses contemporary pop with older forms — highlife, Ga traditional rhythms — into something no one else in Ghana is making. And the fashion got him into Vogue before most of his peers had a stylist. In the La Meme Gang era, Darkovibes was always the one you watched.

START HERE

Kpanlogo front to back, then "Mike Tyson," then "Mercy" for the raw origin and "Inna Song" with King Promise for the romance.

Darkovibes didn't make Accra's underground global. He just refused to leave it — and the world came to him.
