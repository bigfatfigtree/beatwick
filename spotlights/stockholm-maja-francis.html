ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · STOCKHOLM

MAJA FRANCIS

Sweden's 'Emo Dolly Parton' — the Stockholm indie-pop songwriter whose Anxious Angel closed Netflix's Love & Anarchy and whose Mama enlisted First Aid Kit.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · SEPTEMBER 23, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Anxious Angel” — the official acoustic session.

The nickname is “Emo Dolly Parton,” and it's earned twice over: Maja Francis sings emotional songs in a voice with a striking similarity to Dolly's — the trill, the airy melody — over music that swerves from indie pop to country-soul. Born in Helsingborg, raised by the ocean in Ängelholm, now based in Stockholm, she started in café shows at 20, debuted with the Come Companion EP (2016), and took a break after the Cry Baby EPs when the music stopped feeling like hers.

She came back in love with it, and it shows. Her debut album A Pink Soft Mess (2021) — written as a diary, then voice memos, then songs — featured “Mama” with longtime friends First Aid Kit and a cover of Kate Bush's “This Woman's Work.” Her single “Anxious Angel” closed out the Netflix series Love & Anarchy, and 2024's Hello Cowboy pushed the Dolly comparison further into twang and heartbreak territory. She loves small stages, close audiences, and pink — pink is, in her words, the new black.

WHY STOCKHOLM CLAIMS HER

Francis is Stockholm's indie conscience: the songwriter the folk-pop establishment (First Aid Kit and company) took under their wings and who repaid it with a catalog of fearless sad songs. In a city that exports polish, she exports vulnerability — oversharing stark details in moments of sheer vulnerability, as one reviewer put it, in places other songwriters won't go.

THE KEY TRACK

“Anxious Angel” (2020). The Netflix closer: cooing, aching, the opening track of A Pink Soft Mess and the distillation of everything she does. For the twang era, “Mama” with First Aid Kit.

THE VOICE

The voice is the whole pitch — a Dolly-adjacent trill wrapped around brutally honest lyrics about loneliness, wrong turns and love lost. Over muted guitar or full-band country-pop, she sings about pouring wine and not caring, about holding her mother's hand. It's country by way of Swedish indie, and it shouldn't work as well as it does.

START HERE

A Pink Soft Mess for the mission statement, Hello Cowboy for the twang, then the Holler “Mama” video for the First Aid Kit summit. Small stages preferred — she's said so herself.

Maja Francis fell out of love with music, then wrote a diary that became an album. Falling back in has never sounded this good.
