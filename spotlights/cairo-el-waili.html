ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · CAIRO

EL WAILI

Cairo's trippiest producer — a psychedelic shaabi visionary warping Egypt's street sound into something sci-fi, cinematic and entirely his own.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · SEPTEMBER 16, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “El Odissa” — the retrofuturistic instrumental — visuals by NUUN.

El Waili emerged with a psychedelic shaabi sound nobody in Cairo had quite heard before — the street-level pulse of mahraganat stretched, bent and lit up with synthesizers, like electro-chaabi beamed in from a different century. The Egyptian press noticed fast: a huge 2021 saw him sign with El Batron, Cairo's hip-hop label, and land a digital distribution deal with Saudi label Wall of Sound Records, taking his music across the region.

His collaborations show the range. "Ala Nar" paired him with Jordan's Zaid Khaled and Idreesi. Then came the Donia Wael trilogy — "3asal," "3dd El Sokan," and "Masra7 Gareema" — her dreamlike vocals floating over his trippy, left-field production, shaabi-synth textures stretched into something cinematic. And then there's "El Odissa" (2021), the solo instrumental that proved he doesn't need a vocalist at all: retrofuturistic ambience giving way to hip-pop beats, Arab seasoning on every transition, pacing that tells a story without a single word. His Bandcamp tapes, including The Mixtape Vol.2, are free — as if the music itself refuses to sit still.

WHY CAIRO CLAIMS HIM

Cairo's underground runs on wedding-party synths and street MCs; El Waili took that DNA and gave it a psychedelic upgrade. He's the producer the city's alternative scene points to when it wants to prove mahraganat's raw material can become art-house cinema.

THE KEY TRACK

"El Odissa" (2021). No vocals, no safety net — just attention to detail and a producer in full storytelling mode. Retrofuturistic synths, a hip-pop undercurrent, and pacing that ebbs and flows like a film score. The purest dose of the Waili formula.

THE PRODUCTION

Everything he touches gets warped: echoed harmonies, Rhodes chords, synth-drenched choruses, bridges that dissolve into barely-there piano before exploding back into keyboard paradise. It's psychedelia built from shaabi parts — and it sounds like nothing else coming out of Cairo.

START HERE

"El Odissa," then the Donia Wael collaborations ("Masra7 Gareema" first), then the free Bandcamp tapes for the deep cut.

El Waili took Cairo's street sound and launched it into orbit. The city is still catching up.
