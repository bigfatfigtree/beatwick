ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · NEW YORK

MIKE

Brooklyn's underground poet laureate — hazy, sample-drenched, fully independent, and the rapper the rest of the underground cites first.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · NOVEMBER 5, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “nuthin i can do is wrng” — the Beware of the Monkey opener — the official video, directed by Ryosuke Tanzawa.

There is a Brooklyn rap tradition of kids who recorded their diary entries over dusty soul loops and never stopped, and MIKE is the one the rest of the underground points to when it needs a standard-bearer. Michael Jordan Bonema has released around ten projects through his own 10k label — muffled, meditative records about grief, family, and faith that are allergic to the industry's idea of polish. Critics keep calling him the future of New York rap. The records are better than the labels anyway.

The 2022 album Beware of the Monkey is the widest door in: thirteen tracks, produced entirely by MIKE himself under his dj blackpower alias, built on samples that sound rescued from a flooded basement. When Jamaican legend Sister Nancy showed up on "Stop Worry!", it felt less like a feature and more like a blessing — the old guard handing the torch to a kid who earned it one sleepy, devastating verse at a time. The press caught up fast, from Stereogum to BrooklynVegan, but the music never moved an inch toward the mainstream to meet them.

WHY BROOKLYN CLAIMS HIM

MIKE is Brooklyn's underground in human form — raised in the borough, formed in the sLUms collective alongside Medhane and Jadasea, and still headlining the free Young World festival every year like it's a civic duty. When Earl Sweatshirt and The Alchemist both co-signed him — the 2022 collaborative EP One More with Wiki is essential listening — it confirmed what the block already knew: you don't need a major label, you need a neighborhood. He has one.

THE KEY TRACK

"nuthin i can do is wrng." The opener to Beware of the Monkey, and the closest thing underground New York rap has to a thesis statement: a kid walking through the city unable to look up, surrounded by surface-level noise, trying to find stillness. Over a loop that sounds like a Sunday morning, MIKE raps like he's taking inventory of his soul. It never resolves. That's the point.

THE PRODUCTION

The beats deserve as much space as the bars. As dj blackpower, MIKE makes off-kilter drums and half-buried soul samples loop until they decay — rap as bedroom jazz. Nobody else's production sounds like rain on a fire escape.

START HERE

Beware of the Monkey, then DISCO! (2021) for the sunnier side, then May God Bless Your Hustle (2017) for the origin story. Then One More with Wiki and The Alchemist for the collaboration magic.

MIKE doesn't need the industry's permission. He has Brooklyn, a label, and a free festival. That's bigger.
