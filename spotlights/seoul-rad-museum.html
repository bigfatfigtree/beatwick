ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · SEOUL

RAD MUSEUM

The DEAN-discovered alt-R&B singer who designs his own album covers, earned KMA nominations on his very first EP, and makes sadness sound expensive.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · FEBRUARY 17, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Off-Line” — the official video with DEAN and Lee Hi.

Getting discovered by DEAN is the Korean R&B equivalent of being drafted first overall — and Rad Museum made the scouts look smart immediately. Previously recording under the name Camper, he released his debut EP Scene in 2017 and was nominated at the 2018 Korean Music Awards for Best New Artist and Best R&B/Soul album. That's not a slow build. That's arriving with your first release already framed on the wall.

But the music is only half the story. Rad Museum is also a graphic artist and visual director who designs his own album covers, packaging, and music videos — the whole world of the record comes out of his head, not a label's mood board. His debut studio album RAD (2022) was a thirteen-track widescreen statement: "Off-Line" with DEAN and LeeHi, "AirDrop" with Wonstein, "EXIT" with JUSTHIS, "4D" with miso and Loco, and the slow-burn centerpiece "Wet Umbrella," which DEAN co-composed. It's the sound of an artist who treats every release like an exhibition opening.

He rolls with you.will.knovv, DEAN's label, alongside Tabber and miso — the trio took their you.will.knovv tour to North America in January 2024, hitting Toronto, New York, Los Angeles, and Seattle. And he's a member of Clubeskimo, the Seoul collective orbit that birthed the city's alternative R&B wave. In other words: every room that matters in Korean R&B has Rad Museum on the guest list.

WHY SEOUL CLAIMS HIM

Because Seoul's R&B underground runs on exactly this profile — singer, producer, and visual artist in one body, fluent in the city's late-night melancholy. Rad Museum sings the 2am Seoul blues: rain on umbrellas, unread messages, the glow of a convenience store at the end of a bad day. The city made the mood; he just gave it a cover design.

THE KEY TRACK

"Off-Line" (2022), featuring DEAN and LeeHi. It's the thesis statement — hushed, nocturnal, and stacked with the two biggest voices in Korean alt-R&B endorsing the kid they found first. When DEAN co-signs you on your own record, the argument is over.

THE SOUND

Alternative R&B with an art-school finish: muted guitars, dripping-wet synths, drums that barely raise their voice, and Rad Museum's soft, close-mic'd delivery floating above it all. "Wet Umbrella" and "Dancing In The Rain" are his natural habitat — songs that sound like the weather they describe.

START HERE

"Off-Line," then the Scene EP for the raw origin ("Dancing In The Rain"), then all thirteen tracks of RAD in order — it plays like a gallery walk.

Rad Museum curates his career like his cover art: nothing loud, everything intentional, and it all hits.
