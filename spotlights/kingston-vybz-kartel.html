ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · KINGSTON

Vybz Kartel

The Worl' Boss — dancehall's most prolific mind, back where he belongs: on stage.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 26, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Adidja Palmer was born in 1976 and raised in Waterford, Portmore — in Kingston's orbit, in dancehall's blast radius. As a teenager he became a protégé of Bounty Killer, writing songs for the veteran before he could legally enter the clubs where they'd play. He took the name Vybz Kartel and proceeded to become the most prolific, most imitated deejay in the history of the genre.

Then the interruption: convicted in 2014 over the 2011 killing of Clive "Lizard" Williams, sentenced to life. Kartel responded by doing the unthinkable — he kept releasing music from behind bars, recording on an iPhone, dropping albums including King of the Dancehall (2016). "Fever" went gold in the U.S. while he was in a cell. Dancehall went mainstream around the world, and its biggest star watched from prison.

In March 2024 the UK Privy Council overturned the conviction over juror misconduct; that July, Jamaica's Court of Appeal declined to order a retrial, and Kartel walked free on July 31, 2024. On New Year's Eve he played Kingston's National Stadium before thirty thousand people. Grammy nominations, arena tours, and a victory lap across the world's stages followed. The return was as swift as it was undeniable.

The post-release run has been relentless: festival headlines, arena dates, Grammy nominations, and a catalog that keeps growing like the lost years never happened. Younger deejays still study his flows like scripture. Dancehall's center of gravity, it turns out, was wherever he was standing.

The key track

"Clarks," from 2010, with Popcaan and Gaza Slim. A love letter to the desert boot that became dancehall's uniform — it sat in North America's top three reggae singles for forty weeks and turned a shoe into a cultural institution. If one track explains Kartel's gift for making the specific universal, it's this one.

Start here

"Clarks" first, then "Fever" for the slow-burn menace, then "Romping Shop" with Spice — a Billboard Hot 100 entry — for the chemistry. Then understand the lineage: he launched the careers of Popcaan, Spice, and Shenseea. The Worl' Boss title isn't branding. It's a job description.

Thirteen years behind bars, and he never stopped being the most important voice in the room. Some artists have careers. Kartel has gravity.
