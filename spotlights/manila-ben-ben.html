ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MANILA

Ben&Ben

Twin brothers, nine members, one giant folk-pop hug — Ben&Ben turned Manila's hugot into anthems.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · AUGUST 5, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Paolo and Miguel Guico are twins who started busking and uploading songs as The Benjamins, then expanded into a nine-piece folk-pop orchestra called Ben&Ben. Formed in Manila in 2016, they make music for the Filipino emotional specialty known as hugot — the art of pulling feelings out of nowhere and aiming them directly at the chest.

"Kathang Isip" (2017) was the breakthrough: a song about loving someone who only exists in your imagination, which is to say, a song about being young in Manila. Limasawa Street (2021) turned them into arena-fillers. They are, by several measures, the most-streamed Filipino band in history — and they did it without a single drop of cynicism.

Their concerts are famous for the singalongs — nine voices on stage, thousands in the crowd, everybody crying a little. It's church for people who grew up on OPM.

Manila roots

Ben&Ben's Manila is the city's soft underbelly. Their songs are full of UP campus afternoons, jeepney-window daydreams, and the particular ache of loving a city that's always leaving you on read. They came up through the gig circuit — 70s Bistro, Conspiracy Garden Cafe, the school fairs — where Manila's real audiences live: students with guitars and feelings.

What makes them a Manila story rather than just a success story is the scale of the sincerity. Nine members, violins and all, playing folk-pop in an era of trap beats — and the country's biggest streaming numbers anyway. Manila didn't just accept the earnestness; it demanded more of it.

One key track

"Kathang Isip" (2017). "Just a figment of my imagination" — the hugot anthem that launched a thousand karaoke tears. Try to listen once. You won't.

Even their album titles carry the history-class romance: Limasawa Street is named for the island where Magellan's expedition first made landfall in the Philippines — because of course the country's softest band named its biggest record after a 500-year-old footnote.

Nine people, zero irony, and the biggest choruses in the country. Manila's feelings have a house band.

Start here

Limasawa Street (2021) — the full-band, full-heart statement. Then go backward to the early EPs for the busking-days intimacy.
