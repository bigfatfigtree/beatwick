ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · LAGOS

WIZKID

Starboy turned a Surulere church choir into the smoothest voice in global pop — and named his biggest song after a Lagos intersection.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · FEBRUARY 5, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Before the Grammy nods and the Drake link-ups, Ayodeji Balogun was a kid from Surulere singing in church at eleven. Wizkid's genius was never volume — it was glide. While the rest of Afrobeats shouted for the world's attention, he slid underneath it with a voice so smooth it made global pop come to him.

The receipts: “Ojuelegba” (2014), named for a Lagos neighborhood, became a global anthem with remix co-signs from Drake and Skepta. “One Dance” (2016) made him the first Nigerian artist to top the US Hot 100. Made in Lagos (2020) and “Essence” with Tems earned Grammy nominations and turned the summer of 2021 into a victory lap. He barely raises his voice, and the whole industry leans in to hear it.

WHY LAGOS CLAIMS HIM

Born in Surulere, raised in the city's noise, and forever tethered to it. The Ojuelegba intersection is a real place — a chaotic bus junction millions of Lagosians cross daily — and Wizkid turned it into a love letter to everyone stuck in it. Starboy can live anywhere; the songs always point home.

THE KEY TRACK

“Ojuelegba” (2014). The song that proved a track named after a traffic-choked Lagos junction could travel further than most artists ever will. Understated, unhurried, undeniable.

THE ESSENCE ERA

“Essence” with Tems didn't just dominate the summer of 2021 — it became the highest-charting Nigerian song in Billboard Hot 100 history, peaking at number nine, and earned a Grammy nomination. It was the moment the industry's math changed: Afrobeats didn't need a Western feature to cross over. It just needed Wizkid and Tems doing what they do.

THE STARBOY EFFECT

Starboy Entertainment, his label, became the model for the new Lagos: artist-owned, globally distributed, allergic to the old gatekeepers. Morayo (2024) proved the machine still runs. Wizkid barely tweets, rarely explains himself, and remains the most influential stylist in Afrobeats — every quiet-luxury aesthetic in Lagos right now is downstream of him.

START HERE

“Ojuelegba,” then “Essence” for the Tems-era peak, then Made in Lagos front to back. Smoothest discography in the game.

Lagos is the loudest city on earth. Wizkid is the quietest voice that ever conquered it.
