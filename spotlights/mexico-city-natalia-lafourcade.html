ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MEXICO CITY

Natalia Lafourcade

Mexico City's folk-song surgeon — intimate, precise, and quietly unstoppable.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · FEBRUARY 4, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Natalia Lafourcade was born in Mexico City in 1984, raised in Coatepec, Veracruz, in a house full of musicians — and she has spent her career making the case that quiet is a superpower. She debuted in 2002 as a pop-rock teenager, then walked away from the obvious path and rebuilt herself as the finest songwriter of her generation, one spare, devastating song at a time.

The turn came with Mujer Divina (2012), her tribute to Agustín Lara, which announced her as the heir to Mexico's great songbook tradition. Then Hasta la Raíz (2015) made the case undeniable: the title track won Record of the Year, Song of the Year, and Best Alternative Song at the Latin Grammys, and Lafourcade took home four trophies that night — the biggest winner of the ceremony.

Her Mexico City runs deep. The "Hasta la Raíz" video was shot at the storied Estudios Churubusco with three hundred fans; the song itself was built in a CDMX studio session around a huapango riff from her native Veracruz, produced by Cachorro López. She is the living link in the capital's songwriter lineage — from Lara and Chavela to right now — and she headlines the Auditorio Nacional like she was born to it.

She's a serial collaborator across the Latin world, and her stripped-back sessions are as essential as the studio records. Then came De Todas las Flores (2022) — even sparer, voice and guitar and silence — and it still landed like an event. Some artists need production. Lafourcade needs a room.

The key track

"Hasta la Raíz." A song about love as rootedness, built on that stuttering huapango guitar and a melody that feels ancient on first listen. It's intimate and enormous at once — the rare ballad that won the industry's biggest prizes without raising its voice.

Start here

"Hasta la Raíz" first, then "Tú Sí Sabes Quererme" (2017) for the slow-burn follow-up, and "Nunca Es Suficiente" when you're ready to feel things. Then play the whole Hasta la Raíz album in order — it's sequenced like a novel.

In a city of twenty million shouting, she became its biggest star by whispering. Do the math on that confidence.
