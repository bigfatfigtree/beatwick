ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MONTREAL

Céline Dion: Quebec's voice of the century

From Charlemagne to the world stage — the francophone powerhouse who became the best-selling artist Canada ever produced.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JULY 2, 2026 · 4 MIN READ

Céline Marie Claudette Dion was the youngest of fourteen children in Charlemagne, Quebec, a small town across the river from Montreal. She was twelve when her demo tape reached manager René Angélil — who mortgaged his house to fund her first record. She was twenty when she won Eurovision for Switzerland in 1988. By the '90s, she was the biggest voice on the planet.

The numbers are absurd: over 200 million records sold, making her one of the best-selling artists in history and the best-selling Canadian artist ever. "My Heart Will Go On" — the Titanic theme — became one of the best-selling singles of all time and won the Oscar and multiple Grammys. Her Las Vegas residencies redefined what a residency could be: not a retirement, but a coronation.

Why Montreal claims her

Dion is Quebec's cultural monument. She started as a francophone star — her early French-language albums are sacred texts in Quebec — and conquered the English-speaking world without ever abandoning her roots. The accent never left. The French albums never stopped. Montreal treats her the way other cities treat founding myths: with a reverence that's almost religious, and a protectiveness that's entirely earned.

Her recent health battles with stiff-person syndrome, and her triumphant return performance at the Paris Olympics opening ceremony in 2024, only deepened the bond. Quebec doesn't just love Céline — it identifies with her.

Every city has a voice it would recognize blindfolded. Montreal's can shatter glass and heal hearts in the same breath.

She also invented the modern Vegas residency. "A New Day..." ran for five years and over 700 shows, turning the Colosseum at Caesars Palace into the template every pop star has copied since. When health issues forced her off stage, Quebec waited — and her 2024 return at the Paris Olympics opening ceremony, singing from the Eiffel Tower, was the comeback of the decade. The voice endures. It always does.

Key track

"My Heart Will Go On" — the Titanic theme, the wedding song, the karaoke Everest. You know every word. Everyone does. That's the point.

Start here

Falling Into You (1996) for the imperial phase, then D'eux (1995) — the best-selling French-language album ever — for the Quebec roots. Sing along; resistance is futile.

Charlemagne is a small town with one enormous voice. The world just borrows it.
