ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · KINGSTON

IOTO SH

The three-time Grammy-nominated producer behind Koffee's West Indies and Jah9's Note to Self stepped out from behind the boards — Kingston's Iotosh is an artist now.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 7, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Righteous” — the 2025 Sound Ting single — official audio.

Iotosh Poyser spent years building everyone else's sound. The Kingston native — Wolmer's Boys' School, then Fruity Loops on a keyboard — became one of Jamaican music's most in-demand producers: three tracks on Jah9's Note to Self, Koffee's hit "West Indies," work with Protoje, Chronixx, Lila Iké, and Jesse Royal, three Grammy nominations to his name. Now he's releasing his own music through his own label, Sound Ting, and the shift from behind the boards to front and center is complete.

It's the classic producer's dilemma, and Iotosh is answering it the right way — not by trading on his credits, but by building a catalogue. His singles have been coming steadily, and his live sets, from Trench Town Culture Yard to BBC Radio 1Xtra's Jamaica showcase, show an artist who understands both sides of the stage.

WHY KINGSTON CLAIMS HIM

Because he's the connecting tissue of the city's new era. Iotosh produced Protoje's "30 Million," executive-produced Kyraxx's EP, and co-founded Sound Ting — he's been inside the machinery of Kingston's reggae revival since before it had a name. Now that he's stepping out as a vocalist, he's carrying a decade of studio knowledge into every verse.

THE KEY TRACK

"Righteous" (2025). A Sound Ting single that plays like a producer showing his own work — the drums land exactly where a decade of Grammy-nominated engineering says they should, and the vocal has the confidence of someone who finally stopped making other people's records and made his own.

THE PRODUCER'S EDGE

Artists who come from production have a secret weapon: they hear the whole record at once. Iotosh's releases sound self-directed in a way that debut artists rarely manage — every element placed with intention, nothing decorative. That doesn't always translate to star power, but in his case it translates to something better: music that sounds like it knows exactly what it's doing.

The Jamaica Observer caught him at the pivot — a producer openly talking about getting personal with his own releases while juggling production work for everyone else. That's the tension that makes Iotosh interesting: he's not abandoning the boards, he's expanding them. Executive-producing Kyraxx's EP, running Sound Ting's juggling rhythms, and still finding time for his own singles. The board knowledge feeds the artistry, and the artistry is now the headline act.

START HERE

"Righteous," then his other Sound Ting singles, then go backwards through his production catalogue — Koffee's "West Indies," Jah9's Note to Self — to hear how the board shaped the artist.

Iotosh spent years making Kingston's biggest records. Now he's making his own.
