ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · ACCRA

STONEBWOY

1GAD — the dancehall king who made Ashaiman the loudest postcode in African music.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · SEPTEMBER 10, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Livingstone Etse Satekla is Ghana's reggae and dancehall ambassador, and he carries the title like a uniform. From Ashaiman — the Tema township that raised him — Stonebwoy built BHiM Nation into a movement and carried Ghanaian dancehall to stages the genre had never seen.

The catalog is bulletproof. “Go Higher” (2016) is the reggae anthem of a generation. “Ololo” (2019) with Teni bridged Accra and Lagos effortlessly. “Nominate” (2020) with Keri Hilson put him on Anloga Junction, the album that cemented his global standing. And every December, his BHiM concerts are among the loudest nights in Accra — the dancehall congregation in full voice.

WHY ACCRA CLAIMS HIM

Ashaiman boy, Accra king. The BHiM Nation headquarters is here, the December shows are here, the street that raised him is here. Stonebwoy never had to leave to prove the point — he made the world come to the 233.

THE KEY TRACK

“Ololo” (2019) ft. Teni. The Accra-Lagos link-up at its smoothest — two of West Africa's best voices trading verses like old friends. Which, by all accounts, they are.

THE RIVALRY

No Stonebwoy story is complete without the other half of Ghana's great dancehall cold war: Shatta Wale. Their rivalry — real, theatrical, occasionally genuinely heated — has defined an era of Ghanaian music, from chart battles to the infamous 2019 awards-show onstage confrontation. Love it or hate it, the tension made both men bigger.

THE MENTOR

Before the throne, there was the apprenticeship: Stonebwoy came up under Samini, Ghana's dancehall godfather, and the lineage shows — the discipline, the live-band rigor, the refusal to treat dancehall as a costume. He inherited the crown properly, then took it global.

THE STAGE

See him live before anything else. Stonebwoy’s shows — full band, full voice, full BHiM regalia — are the reason the dancehall faithful treat his December concerts like pilgrimage. The records are the invitation; the stage is the proof.

START HERE

“Ololo,” then “Go Higher” for the anthem, then “Nominate” for the global era, then Anloga Junction end to end.

Dancehall was born in Kingston. Stonebwoy made sure Accra gets a verse in its history.
