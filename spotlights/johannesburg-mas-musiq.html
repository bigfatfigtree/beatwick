ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · JOHANNESBURG

MAS MUSIQ

The Tembisa producer whose smooth chords and deep basslines gave amapiano its soul — a chart-topping hitmaker who still operates like the underground made him.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JUNE 24, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Uzozisola” — the double-platinum Auti 'eSharp centerpiece — the official video.

Thabo Ngubane grew up in Tembisa dreaming of making music and went and studied it properly — then built a sound that feels like the dream itself. Brought into New Money Gang Records under DJ Maphorisa's wing, Mas Musiq dropped his debut Mambisa in 2019 and won Best Amapiano Album at the South African Music Awards. He followed it with Mambisa II (2020) and the SAMA-nominated Shonamalanga EP with Aymos — three projects deep before most producers have figured out their kick drums.

The breakthrough record was Auti 'eSharp (2021): "Sengizwile" hit number one on the Official South African Charts, "Uzozisola" went double platinum, and "Inhliziyo" peaked at 17 — a triple hit that made him one of the go-to architects of the amapiano wave. Nini na Nini (2023) went Gold, and Lane Yam (2024) kept the run going. His signature is unmistakable: smooth, almost jazzy chords laid over deep, warm basslines, emotionally rich in a genre that often treats feeling as optional.

WHY JOBURG CLAIMS HIM

Tembisa again — the East Rand keeps producing the genre's most musical minds. Mas Musiq is a producer's producer in a city that treats its DJs like royalty: the Maphorisa co-sign put him in the room, but the chords are what kept him there. When Joburg's biggest names need a track with a spine, his number gets called.

THE KEY TRACK

"Uzozisola" (2021). The double-platinum centerpiece of Auti 'eSharp — all glide and ache, the kind of amapiano that works as well at 2am on a quiet drive as it does packed shoulder-to-shoulder. It proved the soulful lane wasn't a side quest; it was the main road.

THE SOUND

Listen for the chords first. Where his peers build around log-drum pressure, Mas Musiq builds around harmony — keys that feel studied, basslines that carry weight, arrangements with actual dynamics. It's amapiano as composition, not just club pressure, and it's why his records age better than most.

START HERE

Auti 'eSharp front to back, then the Shonamalanga EP with Aymos to hear the chemistry, then Mambisa for where it all started.

Mas Musiq made the dancefloor feel something. Joburg hasn't stopped dancing since.
