ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · STOCKHOLM

Avicii

The Stockholm producer who made EDM cry, sing, and feel something — gone at 28, still everywhere.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 17, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Tim Bergling was born in Stockholm in 1989 and started uploading tracks as Avicii as a teenager — the name came from the Buddhist term for the lowest level of hell, picked because the plain spelling was taken on MySpace. "Levels" (2011), built on Etta James's "Something's Got a Hold on Me," made him the face of EDM's imperial phase: the melody every festival on earth played at midnight.

What separated him from the drop merchants was restlessness. True (2013) was a genuine swerve — "Wake Me Up," with Aloe Blacc singing over acoustic guitar and a dance beat, fused country, folk, and EDM in a way that shouldn't have worked and absolutely did. It became one of the best-selling singles of the decade. Stories (2015) pushed further into songwriting, collaborating across genres while his peers were still building bigger drops.

Stockholm was his base and his lineage: another product of the city's pop-production pipeline, the Cheiron-to-Max-Martin assembly line that treats songwriting as a craft. But the machine chewed him up. Years of relentless touring, health problems, and pressure led him to retire from live performance in 2016 at 26. He died on April 20, 2018, in Muscat, Oman. He was 28.

The aftermath reshaped the conversation around the industry: the Tim Bergling Foundation, established by his family, works on mental health and suicide prevention. Stockholm renamed its most famous arena the Avicii Arena in 2021. His posthumous album Tim (2019) was completed from his demos, with proceeds going to the foundation. The tributes weren't just grief — they were a reckoning with what the EDM boom had cost.

The key track

"Levels" (2011). The Etta James vocal, the synth line like a sunrise, the drop that defined an era. It's the most consequential dance record of the 2010s — the moment EDM went from clubs to the center of pop, and it still sounds enormous.

Start here

True (2013) — the album where he proved he was a songwriter, not just a producer. "Wake Me Up," "Hey Brother," "Addicted to You": the sound of a kid from Stockholm trying to make dance music feel like something, and succeeding.

The take: Avicii gave EDM its biggest melodies and its hardest lesson. Stockholm produces pop like an industry; he reminded the industry there are people inside the machine. The arena carries his name now. It should.
