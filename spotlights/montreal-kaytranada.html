ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MONTREAL

KAYTRANADA: Montreal's groove scientist

From Haitian roots to Montreal basements to a Grammy — the producer who taught the world to dance at 100 BPM.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 5, 2026 · 4 MIN READ

Louis Celestin was a teenager uploading beats as Kaytradamus when the internet started paying attention. Born in Haiti and raised in Saint-Hubert, just outside Montreal, he built a sound nobody had a name for: house music slowed to a hip-hop swagger, R&B flipped into club fuel, drums that bounce like they learned French and Creole at the same time.

The breakout was 99.9% (2016) — a debut album with Anderson .Paak, Vic Mensa, and AlunaGeorge that felt like a block party the whole industry crashed. Then came the Grammy for Best Dance/Electronic Album in 2021, making him the first Black artist to win the category. The kid from the South Shore had out-produced the entire EDM industrial complex from his laptop.

Why Montreal claims him

KAYTRANADA is Montreal's diaspora story in musical form: Haitian heritage, Quebec upbringing, and a sound that belongs to every dancefloor on earth. The city's bilingual, multicultural chaos is baked into his drums. He came up through Montreal's beat scene and its legendary DJ culture — this is the city that hosts one of the world's great jazz festivals and an all-night electronic underground — and he carries both traditions in every track.

Local lore: his Boiler Room-era sets and early Montreal club nights are the origin story a generation of Quebec producers cites as permission to be weird.

House music had a speed limit until KAYTRANADA slowed it down, swung it sideways, and made the whole world move at Montreal time.

The collaborator list tells the rest of the story. His remix of Janet Jackson's "If" is a modern classic, the KAYTRAMINÉ project with Aminé (2023) proved he could carry a whole joint album, and his production touch shows up on records by the biggest names in the game — all while the drums stay unmistakably his. Montreal's groove scientist turned out to be pop's secret weapon.

Key track

"Glowed Up" featuring Anderson .Paak — the 99.9% standout, all rubber-band bass and strut. If a song could wink, this one does.

Start here

99.9% front to back, then Bubba (2019) for the late-night refinement. Play it loud enough that the neighbors learn something.

Montreal makes room for everybody's rhythm. KAYTRANADA just made everybody's rhythm make room for Montreal.
