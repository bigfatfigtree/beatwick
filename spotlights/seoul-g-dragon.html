ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · SEOUL

G-Dragon

Seoul's fashion-alien pop auteur — half the reason K-pop looks the way it does.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JUNE 24, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Kwon Ji-yong was born in Seoul in 1988 and was performing before he was a teenager. As G-Dragon — leader, chief songwriter, and resident chaos agent of BIGBANG — he spent the late 2000s and early 2010s dragging K-pop somewhere it hadn't planned to go: artier, weirder, and dressed like nobody else on the planet.

His solo work is where the auteur lives. Coup d'Etat (2013) with "Crooked" is still the high-water mark for K-pop as personal expression — punk energy, hip-hop bones, and pop hooks, all filtered through a sensibility that treats fashion week and the recording studio as the same room.

His hiatuses are events in themselves — Seoul's fashion and music press track his rare appearances like eclipses. Absence, it turns out, is also a kind of stagecraft.

Seoul roots

G-Dragon is Seoul's id in designer clothing. He trained at YG Entertainment from age twelve, grew up inside the system, and then spent his career vandalizing it from the inside — the bleached hair, the gender-fluid styling, the gallery exhibitions. The city's fashion kids treat his old outfits the way art students treat museum retrospectives.

His influence on the city's visual culture is hard to overstate: the Hongdae boutiques, the Seoul Fashion Week front rows, the entire generation of idols who dress like art projects. BIGBANG's tours turned the Olympic Park arenas into his personal runway. Seoul makes a lot of pop stars; it only made one G-Dragon, because one is all the city could handle.

One key track

"Crooked" (2013). Pop-punk propulsion, a chorus that sounds like a tantrum, and a music video full of glorious bad decisions. It's the sound of the biggest star in Korea refusing to act like it.

The 2017 M.O.T.T.E world tour took the spectacle global — domes across Asia, arenas in North America and Oceania — and cemented the template: the idol as auteur, the concert as art installation, the merch line as fashion week.

K-pop has idols. G-Dragon is what happens when the idol starts directing the movie.

Start here

Coup d'Etat (2013) — features Missy Elliott and Diplo, because of course it does. The rare K-pop solo album that plays like an art statement and a party at the same time.
