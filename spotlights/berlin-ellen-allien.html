ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BERLIN

Ellen Allien

BPitch Control's founder wrote the love letter Berlin's techno scene deserved — then spent 25 years running its best label.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · NOVEMBER 18, 2025 · 2 MIN READ

Ellen Allien — born Ellen Fraatz in West Berlin — found acid house in London in the late eighties, came home to a reunified city, and started DJing in the ruins: Tresor, the Bunker, E-Werk. She ran a show called Braincandy on Berlin's Kiss FM, started a label of the same name, watched it die on distribution problems — and in 1999 built a better one. BPitch Control became the most important electronic label of 2000s Berlin, and its first signing was its founder.

Her debut album Stadtkind (2001) — "city child" — was dedicated to Berlin outright, eleven tracks of warm, melodic, electro-infused techno that treated the city as a love interest. Where the Tresor school went hard and grey, Allien went emotional: vocals, guitar, pop melodies underpinned with serious beats. Berlinette (2003), Thrills (2005), Sool (2008), Dust (2010) — each one a different weather system over the same city.

But the label is the real monument. BPitch Control launched Modeselektor, Apparat, and Paul Kalkbrenner — three acts that would define Berlin electronic music for the next two decades. Running a label that discovers your city's future while you're still headlining its present is a rare trick; Allien has done it for a quarter century. In 2019 she launched the "We Are Not Alone" rave series, first at Griessmuehle and later at RSO.Berlin — still throwing parties, still reading the room.

She is also, quietly, one of the great bridges in electronic music: the link between the squat-party nineties and the festival-headliner era, between the underground's purism and pop's pull. Her collaboration with Apparat as Orchestra of Bubbles (2006) remains one of the era's defining records — two Berlin institutions meeting halfway between the club and the concert hall.

The key track

"Erdbeermund" (2002). The single that distilled the Allien formula — sweet and strange, a lullaby with a kick drum, her whispered German vocals floating over a beat that won't sit still. It sounds like Berlin at 6 a.m.: tender, wired, and unwilling to go home.

Start here

Stadtkind (2001) — the love letter, and still the purest statement of what she does. Then Orchestra of Bubbles with Apparat for the widescreen version, and the BPitch compilations if you want to hear the label's whole universe in one sitting.

The take: Berlin techno has cathedrals and congregations, but Ellen Allien built the printing press. The DJ, the label boss, the city kid — she's the reason the Berlin sound had somewhere to live.
