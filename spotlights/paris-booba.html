ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · PARIS

Booba

French rap's undisputed heavyweight — three decades deep and still the name every new MC has to answer to.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JUNE 11, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Élie Yaffa was born in 1976 in Boulogne-Billancourt, in the Hauts-de-Seine west of Paris — the 92 he would turn into a brand. He started in the nineties as one half of Lunatic, with Ali: their album Mauvais Œil (2000) was a landmark of hardcore French rap, all menace and precision. When the duo split, Booba went solo with Temps Mort (2002) and never looked back. Three decades later, he's still the measuring stick.

The catalogue is a history of French rap itself: Panthéon (2004), Ouest Side (2006) — the classic, the one that made him untouchable — Lunatic (2010), and a run of number-one albums that never really stopped. His flow is the constant: a cold, technical delivery that treats every beat like an interrogation. The 92i collective became an empire — label, clothing, the whole infrastructure of independence.

And then there are the feuds, which in Booba's case are practically a second discography. Rohff, Kaaris — the rivalry that escalated to a brawl in Orly Airport's duty-free in 2018, both men jailed — La Fouine, and half the French rap scene at one point or another. He wields social media like a weapon and treats beef as brand strategy. It's exhausting and it's brilliant and it's never boring, which is the point.

Paris's west side runs through all of it. Boulogne isn't the banlieue of the postcards — it's the 92, and Booba made the department code a status symbol. Every French MC since has had to position themselves relative to him: with him, against him, or in his shadow. Most end up in the shadow.

The key track

"Validée" — the swagger distilled, Booba at his most untouchable over a beat that sounds like money. It's the track that sums up the whole persona: validated, certified, and not asking your opinion.

Start here

Ouest Side (2006) — the classic, the peak, the one every French rap fan can quote. Then Mauvais Œil with Lunatic for the raw origins, and "92i Veyron" for the imperial-phase victory lap.

The take: Booba is French rap's gravity — everything orbits him, whether it wants to or not. Three decades, ten number ones, and a feud list longer than most careers. The 92's heavyweight champion, undisputed.
