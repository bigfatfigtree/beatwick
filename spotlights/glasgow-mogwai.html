ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · GLASGOW

Mogwai

Glasgow's loudest quiet band — five post-rock architects who built their own label and never looked back.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 6, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Mogwai formed in Glasgow in 1995 — Stuart Braithwaite, Dominic Aitchison, and Martin Bulloch, later joined by Barry Burns — and named themselves after the creatures from Gremlins, which tells you everything about their sense of humor and nothing about their music. The music is instrumental, monumental, and built on the quiet-loud-quiet architecture they helped invent: whisper for six minutes, detonate for six more.

Mogwai Young Team (1997) is the founding document of British post-rock — and its centerpiece, "Mogwai Fear Satan," is sixteen minutes of slow-burn guitar that remains the genre's high-water mark. They recorded it young, loud, and mostly live, and it still sounds like weather. The follow-ups (Come On Die Young, Rock Action) refined the formula without softening it: this is music that treats volume as an emotional register.

Glasgow isn't just their hometown — it's their business model. In 1996 they founded Rock Action Records, run from the city ever since, releasing their own records and a roster of fellow travelers. While their peers chased London deals, Mogwai built the infrastructure at home: own label, own terms, own city. It's the most Glasgow move imaginable, and it worked. As the Love Continues (2021) went to number one in the UK — twenty-six years in, on their own label, with no singles strategy whatsoever.

Their other life is the screen: the score for Douglas Gordon's Zidane: A 21st Century Portrait, the Les Revenants soundtrack that redefined what TV music could do, and a sideline in film work that treats their instrumentals as the narratives they are. No singer has ever been missed less.

The key track

"Mogwai Fear Satan" (1997). Sixteen minutes, one idea executed perfectly: a flute melody that builds and builds until the guitars arrive like a natural disaster. Play it loud enough and your furniture will have opinions.

Start here

Mogwai Young Team (1997) — the blueprint, raw and towering. Then The Hawk Is Howling (2008) for the polished version, and As the Love Continues (2021) for proof the fire never went out.

The take: Mogwai are Glasgow's argument for doing it yourself: no singer, no hits, no compromise — just volume, patience, and their own label. Twenty-six years later they got a number one album anyway. The long game works.
