ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BUENOS AIRES

Bizarrap

The producer in the cap who turned a bedroom studio into Argentina's biggest export machine.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 5, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Gonzalo Julián Conde was born in 1998 in Ramos Mejía, in Greater Buenos Aires. He started on YouTube making "combos locos" — franken-remixes welding Argentine trap acapellas to EDM drops — and then he invented a format so simple it felt inevitable: one producer, one artist, one camera, one take. The BZRP Music Sessions became the most important A&R machine in Latin music.

The numbers stopped making sense. The session with Spanish rapper Quevedo — Vol. 52, July 2022 — went to number one on the Billboard Global 200, made Bizarrap the first Argentine artist to top Spotify's global chart, and has passed a billion streams. The Vol. 53 session with Shakira, in January 2023, broke streaming records on arrival. He closed out the era with three sold-out nights at Buenos Aires' Hipódromo Argentino de Palermo, sixty thousand fans deep.

What matters isn't the stats — it's that he did it from Buenos Aires, without moving to Miami or diluting a thing. The sessions are shot in his studio, in his city, and the world learned to come to him. Argentine urban music went from a local scene to a global industry, and the kid in the cap is the reason.

An invitation to the Sessions has become a rite of passage — the Latin industry's equivalent of a knighthood, from Residente to Shakira to whoever's next. The format's genius is its transparency: no label machinery visible, just a producer and an artist building a hit in real time. Every A&R executive on earth has watched those videos taking notes.

The key track

"Bzrp Music Sessions, Vol. 52" with Quevedo. The one that made him the biggest producer on the planet for a summer — Quevedo's Canarian lilt floating over Biza's patient, menacing beat. It taught the entire world Spanish phonetics, one "quédate" at a time.

Start here

Vol. 52 first, then Vol. 53 for the pop-culture earthquake, then Vol. 49 with Residente for the lyrical masterclass. Watch them on video, too — the format is half the art.

He built a global hit factory out of a webcam and a beat. The major labels are still taking notes.
