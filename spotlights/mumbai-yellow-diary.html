ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MUMBAI

THE YELLOW DIARY

Five Mumbai musicians who built an indie empire out of one song — and made a debut video about a child suicide bomber the country couldn't look away from.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · APRIL 28, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Marz” — the official Sony Music video.

Most indie bands have a slow-burn origin story. The Yellow Diary's is a detonation. In 2015, vocalist Rajan Batra — an ex-navy cadet — wrote "Marz" with freelance music producer Himonshu Parikh and IT consultant-turned-guitarist Vaibhav Pani. They put out a rough version independently. The song caught the ears of Raghu Dixit, Vishal Dadlani, Papon, Warren Mendonsa, and Ranjit Barot — basically the entire conscience of Indian indie — and Sony Music signed the band to an exclusive deal in 2017. Not many debut singles do that. Almost none.

The 2018 Marz EP — three tracks: "Marz," "Afzai," "Kashmir" — arrived with one of the most arresting debut music videos Indian indie has ever produced. Directed by Maanavi Bedi, it follows a child suicide bomber at a fair, a concept so bold the band admitted they never imagined the final video would go as far as it did. The song itself is about the dilemma between the mind and the heart, and the video's pulsating climax turned a philosophical alt-rock track into a national conversation piece.

The second EP, Izafa, included "Dheere Se," "Buniyaad," and a reworking of Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan's "Tera Jeya Hor Disda." Then came the songs that made them a festival fixture: "Kashmir," a metaphor for a ruined paradise, and later "Roz Roz" with Shilpa Rao and "Rab Raakha," blending rock, electronics, and Indian classical into something that belongs to no genre and fills every room.

WHY MUMBAI CLAIMS THEM

Because The Yellow Diary is the Mumbai indie success story told the right way — five musicians from wildly different backgrounds (navy cadet, IT consultant, gigging indie veterans Stuart DaCosta and Sahil Shah) who met through one song and turned it into a national footprint without losing the poetry. They are the band the scene's elders co-signed, and the kids stayed for.

THE KEY TRACK

"Marz" (2018). The song that started everything — heavy, intense, and impossible to hear once. Watch the video; it's the rare debut that treats its audience like adults from the first frame.

THE SOUND

Alternative rock with an electronic pulse and Indian classical instincts: soaring Hindi-Urdu vocals from Batra, Parikh's production gluing synths to guitars, and lyrics that wrestle with inner conflict and the state of the country in the same verse. Soulful, heavy, and never once boring.

START HERE

"Marz," then the whole debut EP, then "Roz Roz" for the Shilpa Rao collaboration and "Rab Raakha" for the Punjabi-language side of the band.

The Yellow Diary wrote their name in the margins of one song — and the whole country read the page.
