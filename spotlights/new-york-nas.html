ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · NEW YORK

Nas: Queensbridge's poet laureate

He turned a housing project into literature. Thirty years after Illmatic, New York still rhymes in his sentences.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 15, 2025 · 4 MIN READ

Before Nas, rap about the projects was reportage. After him, it was literature. Nasir Jones grew up in the Queensbridge Houses — the largest public housing development in North America, staring across the East River at the Manhattan skyline he would spend his career rewriting in verse.

At nineteen he was already ghostwriting street-corner mythologies in his head, and at twenty he released Illmatic (1994), ten tracks in under forty minutes that reset the bar for what a rap album could be. Five different legendary producers. Zero skips. The Source gave it five mics, and New York has been arguing about the second-best rap album ever made ever since.

Why New York claims him

Queensbridge isn't a backdrop in Nas's catalog — it's the main character. The 40th Side, the Ravenswood line, the corner stores and the project stairwells: he mapped his neighborhood with the specificity of a city planner and the ear of a jazz musician. His father, Olu Dara, was a jazz cornetist, and you can hear it in the way Nas lets a bar breathe. The city repaid him with immortality: a street in Queensbridge was renamed Nas Way, and his face stares out of murals across all five boroughs.

The beef with Jay-Z in 2001 — "Ether" versus "Takeover" — remains the heaviest title fight in rap history, fought entirely on wax, and it ended with both men richer for it. That is the most New York sentence ever written.

Every generation of New York rappers gets measured against a kid from Queensbridge who peaked at twenty and never stopped raising the floor.

The late-career run silenced any "what have you done lately" talk. Four King's Disease albums with producer Hit-Boy in the 2020s — the first winning the Grammy for Best Rap Album — plus his Mass Appeal label signing the next wave, proved the pen never dulled. Nas didn't just survive three decades of rap; he kept raising the floor he built.

Key track

"N.Y. State of Mind" — the Illmatic opener, DJ Premier's piano loop chopped over drums that sound like a subway car, and the most quoted sixteen bars about the city ever committed to tape. "I never sleep, 'cause sleep is the cousin of death" is Queensbridge's motto now.

Start here

Illmatic, front to back, no skipping — it's 39 minutes, shorter than a crosstown bus ride. Then jump to King's Disease (2020), the late-career Grammy winner that proved the pen never dulled. Listen on the 7 train at golden hour for full effect.

New York made Nas, and Nas returned the favor by making Queensbridge the most famous housing project on earth.
