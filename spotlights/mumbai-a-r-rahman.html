ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MUMBAI

A.R. Rahman

Chennai-born, Mumbai-made — the composer who dragged Bollywood's sound into the future, then scored Hollywood.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · DECEMBER 10, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

A.R. Rahman was born A.S. Dileep Kumar in Chennai in 1967, but his legend was manufactured in Mumbai. When Mani Ratnam handed the 25-year-old his film debut with Roja in 1992, the Bollywood machine was running on formula; Rahman showed up with synthesizers, Sufi poetry, and qawwali structures nobody had dared put in a Hindi film, and the formula never recovered.

By the time Slumdog Millionaire won him two Academy Awards in 2009 — Best Original Score and Best Original Song for "Jai Ho" — he had already re-scored an entire industry. The "Mozart of Madras" nickname stuck, but his real title is simpler: the man who made Mumbai's film music sound like the future.

He works famously fast and famously alone, sketching melodies at hours when Mumbai's studios are dark. The city's film industry runs on deadlines that would kill most composers; Rahman treats them as a metronome.

Mumbai roots

Mumbai is where Rahman's experiments became an industry. The city's studios — Mehboob, Filmistan, Film City in Goregaon — are where his hybrids were stress-tested against Bollywood's impossible demands: a song has to work in a rickshaw, a wedding, and a stadium. His genius was never just the fusion of Carnatic melody with electronica; it was making that fusion survive Mumbai's brutal commercial logic without losing its strangeness.

The city's composers still work in the crater he left. Every Bollywood score that dares an odd time signature or a Tamil folk sample is paying rent to Rahman — and Mumbai, the city that turned his weirdness into box office, takes its cut in pride.

One key track

"Jai Ho" (2008). Written for Slumdog Millionaire, it became a global victory anthem — and back home, a wedding-and-cricket staple. Underneath the bombast is classic Rahman: a devotional chant rebuilt as pop architecture.

Bollywood had a sound. Then Rahman arrived, and Bollywood had a before and an after.
