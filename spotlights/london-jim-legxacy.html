ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · LONDON

JIM LEGXACY

Catford's one-man genre blender wrote the decade's biggest UK rap hit, then signed to XL and made the weirdest, best record of 2025 anyway.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · DECEMBER 10, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

James Olaloye spent years underground before the mainstream found him — which is how you end up co-writing and co-producing “Sprinter,” the Dave and Central Cee smash that became the longest-running UK rap number one ever, without anyone knowing your name. The Catford kid wanted to be a graphic designer. Instead he became the most interesting rapper, singer and producer in London, blending rap, lo-fi, emo, afrobeats and indie-rock samples into something critics reach for new words to describe.

The pipeline: three self-released EPs (Dynasty Program, BTO!, Citadel) built the cult, then 2023's homeless n***a pop music mixtape and the heart-shattering single “nothings changed (!)” — written for his late sister — made the cult undeniable. He showed up twice on Fred Again's Ten Days, played his first ever live show at The Albany in Deptford, then signed to XL Recordings in 2024. His first label project, the mixtape black british music (2025), flipped a Chipmunk “Oopsy Daisy” sample into “aggressive” and took five stars from NME.

WHY LONDON CLAIMS HIM

Jim Legxacy is Lewisham in sonic form: drill-influenced rap bleeding into shoegazey indie and afrobeats rhythms, lyrics that name the bus stops and the estates. He came up entirely through the borough's DIY ecosystem — self-recorded, self-released, art-school eye — and the whole south-of-the-river underground now points at him as proof the majors still sign weirdness.

THE KEY TRACK

“aggressive” (2025). The lead single from black british music (2025) — Chipmunk's 2009 chart-topper “Oopsy Daisy” chopped into a paranoid, twitching rap banger. It's the sound of a pop-culture magpie turning nostalgia into menace, and it sums up everything he does.

THE VOICE

He raps, sings and croons, sometimes in the same eight bars, with a delivery that can sound wounded one moment and unbothered the next. The production — which he mostly handles himself — is where the real signature lives: crackly samples, emo guitar, drill hats and afrobeats swing all in one track, like someone scrolling through their entire teenage listening history at once.

START HERE

black british music (2025) first — it's the complete statement. Then “nothings changed (!)” for the rawest three minutes of his catalogue, then back to BTO! to hear the underground origin.

Jim Legxacy spent five years being the scene's best-kept secret. The secret's out. The weirdness stayed.
