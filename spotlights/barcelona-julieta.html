ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BARCELONA

JULIETA

The Barcelona engineer-turned-pop auteur who self-produced her way from GarageBand to Primavera Sound — Catalan club romanticism at its finest.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · JULY 1, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

No official “Tu Juru Ju” video could be verified — start with the Juji EP.

Julieta Gracián Salas was studying telecommunications engineering at Pompeu Fabra when the pandemic hit, so she did what any future audio engineer would do: taught herself GarageBand and Logic, self-produced her own singles, and became one of the revelation voices of Spanish pop. Her first songs — “Tum valores,” “Tu juru ju,” collected on the debut Juji (2021) — arrived fully formed: electronic, urbano and reggaetón threads woven through Catalan-language songwriting, with an engineer's obsession with how sound actually works.

The city noticed fast. Barcelona Cultura called her one of the most destacado artists of the new Catalan scene, Primavera Sound and the Apolo and Razzmatazz rooms all booked her, and she took home an Enderrock award in 2023 alongside Mushkaa — the two faces of the new generation. Her follow-up Ni llum ni lluna expanded the palette into love and heartbreak across varied sonorities. She writes in Catalan, French, English and Spanish, plays guitar and piano, and her radio support runs from iCat to Europa FM. An engineer who investigates sound “with her own hands,” as she puts it — the songs are the lab reports.

WHY BARCELONA CLAIMS HER

Born in Barcelona, formed in Barcelona's bedrooms and booked across Barcelona's stages, Julieta is the other half of the Catalan urbano-pop explosion — the DIY counterweight to the scene's bigger names. Festival B, Share Festival, Strenes: every local showcase that matters has her on the bill, and she treats the Catalan language as a dancefloor tool, not a folk obligation.

THE KEY TRACK

“Tu Juru Ju” (2021). The second single that made her a reference point in a year — fresh, unforced, the sound of Catalan club romanticism inventing itself. Barcelona Cultura flagged it as the moment to pay attention. They were right.

THE VOICE

Sweet but never naive, with a producer's ear for where the voice should sit in the mix — of course, she mixes it herself. Her songs move from the daring dance end of the spectrum to intimate pop, and the multilingual writing keeps her out of any single lane. Barcelona's press calls her one of the greats of romanticism of club in Catalan. Hard to argue.

START HERE

Juji for the bedroom origins, Ni llum ni lluna for the growth, then the live sets — she's better on stage than on record, and the record is already good.

Julieta paused an engineering degree to make pop music. The pop music is so well-engineered it's basically a thesis.
