ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · KINGSTON

MORTI MER

Kingston's most honest new reggae voice took a decade to make his debut — then dropped From Within, with Damian Marley, Kabaka Pyramid, and Lila Iké on the tracklist.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · AUGUST 5, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Mortimer McPherson almost became a soldier, or an auto engineer. Kingston had other plans. The singer spent over a decade honing a voice that sits at the intersection of tradition and invention — raspy alto, silky falsetto, zero wasted emotion — and when his debut finally arrived, it landed like a statement. From Within, released September 20, 2024 via Overstand Entertainment and Easy Star Records, is 14 tracks of classic reggae folded into R&B, '80s pop, and even reggae disco.

The album opens with a statement of intent: "In My Time" featuring Damian "Jr. Gong" Marley. Then "Bruises," with Kabaka Pyramid and Lila Iké, on resilience and equality. The singles — "My Child," "Whole Heap," "Slowly" — kept coming for years before the record, each produced with Winta James, with Mortimer co-producing nearly half the album himself. Nothing about this was rushed. It shows.

WHY KINGSTON CLAIMS HIM

Because he's the reggae revival's most vulnerable voice. Mortimer writes openly about depression, trauma, political corruption, and the duality of daily life — topics Rasta Jamaican men are rarely heard addressing this directly. That honesty is why the elders co-signed him so fast: Protoje tours, Kabaka features, Marley collaborations. Kingston recognizes its own.

THE KEY TRACK

"Whole Heap." An introspective, acoustic-heavy record about carrying weight — it's the song that explains the man. For the anthem, "Lightning," with its 25-million-plus YouTube views, is the proof of scale.

THE SLOW BUILD

His earlier work set the stage: the Fight The Fight EP (2019) and a run of singles that built a global audience without an album. In an era of artists slinging content that disappears in weeks, Mortimer took his time and made something meant to last. The full-length debut took over a decade. It was worth every year.

And he hasn't stopped since the debut. 2025 brought new singles — "Flowers & Flames," "Round & Round" with Zion I Kings — continuing the pattern of steady, considered releases rather than content churn. Mortimer's touring life has him in front of international reggae audiences who already knew the singles by heart; the album just gave them the full story. The slow build is becoming a movement, one vulnerable song at a time.

START HERE

"Whole Heap," then "Lightning," then the full From Within album — and don't skip the reggae-disco detour, it's earned.

Mortimer didn't rush his moment. He built it, and it holds.
