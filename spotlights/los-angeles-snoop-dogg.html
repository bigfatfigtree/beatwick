ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · LOS ANGELES

Snoop Dogg: Long Beach's coolest export

Discovered by Dr. Dre, immortal by thirty, and still the smoothest voice in rap. Uncle Snoop is LA's permanent ambassador.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MAY 14, 2026 · 4 MIN READ

Calvin Broadus was a teenager from Long Beach when Dr. Dre heard his demo tape and put him on The Chronic (1992) — and rap has never sounded the same. That drawled, melodic, impossibly laid-back flow was a revelation: menace you could dance to, delivered like the words were too cool to hurry.

Doggystyle (1993) debuted at number one and made Snoop an instant icon — the tall, skinny kid from the LBC with the braids and the effortless charisma. Three decades later, he's done the thing almost no rapper manages: he became a beloved institution without becoming boring. Martha Stewart cooking shows, Olympics commentary, youth football leagues, a star on the Walk of Fame — Uncle Snoop contains multitudes, and all of them are smooth.

Why Los Angeles claims him

Snoop is the G-funk era's living monument. The Chronic and Doggystyle defined the West Coast's '90s sound — Parliament-Funkadelic synths, Long Beach bounce, Dre's immaculate production — and Snoop's voice is its signature instrument. He's Long Beach's favorite son in a region that treats its rap legends like civic treasures, and his longevity is the proof of concept: LA makes stars, but Snoop made himself permanent.

Local lore: the Snoop Youth Football League has been giving Long Beach kids a field and a coach for twenty years. The ambassador title isn't ceremonial.

Every era of LA rap has a new king. Snoop Dogg is the one they all have to pay respects to first.

The mogul era keeps expanding. He bought Death Row Records — the label that made him — in 2022, turning his own origin story into an asset. He stole the 2024 Paris Olympics as NBC's breakout commentator, a gig nobody predicted and everybody loved. At this point Uncle Snoop isn't just a rapper or a personality; he's a one-man institution, and Long Beach collects the royalties in pride.

Key track

"Gin and Juice" — the Doggystyle classic, all house-party ease over one of the funkiest loops ever committed to wax. "Rollin' down the street, smokin' indo" is Long Beach's unofficial motto.

Start here

Doggystyle for the coronation, then The Chronic to hear the introduction that started it all. Best experienced with the top down on the 710.

Long Beach made Snoop. Snoop made "the LBC" three letters the whole world knows.
