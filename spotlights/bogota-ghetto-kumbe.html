ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BOGOTÁ

GHETTO KUMBÉ

A masked trio from Colombia's coast turned Bogotá into their ritual ground — Afro-Caribbean drums folded into house, hip-hop, and bass on ZZK Records, with masks on and volume up.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MARCH 4, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

No official “Waré Warriors” video could be verified — start with the Soy Selva EP.

Ghetto Kumbé don't play concerts. They perform ceremonies. The Bogotá-based trio — three costeños from Santa Marta, Cartagena, and La Guajira — arrived in the capital with masks, tambores, and a conviction that the drum is a spiritual technology. Their self-titled 2020 debut on ZZK Records made the case; 2023's Clubbing Remixes, with reworks from four continents, proved the ritual travels.

The sound is Afro-Caribbean rhythm science updated for the club: traditional drumming and mysticism folded into modern hip-hop, house, and bass music — an Afro-futurist vision built from the rhythms they grew up with. The masks aren't a gimmick. As member El Guajiro has put it, what they do is "more than a concert, it is an experience" — a ritual ceremony, and the crowd is part of it.

WHY BOGOTÁ CLAIMS THEM

Because Bogotá is where three coastal drummers became one of Colombia's most prominent alternative acts. The capital's stages, collectives, and its appetite for the strange gave the trio a base, and they gave the city back a new mythology — one where the Guajira meets the dancefloor and neither has to explain itself.

THE KEY TRACK

"Waré Warriors," from the Soy Selva EP — a galloping dance rhythm built with Wayúu instruments from the Guajira Peninsula, carrying a message about Colombia's forgotten indigenous peoples. A song born of the rage around the 2019 social protests, proof that behind the festive spirit there's a country demanding change.

THE GLOBAL REACH

European tours, Festival Med in Portugal, a remix LP enlisting Trooko, Nickodemus, Uproot Andy, DJ Firmeza, and fellow ZZK artist Montoya — Ghetto Kumbé went from Bogotá's underground to an international ritual circuit without sanding down a single edge. Their Bandcamp is the portal; their live show is the conversion.

The discipline behind the ritual is the point. The trio's press bio calls it transportive Afro-futurism, but on the ground it's simpler: drums as the center of gravity, mysticism as the organizing principle, and a live show designed as a ceremony rather than a setlist. From the Soy Selva EP through the self-titled debut to the club-ready remixes, every release has moved further outward without losing the core. The mask stays on; the message stays clear.

START HERE

The 2020 self-titled debut front to back, then "Waré Warriors," then the Clubbing Remixes LP for the version built for the world's dancefloors.

Ghetto Kumbé don't follow Bogotá's scene. They summon their own.
