ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · LAGOS

BURNA BOY

The African Giant turned Lagos street wisdom into the world's default party language — and the city that raised him is still his loudest crowd.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 22, 2025 · 3 MIN READ

Burna Boy didn't climb out of Lagos. He weaponized it. Damini Ogulu took the Fela blueprint he inherited — his grandfather, Benson Idonije, was Fela Kuti's manager — and rebuilt it for the streaming era: afrobeats with a growl, dancehall with politics, stadium songs that still smell like Surulere traffic.

The arc is ruthless. In 2018, “Ye” turned pidgin philosophy about grinding into a continental anthem. African Giant (2019) earned a Grammy nomination. Twice as Tall (2020) won Best Global Music Album in 2021. “Last Last” (2022) put him on every festival main stage on earth. London claims him, the Grammys call him world music, and Lagos just calls him Damini.

WHY LAGOS CLAIMS HIM

He was born in Port Harcourt, but Lagos is where the lore lives: the early come-up years, the nights at the New Afrika Shrine, the Detty December headline sets that shut the island down. Burna's Lagos is the Lagos of the hustle — the city is in the lyrics, the slang, the posture. No audience on earth receives his music better than the one that gave him his vocabulary.

THE KEY TRACK

“Ye” (2018). Five minutes of slow-burn confidence over a stripped-back beat — the single that turned Nigeria's biggest secret into the continent's loudest export. If you only hear one Burna Boy song, hear the one where he tells you exactly how he's going to win, then spends the next six years doing it.

THE STADIUM YEARS

By 2023, the arc was complete: Burna Boy became the first African artist to headline a UK stadium at London Stadium, and Twice as Tall's Grammy had already made him the continent's most decorated export of his generation. I Told Them... (2023) and festival headline slots everywhere confirmed the new math — Afrobeats wasn't asking for a seat at the global table anymore. It was the table.

THE LINEAGE

The Fela connection isn't branding, it's blood — and Burna knows exactly what he's carrying. The political bite of African Giant, the pan-African sermons between songs, the Shrine nights: it's a deliberate inheritance. Where Fela fought the Nigerian state with a horn section, Burna fights the world's short memory with a catalog. Same war, new weapons.

START HERE

“Ye” first, then “Last Last” for the pop side, then African Giant front to back. That's the arc: street prophet to global star, no filler, no skips.

Lagos makes a new Afrobeats king every eighteen months. Burna Boy is the one who refused to hand the crown back.
