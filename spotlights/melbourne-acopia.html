ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MELBOURNE

ACOPIA

Naarm's dream-pop trio makes music for yearners — three albums in three years, Pitchfork's blessing, and a sold-out London show to prove it.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · SEPTEMBER 9, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Talk About It” — the official video.

Nobody in Melbourne is working faster or quieter than Acopia. The Naarm trio — Kate Durman, Lachlan McGeehan, and Morgan Wright — formed in 2018 with a minimalist palette: slow tempos, sparse arrangements, breathy vocals, and a sense of restraint so complete it felt like a philosophy. The FADER called them dream-pop for yearners, and the name itself refers to being ill-suited to everyday life. This is a band that turned not-belonging into an aesthetic and then made the whole indie world want in on it.

Three albums in three years: the somber DIY debut Chances (2022) on Naarm label Companion, the self-titled Acopia (2023) with its new polish, and Blush Response (September 2025), their first on Scenic Route — eight tracks of clipped guitars, snapping drums, and songs about emotional distance that Pitchfork, The Guardian, The FADER, and The Beat all praised. Pitchfork summed up their lane as "a nod to 1980s ennui and 2020s anxiety," which is about as accurate a description of being alive right now as anyone's managed.

The international footprint keeps growing: support slots for Bar Italia and Tirzah, festival appearances at Golden Plains and Rising, a sold-out 2024 headline show at London's Lexington, and UK producer Daniel Avery showing public support. For a band built on intimacy and distance in careful balance, Acopia has gotten remarkably good at closing the distance.

WHY MELBOURNE CLAIMS THEM

Because Acopia is the sound of Naarm's downtempo underground at its most refined — the city's dream-pop and trip-hop scene distilled into songs that feel like late-night tram rides through the inner north. They function like a traditional band (guitar, bass, vocals) in a scene built on dubwise effects and sampling, and that classicism is exactly what makes them stand out.

THE KEY TRACK

"Talk About It" (2025). The Blush Response opener — clipped guitar, drums that snap, and Durman's breathy delivery mapping the tension of words left unsaid. It's the trio at their most direct, and it still sounds like a secret.

THE SOUND

Downtempo dream-pop between trip-hop, shoegaze, and indie: sultry vocals, pensive synths, smouldering basslines, and synths plus drum machines doing the emotional heavy lifting. "This Conversation is Getting Boring" could've been a 90s chill-out room track; "Falter" is a song about moving on that sounds like floating.

START HERE

Blush Response front to back, then Chances for the DIY origin, then the Live at Phoenix film of "See You In Everyone" to hear how the bedroom intimacy survives a stage.

Acopia makes music for people who feel too much and say too little. Melbourne has a lot of those. So does everywhere else.
