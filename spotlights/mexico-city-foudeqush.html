ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MEXICO CITY

FOUDE QUSH

Mexico City's alt-soul voice went from the capital's scene to the Black Panther: Wakanda Forever soundtrack — and her own album ANGELA is where the story really starts.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · FEBRUARY 4, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

Listen → Listen on Spotify

Start with “Con La Brisa” — the Wakanda Forever standout — official lyric video.

Foudeqush is the Mexico City singer who quietly became one of the most interesting voices in Latin alt-soul without playing anyone's game. Her album ANGELA — and its deluxe edition — is the center of gravity: smoky, late-night songs that sit somewhere between R&B, soul, and the Spanish-language indie she grew up on in the capital.

Then came the left-field co-sign that changed her scale. "Con La Brisa," her collaboration with Ludwig Göransson, landed on the Black Panther: Wakanda Forever soundtrack in 2022 — and climbed to number four on the Shazam global chart with millions of shazams. That's the kind of look most indie artists never get. Foudeqush treated it like what it was: fuel.

WHY MEXICO CITY CLAIMS HER

CDMX's alt-R&B scene is a small, stubborn world of its own — artists who'd rather build a catalogue than chase a viral moment. Foudeqush is its most patient proof of concept: years of steady releases, a sound that gets richer with every record, and a live presence built on control rather than spectacle.

THE KEY TRACK

"Con La Brisa." Yes, it's the soundtrack one — but strip away the Marvel halo and it's simply a gorgeous song, her voice floating over Göransson's production like it was always supposed to be there. Then go find "FOLEN ENYELS" for the version of her that's purely hers.

THE LONGEVITY

Here's why Beatwick is pushing her: artists who survive a blockbuster look without becoming a feature-circuit act are rare. Foudeqush went back to her own records, kept writing, and put out a deluxe edition of ANGELA that stands taller than the soundtrack song. That's not luck. That's a career.

The sound itself is the real story. ANGELA lives in that smoky space where alt-soul meets Spanish-language intimacy — no chasing trends, no anglicized compromises. Foudeqush sings like someone who's studied the classics and decided to write her own chapter instead of covering theirs. In a CDMX scene crowded with acts trying to be everything at once, her restraint is the most radical thing on the table.

START HERE

"FOLEN ENYELS," then "Con La Brisa," then the full ANGELA album, deluxe version if you can find it. Best played after midnight.

Foudeqush didn't chase the blockbuster. She let it find her, then went home and got back to work.
