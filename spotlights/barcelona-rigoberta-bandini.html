ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · BARCELONA

Rigoberta Bandini

Barcelona's theater kid turned pop provocateur — the voice that made "Ay Mamá" a national anthem.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · OCTOBER 6, 2026 · 2 MIN READ

Paula Ribó González was born in Barcelona in 1990 and spent her childhood in dubbing booths — the Spanish voice of Caillou, among others — before studying theater at the city's Institut del Teatre and forming the musical-theater trio Mamzelles. In 2019, at 29, she invented Rigoberta Bandini: a pop project with a borrowed name, a theater kid's sense of drama, and absolutely no interest in playing it safe.

The singles landed like small explosions. "In Spain We Call It Soledad" (2020), "Perra" (2020) — a feminist anthem that became a rallying cry — and then "Ay Mamá" (2021), an ode to motherhood and female power that finished second at Benidorm Fest 2022 and should have won. The song became a genuine national phenomenon: chanted at demonstrations, sung at weddings, impossible to escape. La Emperatriz (2022), her debut album, gathered the singles into one of the decade's great Spanish pop records.

Barcelona is the whole context. The theater training, the dubbing studios, the Catalan cultural scene that treats pop as art — Bandini is what happens when that world decides to make bangers. She followed the debut with the ambitious double album Jesucrista Superstar (2025), doubling down on the theatricality. Her live shows — Palau Sant Jordi, Razzmatazz, festival headline slots — are productions in the fullest sense: this is a performer who learned staging before she learned pop.

What she represents is the Barcelona alternative to the Madrid hit machine: literate, feminist, theatrical, and completely uninterested in permission. Spanish pop's most interesting voice isn't playing the industry game. She's directing it.

The key track

"Ay Mamá" (2021). The Benidorm Fest entry that became an anthem — a celebration of mothers, breasts, and female power that the whole country adopted. Pop as collective catharsis, delivered with a wink and a war cry.

Start here

La Emperatriz (2022) — the debut, the singles, the statement. Then Jesucrista Superstar (2025) for the maximalist double-album sprawl, and "Perra" when you need the earlier, rawer version.

The take: Rigoberta Bandini turned a theater education into Spain's most vital pop project. Barcelona's dubbing booths and drama schools produced a star the hit factories couldn't have invented. "Ay Mamá" indeed.
