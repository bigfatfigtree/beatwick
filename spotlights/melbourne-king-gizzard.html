ARTIST SPOTLIGHT · MELBOURNE

King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard

Melbourne's most prolific band turned weirdness into a work ethic — and the work ethic into a cult.

BY THE BEATWICK DESK · MAY 13, 2026 · 3 MIN READ

King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard formed in Melbourne in 2010, and frontman Stu Mackenzie immediately set about making the rest of the music industry look lazy. Five studio albums in 2017 alone. Microtonal guitars. A thrash-metal record followed by a jazzy boogie record followed by three-hour marathon shows. The band treats genre the way Melbourne treats weather: as something that changes every twenty minutes, and you'd better keep up.

The joke was that they couldn't possibly sustain it. The punchline is that they did — building one of the most devoted fanbases in rock, a bootlegger program that lets fans release their own live recordings, and the Gizzfest festival. Prolific stopped being a criticism and became the whole point.

Their bootlegger program — fans can press and sell their own live recordings — is the most Melbourne business model imaginable: give away the product, sell the community, and let the weirdos run the merch table.

Melbourne roots

Gizzard is the ultimate Melbourne band-room success story. They came up through the same sticky-carpet circuit as everyone else — the Tote, the Corner, the regional RSLs — but their real home became the marathon show: three hours, no setlist repeats across a residency, the crowd treated as collaborators. Melbourne's live scene rewards stamina and strangeness in equal measure, and Gizzard maxed out both stats.

The city's record stores still do a brisk trade in their endless vinyl variants, and their microtonal experiments — custom-fretted guitars playing in-between notes — gave Melbourne's psych scene a genuinely new musical language. Nobody else was going to invent it. Trust Melbourne's weirdest band to do it by accident, on purpose.

One key track

"Rattlesnake" (2017). Eleven minutes of microtonal motorik that starts as a groove and ends as a trance state. It's the best single-song summary of the band: repetitive, hypnotic, and completely unwilling to stop.

Most bands have a sound. Gizzard has a release schedule — and somehow that's more distinctive.

Start here

Nonagon Infinity (2016) — nine songs designed to loop forever, each flowing into the next. Play it on repeat and you'll understand the cult. Resistance is part of the ritual.
