On the air — Los Angeles

LOS ANGELES

Los Angeles is the music industry's company town, and it sounds like it: Dr. Dre's G-funk and the N.W.A lineage out of Compton rewrote rap, the pop songwriting camps on the Westside mint hits for the whole planet, and the indie pipeline runs from Laurel Canyon's ghosts to Silver Lake's current crop. The Latin crossover wave — reggaetón sessions, música mexicana's stadium era — increasingly routes through here too. The smog has a kick drum under it.

Good questions

FAQ

What is Los Angeles known for musically?

It's the industry's company town: Dr. Dre's G-funk and the N.W.A lineage out of Compton rewrote rap, Westside pop songwriting camps mint hits for the whole planet, and the indie pipeline runs from Laurel Canyon's ghosts to Silver Lake's current crop.

Who are the best new artists in Los Angeles?

Start underground: Pink Siifu, the most prolific shape-shifter in rap, folding jazz, punk and Southern rap into one pot, and Navy Blue, the LA-born skater whose drumless loops and warm jazz samples carry the city's quietest fire.

Where's the live music in Los Angeles?

The Hollywood Bowl for the under-the-stars coronation, the Troubadour in West Hollywood — the 500-cap room that launched Elton John in America — and Whisky a Go Go on the Sunset Strip, where The Doors were the house band.

What's the Latin wave in LA?

Reggaetón sessions and música mexicana's stadium era increasingly route through LA. The city has always been a border sound; now the industry's infrastructure is catching up to the street.
