MUMBAI

Mumbai is Bollywood's capital, which means it's the capital of film music in a country where film music is the music industry. But the city's second act is louder: gully rap from the streets Divine put on the map with Gully Boy, a ferocious metal scene led by Bloodywood, and an indie wave fronted by Prateek Kuhad. Nineteen million people, zero chill.

GOOD QUESTIONS

FAQ

What defines Mumbai's music scene right now?

Bollywood is still the industry — film music is the music industry here. The second act is louder: gully rap from the streets DIVINE put on the map, Bloodywood's ferocious metal, and Prateek Kuhad's indie wave.

What is gully rap?

Hindi hip-hop from Mumbai's working-class neighborhoods — “gully” means lane. DIVINE's rise, fictionalized in Gully Boy, took it from street cyphers to stadiums.

Which Mumbai venues should I know?

NCPA at Nariman Point for the prestige bills, antiSOCIAL's basement in Lower Parel — arguably the city's best sound system — and Dublin Square at Phoenix MarketCity, where half the touring acts end up anyway.

Who are the new names on Mumbai's dial?

Kaam Bhaari, who went from Kandivali's streets to his own label Art Nagar, and The Yellow Diary, five musicians who built an indie empire out of one song.
