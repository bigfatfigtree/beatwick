ON THE AIR — LISBON

LISBON

Kuduro came to Lisbon from Angola and never left — it mutated in the city's clubs into something faster, harder, and entirely its own, with Buraka Som Sistema carrying it to festival mainstages worldwide. Around it, an Afro-Portuguese rap scene has made Lisbon one of Europe's most interesting hip-hop cities, moving between Portuguese, Creole, and English without breaking stride.

PHOTO: Alvesgaspar · CC BY-SA 3.0 VIA WIKIMEDIA COMMONS

Lisbon's sound is kuduro, fado and Portuguese hip-hop braided together — Buraka Som Sistema took the city's Angolan roots global, and a new generation of MCs sells out the Coliseu. This is Beatwick's guide to what's moving in 2026 — 30 essential tracks, the rooms that matter (Coliseu, Campo Pequeno, Musicbox), and the new artists to watch.

Lisbon FAQ

What is the Lisbon music scene known for?

Kuduro (Buraka Som Sistema took the city's Angolan roots global), fado (Amália Rodrigues, Mariza, Ana Moura), and Portuguese hip-hop's new wave — Papillon sold out the Coliseu, and Nídia Minaj carries the Príncipe Discos torch.

Where do locals see live music in Lisbon?

The Coliseu dos Recreios for the sold-out homecoming, Campo Pequeno for the big arena night, and Musicbox in Cais do Sodré for the late club set.

Who are the new Lisbon artists to watch?

Our New to the Dial section tracks them — Papillon and Nídia Minaj — alongside spotlights on Buraka Som Sistema, Mariza and Salvador Sobral.
