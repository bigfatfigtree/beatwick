TOKYO

City pop died in 1989 and was resurrected by an algorithm — Tatsuro Yamashita and Mariya Takeuchi's glossy '80s funk became the internet's favorite time machine. Now Tokyo runs two sounds at once: the nostalgia economy and the feral present, where Creepy Nuts, King Gnu, and Vaundy turn J-rap and art-pop into chart monsters. Nobody remixes the past quite like this city.

GOOD QUESTIONS

FAQ

What defines Tokyo's music scene right now?

Two sounds at once: the city-pop revival — Tatsuro Yamashita and Mariya Takeuchi's '80s funk, resurrected by streaming algorithms — and the feral present, where Creepy Nuts, King Gnu, and Vaundy turn J-rap and art-pop into chart monsters.

What is city pop?

'80s Japanese pop-funk built for Tokyo's boom years — glossy, coastal, expensive-sounding. It died commercially around 1989 and came back through YouTube recommendations and vinyl reissues. Mariya Takeuchi's “Plastic Love” is the genre's unofficial anthem.

Which Tokyo venues should I know?

Blue Note Tokyo in Aoyama for jazz, Liquidroom in Ebisu for electronic and rock, and Zepp Haneda for J-pop's biggest touring productions.

Who are the new names on Tokyo's dial?

Rapper Tohji — the Mall Boyz MC turning mall culture and trance into underground anthems — and Luby Sparks, five shoegaze nerds singing in English. Both are in our New to the Dial spotlights above.
