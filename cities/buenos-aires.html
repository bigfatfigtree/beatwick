On the air — Buenos Aires

BUENOS AIRES

Trap argentino turned Buenos Aires' barrios into the Spanish-speaking world's rap capital. Duki, Cazzu, and Khea built the scene from freestyle battles; Bizarrap's BZRP sessions rewired the global Latin charts from a home studio. Underneath it all: rock nacional's permanent claim on the city's soul — Soda Stereo and Charly García still fill stadiums from beyond. Nobody freestyles hungrier than a porteño with something to prove.

The short answer: Buenos Aires freestyles hungrier than anywhere else. Trap argentino was built in Parque Rivadavia's rap battles and Bizarrap's home studio, then rewired the Spanish-speaking charts; underneath, rock nacional — Soda Stereo, Charly García — still owns the city's soul. The Monumental in Núñez takes the stadium nights; Corrientes takes everything else.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What is trap argentino?

Argentina's homegrown rap wave, built in the late 2010s from freestyle-battle culture — the legendary El Quinto Escalón competition and the Parque Rivadavia battles — by Duki, Cazzu, Khea, and Ysy A, then exported worldwide by Bizarrap's sessions.

What are the BZRP Music Sessions?

Producer Bizarrap's YouTube session series, recorded in his home studio — each one a single-take collaboration. Vol. 53 with Shakira, released in January 2023, became one of the biggest Latin records of the decade.

Where do the biggest shows happen in Buenos Aires?

Estadio Monumental — River Plate's stadium in Núñez — is the biggest stage in Argentine music; Luna Park in Puerto Madero is the historic indoor temple; and the Teatro Gran Rex on Corrientes is where every Argentine act earns its stripes.

What is rock nacional?

Argentina's rock movement, born in the late 1960s and still the city's soul music: Soda Stereo (formed 1982), Charly García, Fito Páez, Los Fabulosos Cadillacs. The trap generation grew up on it.
