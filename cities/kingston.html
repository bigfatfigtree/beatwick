On the air — Kingston

KINGSTON

Dancehall's living root. Reggae was born in Trench Town's yards, sound-system culture invented the remix and the DJ right here, and Kingston's studios — Studio One, Tuff Gong — wrote the blueprint every bass-heavy genre still follows. Bob Marley is the patron saint, but the modern universe runs through Vybz Kartel, Spice, and Sean Paul. The whole world dances to basslines this city invented; Kingston barely noticed.

The short answer: Kingston invented the remix, the DJ, and the bassline the whole world dances to — sound system culture out of 1950s yards, Studio One's blueprint, Trench Town's gospel. Bob Marley's house at 56 Hope Road is a museum now; Dub Club's Sunday sessions on Skyline Drive are still the real church.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What is sound system culture?

Born in 1950s Kingston: mobile DJ rigs — towering speaker stacks on trucks — battling for the crowd's love at street dances. The sound system invented the remix (dub), the DJ as performer, and the bassweight the whole world now dances to.

Where is reggae's history in Kingston?

Trench Town, where Bob Marley grew up; 56 Hope Road, his home and now the Bob Marley Museum; and the studios that wrote the blueprint — Studio One, founded by Coxsone Dodd in 1963, and Tuff Gong.

What is Reggae Month?

Every February, Jamaica celebrates Reggae Month with concerts, lectures, and events across Kingston — the city's official salute to the music it gave the world.

Who runs dancehall right now?

The Worl' Boss Vybz Kartel, Spice, Popcaan, and Sean Paul still set the terms — with the new roots wave (Chronixx, Protoje, Koffee, Lila Iké) and producers-turned-artists like Iotosh pushing it forward.
