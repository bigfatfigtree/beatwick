On the air — Bogotá

BOGOTÁ

Latin alternative's laboratory sits at 2,600 meters, and the altitude does something to the music. Bomba Estéreo took cumbia into the global festival circuit from a Bogotá base, Aterciopelados and Monsieur Periné built the city's alt-pop identity, and Rock al Parque remains the biggest free rock festival in Latin America. Medellín's reggaetón gets the headlines; Bogotá does the experiments — tropical futurism with a mountain chill.

The short answer: At 2,600 meters, Bogotá turned thin air into a sound: Bomba Estéreo's tropical futurism, Aterciopelados' alt-rock, Monsieur Periné's swing-era cumbia — while Rock al Parque holds the biggest free rock festival in Latin America at Parque Simón Bolívar. Medellín gets the reggaetón headlines; Bogotá runs the experiments.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What is Rock al Parque?

Bogotá's free rock festival, first held in 1995 at Parque Simón Bolívar — one of the biggest free rock festivals in Latin America, and the city's loudest annual ritual.

Why call Bogotá Latin alternative's laboratory?

At around 2,600 meters, the capital became the base for the continent's experiments: Bomba Estéreo's electro-cumbia, Aterciopelados' alt-rock, Monsieur Periné's jazz-cumbia, and a whole underground of tropical bass on labels like ZZK.

What venues anchor Bogotá's scene?

The Movistar Arena for arena ambitions, Estadio El Campín for the biggest outdoor nights, and the Teatro Colón — the 19th-century national theatre, opened in 1892 — for the grandest stage in the city.

What is tropical futurism?

Shorthand for the Bogotá-bred fusion of cumbia, champeta, and Afro-Caribbean rhythms with electronic production — Bomba Estéreo took it to the global festival circuit; Ghetto Kumbé drags it through house and hip-hop.
