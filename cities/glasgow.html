ON THE AIR — GLASGOW

GLASGOW

London gets the music press; Glasgow gets the bands. The Jesus and Mary Chain, Primal Scream, Teenage Fanclub, Mogwai, Belle and Sebastian, Franz Ferdinand — decade after decade, Scotland's biggest city produces guitar music the rest of the country wishes it made, usually while being completely ignored by the industry down south.

PHOTO: M J Richardson · CC BY-SA 2.0 VIA WIKIMEDIA COMMONS

Glasgow is the best music city in Britain and it isn't close — Franz Ferdinand, Primal Scream, Belle and Sebastian, Chvrches and SOPHIE all started here, and the Barras remains the loudest room in Scotland. This is Beatwick's guide to what's moving in 2026 — 30 essential tracks, the legendary venues (Barrowland, King Tut's, O2 Academy), and the new bands coming up.

The legends

DEFINED THIS CITY

  • Belle and Sebastian — The shyest band in Glasgow became its most beloved — twee as a weapon, melodies as armor.
  • Franz Ferdinand — Four art-school misfits who turned Glasgow's post-punk attic into the biggest guitar band of the 2000s.
  • Mogwai — Glasgow's loudest quiet band — five post-rock architects who built their own label and never looked back.

Glasgow FAQ

What is the Glasgow music scene known for?

Indie rock and post-punk with an absurd hit rate: Franz Ferdinand, The Jesus and Mary Chain, Primal Scream, Belle and Sebastian, Mogwai, Travis, Chvrches, Texas, Simple Minds — and electronic outliers like SOPHIE, Hudson Mohawke and Rustie.

Where do locals see live music in Glasgow?

The Barrowland Ballroom in the Gallowgate — the loudest, sweatiest, most beloved room in Scottish music — King Tut's Wah Wah Hut, where Oasis got signed and every Glasgow band starts, and the O2 Academy on the Southside for the big-room graduation.

Who are the new Glasgow bands to watch?

Our New to the Dial section tracks them — trance-punk five-piece VLURE and post-punk quintet Humour. The names that defined the city (Franz Ferdinand, Mogwai and Belle and Sebastian) live in the Legends list below.
