JOHANNESBURG

Joburg invented a genre from a bassline that sounds like a log drum being dropped down stairs, then watched the whole planet dance to it. Amapiano grew out of township house parties into the most exported South African sound since kwaito — Kabza De Small and DJ Maphorisa turned bedroom production into a global industry. Nobody does a groove like this city; everyone else is just remixing.

Johannesburg is the home of amapiano — the log-drum bassline that grew out of township house parties into the most exported South African sound since kwaito. Kabza De Small and DJ Maphorisa turned bedroom production into a global industry, and open-air grounds like Constitution Hill carry the groove to thousands at a time.

Good to know

FAQ

What is amapiano?

South Africa's dominant house-music export — deep, rolling grooves built around the log drum bassline, born in township parties and now filling clubs on every continent.

Who is the King of Amapiano?

Kabza De Small — the producer whose township bedroom setup became the most imitated sound on the planet.

What is the log drum?

Amapiano's signature: a deep, bouncy bassline that sounds like its name — the low-end heartbeat under nearly every yano anthem.

Where did amapiano start?

In township house parties around Johannesburg and Pretoria, where DJs and bedroom producers built a new groove out of deep house, jazz and kwaito — then took it global.
