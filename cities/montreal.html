On the air — Montréal

MONTRÉAL

A French-speaking island that threw the world's biggest jazz festival and accidentally became indie rock's northern capital. Arcade Fire's funeral march started in Mile End lofts, Godspeed You! Black Emperor builds post-rock cathedrals, and KAYTRANADA rewired dance music from a Montreal bedroom. The rest of North America pretends the French-language pop scene doesn't exist; the rest of North America is wrong. The cold makes everything louder.

Good questions

FAQ

What is Montreal known for musically?

Indie rock's northern capital — Arcade Fire's funeral march started in Mile End lofts, Godspeed You! Black Emperor builds post-rock cathedrals — plus KAYTRANADA rewiring dance music from a Montreal bedroom, and the world's biggest jazz festival every summer.

Who are the best new artists in Montreal?

Lydia Képinski, the francophone chaos-pop queen with two Polaris-longlisted albums, and Ouri, a classically trained cellist fusing orchestral composition with the rave scene's electronic grit.

Where's the live music in Montreal?

The Bell Centre downtown — the biggest room in French Canada — MTELUS, the old Metropolis theatre where bilingual chaos fits one stage, and Place des Arts at the heart of the jazz festival's summer takeover.

Does the French-language pop scene matter?

Yes — and the rest of North America pretending it doesn't exist is exactly the problem. From Beau Dommage to Cœur de pirate to Lydia Képinski, the francophone scene is one of the city's load-bearing walls.
