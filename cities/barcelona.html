ON THE AIR — BARCELONA

BARCELONA

Barcelona runs on a different clock: Sónar turned it into electronic music's annual summit, and a generation of Catalan artists wired Latin rhythms straight into the club. Rosalía took flamenco to the global pop charts without asking permission, and Bad Gyal made the city's dembow-inflected underground impossible to ignore.

PHOTO: Alvesgaspar · CC BY-SA 4.0 VIA WIKIMEDIA COMMONS

Barcelona is where flamenco-pop conquered the planet and urbano learned Catalan — Rosalía rewrote the global playbook from here, and a Catalan-language rap scene runs parallel to the stadiums. This is Beatwick's guide to what's moving in 2026 — 30 essential tracks, the rooms that matter (Razzmatazz, Sala Apolo, Palau Sant Jordi), and the new artists breaking out.

The legends

DEFINED THIS CITY

  • Bad Gyal — The Vilassar de Mar singer who took dancehall to Catalonia — and made Barcelona the capital of Spanish perreo.
  • Rigoberta Bandini — Barcelona's theater kid turned pop provocateur — the voice that made 'Ay Mamá' a national anthem.
  • Rosalía — A Catalan flamenco student rewired pop in three languages — and made Barcelona the center of the Latin avant-garde.

Barcelona FAQ

What is the Barcelona music scene known for?

Flamenco-pop's global conquest (Rosalía rewrote the playbook from here), Catalan urbano (Mushkaa, Julieta), Barcelona rap (Morad, Yung Beef), and a deep indie lineage — Love of Lesbian, Dorian, Estopa — plus 'Barcelona' by Freddie Mercury and Montserrat Caballé, the 1992 Olympics anthem.

Where do locals see live music in Barcelona?

Razzmatazz in Poblenou for the multi-room club night, Sala Apolo on Paral·lel for the mid-size rite of passage, and Palau Sant Jordi on Montjuïc for the big arena show. Primavera Sound's home turf.

Who are the new Barcelona artists to watch?

Our New to the Dial section tracks them — Mushkaa and Julieta lead Catalan urbano's new wave — plus spotlights on Rosalía, Bad Gyal and Rigoberta Bandini.
