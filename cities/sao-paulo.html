On the air — São Paulo

SÃO PAULO

South America's biggest city makes the continent's loudest party music. Baile funk's bruxaria — raw, favela-born, 150 BPM and climbing — went from Rio's bailes to São Paulo's pressure cooker and then global, with Anitta and MC Fioti carrying it past every border. Underneath the funk: the biggest electronic festival circuit in Latin America and a rock underground that never died. Paulistanos don't do quiet.

The short answer: São Paulo runs on baile funk at 150 BPM — the bruxaria wave that turned bailes in Paraisópolis and Heliópolis into the country's main stage — plus the biggest festival circuit in Latin America (Lollapalooza Brasil and The Town at Interlagos) and a rock underground that still packs Rua Augusta's bars. Allianz Parque in Barra Funda takes the stadium tours; Villa Country takes the sertanejo faithful.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What is baile funk?

Baile funk — funk carioca — grew out of Rio de Janeiro's dance parties in the 1980s and '90s, built on Miami bass and freestyle. The current bruxaria wave pushes past 150 BPM, and São Paulo is now its loudest stronghold, with the city's bailes functioning as the genre's main stage.

Where do the biggest concerts happen in São Paulo?

Allianz Parque in Barra Funda takes the global stadium tours, Espaço Unimed handles the big-room circuit, and the festival calendar runs through Interlagos — home to both Lollapalooza Brasil and The Town, Rock in Rio's São Paulo sibling, which debuted in 2023.

What is Virada Cultural?

São Paulo's 24-hour free culture festival, running since 2005 — usually in May, with stages and shows spread across the city center, all night long.

Who are São Paulo's defining rap voices?

Racionais MC's, formed in the late 1980s, broke nationally with Sobrevivendo no Inferno (1997) and never left; Emicida came up through the Santa Cruz freestyle battles; Criolo and the late Sabotage filled out a lineage the whole country rhymes over.
